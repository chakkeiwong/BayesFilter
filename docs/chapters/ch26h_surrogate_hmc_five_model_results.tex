\chapter{The Completed Surrogate-HMC Case Study}
\label{ch:bf-surrogate-hmc-five-models}

This chapter records the completed end-to-end test of the surrogate Hamiltonian
Monte Carlo construction.  The question is deliberately specific: can a fixed
LEDH particle-filter computation supply a useful proposal force while a separate
Metropolis correction samples the declared finite posterior target?  The test
is therefore about the HMC construction, including its endpoint and its
sampling diagnostics.  A finite-filter agreement check is evidence about the
filter; it is not a substitute for the HMC run.  The numerical results are the
runs archived on 23 October 2026.  Their frozen configurations and recorded
source revisions define the tested method; these chains do not evaluate later
optional guidance or proposal variants.

A separate structural-transition extension completed on 24 October 2026 is
documented in Section~\ref{sec:bf-structural-ledh-ab-campaign}.  It applies the
same endpoint--force distinction to a rank-one AR(2) transition and a nonlinear
deterministic completion, including the innovation-space proposal, localized
reset precision repair, Model B NeuTra training, and final posterior results.
Those results supplement, rather than replace, the five runs reported here.

\section{What ``surrogate'' means here}
\label{sec:bf-surrogate-meaning}

For an unconstrained parameter $q$, let $\theta=C(q)$ be the physical model
chart and let $\widehat\ell_F(q)$ be the log likelihood returned by the fixed
LEDH covariance-guided particle filter.  The filter carries an analytical
sensitivity for every parameter through one time recursion, so it returns
$\nabla_q\widehat\ell_F$ for the same finite program that returned
$\widehat\ell_F$ on valid regions, with the branch labels and numerical controls held fixed.  The proposal routine uses the gradient of the finite negative log posterior,
\begin{equation}
 U_F(q)=-\log p_q(q)-\widehat\ell_F(q),\qquad F_F(q)=\nabla_q U_F(q).
 \label{eq:bf-surrogate-filter-force}
\end{equation}
The word surrogate refers to this proposal force: it is a computational guide
and need not be the derivative of the endpoint potential used for acceptance.
It does not mean that the Metropolis step accepts against an unspecified or
silently replaced target.

The endpoint is written $U_E(q)=-\log\widetilde\pi_E(q)$ and is evaluated
separately.  For LGSSM, $\widetilde\pi_E$ uses the exact Kalman likelihood.
For KSC stochastic volatility it uses the fixed 1,201-node grid refinement.
For predator--prey and SIR it is the average of four fixed bootstrap endpoint
likelihood estimates, and for range--bearing it is the average of four fixed,
full-support trajectory-importance estimates.  The latter four endpoints are
explicit approximate numerical targets; they are not exact model oracles.
Only the LGSSM endpoint is exact; this does not imply that its finite LEDH score
equals the Kalman score.  The correction below targets the stated endpoint
under the proposal conditions proved in Section~\ref{sec:bf-surrogate-mh-proof}.

The SIR NeuTra run adds a frozen change of variables $q=T(z)$.  Its endpoint and
force are
\begin{align}
 U_Z(z) &= U_E(T(z))-\log|\det DT(z)|,\\
 F_Z(z) &= DT(z)^T F_F(T(z))
          -\nabla_z\log|\det DT(z)|.
 \label{eq:bf-surrogate-neutra-composition}
\end{align}
The map is frozen before HMC.  No parameter-dependent retraining or retuning
occurs during the chain.

\section{One shared finite-filter sweep}
\label{sec:bf-surrogate-shared-sweep}

All five model adapters enter the same consumer call chain:
\begin{center}
\small
\path{surrogate_hmc_five_model.py}\ $\rightarrow$
\path{make_batched_score_filter}\ $\rightarrow$
\path{NativeModels}\ $\rightarrow$
\path{build_maps/draw_from_maps}\ $\rightarrow$
\path{reset_cloud}\ $\rightarrow$
\path{analytical score}.
\end{center}
The adapter supplies transition, observation and parameter-partial callbacks; it
does not contain a second model-specific filtering recurrence.  The repeated
numerical kernels are TensorFlow functions with stable signatures and XLA
enabled.  Time, transport-iteration and native-shard loops are TensorFlow
loops.  Python orchestrates phases and writes provenance outside the numerical
kernel.

After the preceding reset, the $N$ ancestors have equal weights.  Write
$m_{t,j}=f_\theta(x_{t-1,j})$ and $Q_\theta$ for the transition mean and
additive process covariance.  The shared recurrence forms
\begin{equation}
 \bar m_t=\frac1N\sum_jm_{t,j},\qquad
 P_t=Q_\theta+\frac1N\sum_j(m_{t,j}-\bar m_t)(m_{t,j}-\bar m_t)^T.
 \label{eq:bf-surrogate-predictive-mixture}
\end{equation}
It constructs one UKF observation guide and three proposal components: the
prior transition, the UKF-guided component, and a local affine LEDH flow.
Let $A_{t,cj}$ and $b_{t,cj}$ be the affine map for component $c$ and ancestor
$j$.  Component zero has $A_{t,0j}=I$ and $b_{t,0j}=0$.  The component densities
and incremental likelihood are
\begin{align}
 q_{t,c}(x)&=\frac1N\sum_j\mathcal N\!\left(x;A_{t,cj}m_{t,j}+b_{t,cj},
                         A_{t,cj}Q_\theta A_{t,cj}^T\right),\\
 q_t(x)&=\sum_{c=0}^2\beta_c q_{t,c}(x),\qquad
 r_{t,i}=\frac{g_\theta(y_t\mid x_{t,i})q_{t,0}(x_{t,i})}{q_t(x_{t,i})},\\
 \widehat\ell_t&=\log\left(\frac1N\sum_i r_{t,i}\right),\qquad
 \widehat\ell_F=\sum_{t=1}^T\widehat\ell_t.
 \label{eq:bf-surrogate-mixture-correction}
\end{align}
The numerator uses the whole predictive mixture, not the transition density
conditional on a single sampled ancestor.  Normalize the ratios to weights
$w_i$ and compute the weighted posterior moments $(\mu,P_w)$.  Finite Sinkhorn
transport produces a cloud $y_i$ with unweighted moments $(\bar y,S_y)$.
The reset is then
\begin{equation}
 z_i=\mu+L_{P_w}L_{S_y}^{-1}(y_i-\bar y).
 \label{eq:bf-surrogate-reset}
\end{equation}
On valid positive-definite inputs this restores the weighted posterior moments
in exact arithmetic; the code checks finite-precision residuals and removes the
residual mean of the centered cloud.  The same operations, including mixture
responsibilities, Cholesky tangents, transport contractions and reset weights,
are differentiated analytically.
Thus the reported score is a total derivative of the implemented finite value
program, not a partial derivative of only the transported cloud.  Invalid
rows are marked and carried as telemetry; a finite large energy error is not a
sampler stopping rule.

\section{Why the endpoint correction is legitimate}
\label{sec:bf-surrogate-mh-proof}

Write $M$ for the fixed diagonal mass matrix and $F=\nabla U_F$ for the frozen
proposal force.  One symmetric numerical step in the implementation is
\begin{align}
 p_{1/2}&=p_0-\tfrac{\epsilon}{2}F(q_0),\\
 q_1&=q_0+\epsilon M^{-1}p_{1/2},\\
 p_1&=p_{1/2}-\tfrac{\epsilon}{2}F(q_1),
 \end{align}
repeated for a fixed number of steps and followed by one terminal momentum
flip.  Each kick and drift is a triangular shear with
unit determinant, so their composition has unit volume determinant.  Reversing
the momentum and applying the same shears in reverse order gives the original
state, provided the frozen force is a deterministic function of position.
The Metropolis probability therefore uses the endpoint Hamiltonian,
\begin{equation}
 a((q,p),(q',p'))=\min\left\{1,
 \exp[-H_E(q',p')+H_E(q,p)]\right\},\qquad
 H_E=U_E+\tfrac12p^TM^{-1}p.
 \label{eq:bf-surrogate-mh-correction}
\end{equation}
The proposal force can be inexact or based on a different finite likelihood;
that changes efficiency and energy error, while the acceptance correction
addresses the declared endpoint.  The statement requires a fixed, measurable,
finite proposal field and a correctly evaluated endpoint.  It does not prove
that an approximate endpoint equals the scientific model posterior.

\section{Scope, priors, and numerical settings}
\label{sec:bf-surrogate-scope}

The five cases use the scientific priors recorded in the local paper ledger
\path{docs/plans/surrogate-hmc-literature-priors-2026-10-15.md}.  The
predator--prey box is the unchanged Zhao--Cui setting
\citep{zhao2024ttsequential}; the other choices are documented adaptations of
published state-space and disease-transmission settings
\citep{dahlin2019particle,sarkka2009recursive,grinsztajn2021bayes,sarkka2013variational}.
The LGSSM prior is $\phi\sim\mathcal N(0,0.5)$ conditioned on
$|\phi|<0.55$, independent process and observation variance multipliers
$v_Q,v_R\sim\operatorname{IG}(1,1)$, and an initial-mean coefficient
$m\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$.  Here the Normal second argument is a variance and
$\operatorname{IG}(a,b)$ has density proportional to
$v^{-a-1}\exp(-b/v)$.  Restricting the AR prior and applying the inverse-Gamma
family to a process multiplier are declared modeling adaptations.

KSC uses $(\gamma+1)/2\sim\operatorname{Beta}(20,1.5)$ conditioned on
$0<\gamma<1$ and $\log\beta\sim\mathcal N(0,1.25)$, with state innovation
variance fixed at one.  Its benchmark likelihood and initial law are preserved;
this is not a full reproduction of the source model.  Predator--prey uses
independent uniform priors with lower bounds $(0.1,110,20,0.1,0,0)$ and upper
bounds $(1.1,130,30,1.1,1,1)$ for $(r,K,a,s,u,v)$.  The logistic sampling chart
preserves this physical box.

For SIR, the daily infection and recovery rates have positive-truncated Normal
priors $b\sim\mathcal N_+(2,1^2)$ and
$g\sim\mathcal N_+(0.4,0.5^2)$.  The synthetic calibration is 500 people per
region and 45 days per benchmark time unit, so the code uses
$b=500(0.1)e^{\theta_1}/45$ and $g=18e^{\theta_2}/45$.
This unit calibration is a local assumption.  The Gaussian observation
variance multiplier has $\operatorname{IG}(1,1)$ prior; the source paper's
negative-binomial dispersion prior does not apply to this observation model.
Range--bearing uses independent $\operatorname{IG}(1,1)$ priors on the common
process and observation variance multipliers, preserving their covariance
shapes.  These are proper priors, although $\operatorname{IG}(1,1)$ has infinite
mean.  They are recorded choices, not uniquely determined scientific priors.

Every density is evaluated in the sampled coordinates, including its chart
Jacobian.  The benchmark data are one synthetic data set per model, so truth
containment below is a diagnostic of those fixed targets, not repeated-data
coverage.

\section{Adaptive sampling and diagnostics}
\label{sec:bf-surrogate-sequential-sampling}

The shared sequential controller archives every warm-up chunk and excludes it
from posterior summaries.  It checks the maximum of rank-normalized split and
folded R-hat on the latest 1,000 transitions after at least 2,000 warm-up
transitions.  Readiness requires $\widehat R\leq1.05$; warm-up can extend to
10,000 per chain.  Retained draws grow
cumulatively; the controller checks all retained draws at each scheduled look,
accepts $\widehat R\leq1.01$, and can extend to 20,000 per chain.  This is why
retained counts differ across models below.  There is no fixed ``2,000 samples
and stop'' rule and no extra validation-sample phase.

Acceptance rate, effective sample size, MCSE and finite energy error are
reported diagnostics.  They explain sampler behavior and estimator precision;
acceptance is not convergence evidence, ESS is not a convergence criterion, and
a finite energy error does not veto a transition.  A non-finite proposal endpoint or Hamiltonian difference forces rejection.
Retained chunks must still satisfy their finite-state, endpoint, log-acceptance
and declared health checks.  A failed filter force is replaced by the fixed
position-only zero extension and recorded; it does not replace the endpoint.  The current
runs recorded zero divergences; range--bearing recorded one force-fallback
telemetry event, which was finite and retained under the declared fallback
policy.

\section{SIR transport training and computation}
\label{sec:bf-surrogate-sir-neutra}

The SIR map is the canonical repaired author-style IAF: three stages, two
width-six ELU conditioner layers per stage, author block masks, full coordinate
reversal, variance-scaled initialization, and the free bias outside the
conditional scale cap $c=2$.  The selected target-specific training arm used
512 accepted updates at learning rate $0.01$, scale $0.35$ and seed 201.  The
optimizer consumed batches of eight, evaluated as two native four-row target
shards, and applied one checked Adam update after the shard reductions.  The
map's inverse reconstruction error was below $10^{-15}$ in the saved artifact,
and its log-determinant round trip was zero to the recorded precision.

A forward/reverse NAF study exists as a separately labeled alternative
architecture experiment.  It is not the map used for this SIR result and does
not change the canonical IAF claim.  The SIR training target and HMC proposal force use the analytical LEDH
value/score adapter.  HMC acceptance uses the separate bootstrap endpoint;
both force and endpoint are composed with the same frozen map.

A bounded optimization study measured the native implementation on paired
$N=4032$, $K=2016$, FP32, TF32-off, XLA evaluations.  The candidate and
protected baseline were bitwise equal in value and score for LGSSM, KSC,
predator--prey and SIR; range--bearing differed only at the recorded floating
roundoff level (maximum absolute value difference $8.8\times10^{-5}$ and score
component difference $8.6\times10^{-4}$ in that diagnostic).  Warmed medians
were 3.95, 1.79, 2.83, 8.17 and 1.58 seconds for LGSSM, KSC, predator--prey,
SIR and range--bearing in the candidate arm.  These are single-machine
engineering measurements, not a statistical speed ranking or a claim that the
filter is exact.

\section{What the completed tests establish}
\label{sec:bf-surrogate-boundaries}

The five completed runs passed the retained-sample R-hat and health checks
for their fixed numerical targets.  The proposal construction is supported by
the reversible, volume-preserving argument above and by the focused tests of
the implemented force, endpoint and map composition.  All 17 generating
values lie within their respective marginal intervals.  This is a useful
one-dataset check for each model, although it does not measure repeated-data
coverage.

The exact Kalman endpoint makes the LGSSM posterior target exact under its
stated model and prior, subject to numerical precision and Monte Carlo error.
The four nonlinear endpoints define approximate posteriors.  Convergence to
those numerical targets does not establish their equality to the original
nonlinear posteriors.  Nor does this campaign establish a ranking against
bootstrap or Gaussian filters: that would require a comparison with those
methods and uncertainty across replicated data.

remaining scientific limitation: the endpoint approximation must be improved or
benchmarked against stronger references before exact nonlinear-posterior claims
are made.
