\section{Learning guides for moments and scores without a posterior oracle}
\label{sec:supervised-guidance}

\noindent\textbf{Empirical status, 23 October 2026.}
The $N=64$ studies reported in this section are disqualified. Their results,
trained selections and negative conclusions must not support scientific or
performance decisions. The minimum particle count for fresh experiments is
$N=1008$; the propositions below do not establish the outcome of those experiments.

Contract E preserves specified moments of the weighted cloud presented to the
reset. It cannot correct a cloud whose moments already differ from the desired
posterior moments. We therefore keep the reset and its analytical derivative
fixed and change the objective used to fit and select the upstream guide.
The distinction is consequential: a small error in a backward-message
regression, or small variability of importance weights, need not imply a
small error in the parameter score. The proposal below trains against the
quantities we wish to estimate, while avoiding an observed-score oracle.

The starting point is simulation. A simulated latent state is not a posterior
mean, but it is a noisy label whose conditional mean is precisely that
posterior mean. This observation gives an exact population-risk identity.
Complete-data parameter scores provide an analogous label for the observed-data
score. Neither identity asserts that a finite optimization finds its population
minimum, or that improvement averaged over simulated datasets transfers to
every particular observation sequence.

\subsection{Why one simulated trajectory can supervise conditional moments}

Let $C$ be the conditioning information available to the estimator, and let
$V=\Phi(X,C)\in\R^r$ be a vector of features of a simulated latent variable.
For filtering moments $C=(\theta,Y_{1:t})$; for a proposal informed by future
observations it may instead be $(\theta,X_{t-1},Y_{t:T})$. These are different
conditional distributions and their labels must not be interchanged. Define
$\mu(C)=\mathbb E[V\mid C]$. The estimator
$A_\eta(C,U)$ uses guide settings $\eta$ and algorithmic randomness $U$.
The features can contain state coordinates, raw products $X_jX_k$, and
selected nonlinear functions appearing in transition or observation scores.
Raw products are labels for second moments; covariance is subsequently
obtained by subtracting the outer product of the estimated mean.

\begin{assumption}[Simulation labels and information separation]
\label{ass:sg-separation}
Training pairs $(C,V)$ have the declared model law. Conditional on $C$, the
particle randomness $U$ is independent of $V$. For each fixed candidate
$\eta$, $A_\eta$ is measurable with respect to $(C,U)$ and does not receive
the latent label. Both $V$ and $A_\eta$ are square integrable. The symmetric
weight matrix $W(C)$ is positive semidefinite and specified without access to
the latent evaluation label beyond $C$; it is the same for every candidate.
All displayed weighted second moments are finite. Learned weights and settings are treated
as fixed when evaluating a fresh validation or test dataset.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Moment supervision without known posterior moments]
\label{prop:sg-moment-risk}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sg-separation}, with
$\|v\|_W^2=v^\top Wv$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E\|A_\eta-V\|_W^2
=\mathbb E\|A_\eta-\mu(C)\|_W^2
 +\mathbb E\|V-\mu(C)\|_W^2.
\label{eq:sg-moment-risk}
\end{equation}
Consequently the supervised population loss and the actual conditional-moment
MSE have the same minimizers over any fixed candidate family.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $A_\eta-V=(A_\eta-\mu)-(V-\mu)$ and expand the square.
The mixed term has conditional expectation
\[
\mathbb E[(A_\eta-\mu)^\top W(V-\mu)\mid C,U]
=(A_\eta-\mu)^\top W\,
  \mathbb E[V-\mu\mid C,U]=0.
\]
The last equality uses conditional independence and the definition of $\mu$.
Taking expectations gives the identity. Its second term is independent of
$\eta$, proving the minimizer statement.
\end{proof}

For example, if $X\mid C$ is scalar normal with mean $m$ and variance $v$,
then $\mathbb E[(a-X)^2\mid C]=(a-m)^2+v$.
Replacing $X$ by $X^2$ gives a label for $m^2+v$; its irreducible conditional
variance is $2v^2+4m^2v$. Higher-order features therefore bring additional
label noise. We choose them because the score uses them, rather than assuming
that matching every available moment must help. A moment-matching objective
alone gives no theorem about the derivative of the finite LEDH program.

\subsection{Complete-data labels target the actual LEDH score risk}

Fix a parameter $\theta$ and a joint density $p_\theta(x,y)$ with a
parameter-independent dominating measure. Suppose differentiation can pass
under the integral defining $p_\theta(y)$, the relevant scores are square
integrable, and $p_\theta(y)>0$. Define
\begin{equation}
 H_\theta(x,y)=\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(x,y),\qquad
 s_\theta(y)=\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(y).
\label{eq:sg-score-definitions}
\end{equation}
The latent path is held fixed in $H_\theta$. Differentiating the simulated
path itself with common base noise generally computes a different quantity.

\begin{proposition}[Score supervision and paired comparisons]
\label{prop:sg-score-risk}
Under the preceding regularity conditions,
$\mathbb E[H_\theta(X,Y)\mid Y]=s_\theta(Y)$. For any estimator
$\widehat s_{\theta,\eta}(Y,U)$ satisfying the separation and integrability
conditions of Assumption~\ref{ass:sg-separation},
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E\|\widehat s_{\theta,\eta}-H_\theta\|_W^2
 =\mathbb E\|\widehat s_{\theta,\eta}-s_\theta(Y)\|_W^2+C_\theta,
 \quad C_\theta=\mathbb E\|H_\theta-s_\theta(Y)\|_W^2.
\label{eq:sg-score-risk}
\end{equation}
For two fixed candidates $a,b$, the expectation of their difference in
supervised losses equals their difference in true score MSE.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For almost every $y$ with positive marginal density,
\[
 \int H_\theta(x,y)p_\theta(x\mid y)\,\dd x
 =\frac{1}{p_\theta(y)}\int\nabla_\theta p_\theta(x,y)\,\dd x
 =\frac{\nabla_\theta p_\theta(y)}{p_\theta(y)}.
\]
This is the stated conditional identity. Apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:sg-moment-risk} with $C=(\theta,Y)$ and $V=H_\theta$.
Subtracting the identities for $a$ and $b$ cancels $C_\theta$.
\end{proof}

Here $\widehat s$ can be the analytical total derivative of the same frozen
finite LEDH likelihood program used for evaluation. The result does not
replace that derivative by a smoothing estimator: complete-data scores are
training labels only. Nor does it require $\widehat s$ to be unbiased.
The risk includes both squared bias and particle variance. If $\theta$ is
drawn from a specified training distribution, integrating the identity over
that distribution gives the corresponding average-risk statement. A fit at
one $\theta$ establishes no coverage of other parameter regions.

The population identity does not make an in-sample loss difference an unbiased
assessment of a selected candidate. Select settings using calibration and
validation data, freeze them, and assess them on independent test sequences.
For each sequence share its label and, where possible, particle seeds between
candidates. Average particle replicates within a sequence before computing
uncertainty across independent sequences. Reusing a seed to generate both the
latent label and the particle approximation can invalidate the proof.

\subsection{Exact labels for the SIR scope actually being computed}

The active continuous SIR comparison has
$X_0\sim\mathcal N(m_0,I)$,
$X_t\mid X_{t-1}\sim\mathcal N(f_\theta(X_{t-1}),I)$ and
$Y_t\mid X_t\sim\mathcal N(BX_t,\sigma_\theta^2 I_9)$,
where $B$ extracts infectious coordinates and
$\sigma_\theta=10e^{\theta_3}$. The first two parameters are log scales of
infection and removal coefficients in the existing RK4 transition.
This is the continuous law used by the shared consumer, not an assertion
that a simulator which clips susceptible states has the same joint density.
The initial distribution is parameter independent in this scope.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-path SIR score labels]
\label{prop:sg-sir-label}
Let $r_t=X_t-f_\theta(X_{t-1})$ and $e_t=Y_t-BX_t$.
The complete-data label in these three parameter coordinates is
\begin{equation}
 H_j=\sum_{t=1}^T
       r_t^\top\partial_{\theta_j}f_\theta(X_{t-1}),\quad j=1,2,
 \qquad
 H_3=\sum_{t=1}^T(\|e_t\|^2/\sigma_\theta^2-9).
\label{eq:sg-sir-label}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The transition log density contributes $-\tfrac12 r_t^\top r_t$ and a
parameter-independent normalizer. At fixed adjacent states,
$\partial_{\theta_j}r_t=-\partial_{\theta_j}f_\theta(X_{t-1})$, giving
the first expression. The observation term is
$-9\log\sigma_\theta-\|e_t\|^2/(2\sigma_\theta^2)$ plus a constant.
Since $\partial_{\theta_3}\log\sigma_\theta=1$ and $BX_t$ is fixed,
its derivative is the second expression. Sum over time; the initial law
contributes zero.
\end{proof}

The implementation must reuse the consumer's transition and its analytical
parameter tangent with zero incoming state tangent. This prevents a second,
inconsistent SIR integrator from defining the labels. A separate fixed-path
finite-difference check verifies the sum of joint log densities. For an
alternative clipped model, one must instead use a correct mixed-measure
density or a continuous pre-clipping augmentation with the correct observation
and transition laws. Simply evaluating a Gaussian density on clipped states
would violate the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:sg-score-risk}.

\subsection{Changing controlled SMC and KDM at their actual consumers}

Controlled SMC supplies a useful initial policy through the backward
least-squares recursion of \citet[Section 4.2, equations (20)--(21)]{heng2020controlled}.
We retain that recursion and evaluate settings such as basis, iteration count
and guide strength by the moments or analytical scores produced downstream.
The first implementation can select a finite, declared bank of such settings;
continuous optimization of every control coefficient is a further method,
requiring its own derivative and optimization checks. A smaller backward
residual is an explanatory measurement, not the supervised objective.

For KDM, \citet[Section 5, equation (18)]{younis2023} train density NLL and
also describe subsequent refinement of the weighted mean by MSE; Appendix
D.1, equation (23), defines that mean loss. Their released full-sequence
trainer evaluates the chosen loss on latent-state labels. Replacing the mean
feature by selected features gives Proposition~\ref{prop:sg-moment-risk};
using the actual LEDH score and complete-data labels gives
Proposition~\ref{prop:sg-score-risk}. These are adaptations. Our positive-RBF
SIR message export is KDM-inspired and does not become original neural KDM
training merely by changing its selection objective.

The consumer matters as much as the loss. If a Gaussian base is multiplied
by a Gaussian-mixture message, each product component has mean $m_k$ and
covariance $C_k$, and normalized product weight $\alpha_k$. Its moments are
\begin{equation}
 \bar m=\sum_k\alpha_km_k,\qquad
 \bar C=\sum_k\alpha_k\{C_k+(m_k-\bar m)(m_k-\bar m)^\top\}.
\label{eq:sg-product-moments}
\end{equation}
This follows by expanding
$\mathbb E[(X-\bar m)(X-\bar m)^\top\mid k]$ and averaging over $k$.
Averaging component precision matrices omits the displayed between-component
dispersion. We can use $\mathcal N(\bar m,\bar C)$ as the proposal while
retaining all analytical derivatives of these moments. It is a moment-matched
Gaussian, not a draw from the full product mixture. Consequently the
importance denominator must be this Gaussian density, followed by the
existing flow Jacobian correction. The original target density is unchanged.

\begin{proposition}[Admissible quadratic guidance]
\label{prop:sg-quadratic-domain}
Multiplying $\mathcal N(m,Q)$, $Q\succ0$, by
$\exp(-x^\top\Lambda x/2+b^\top x)$ with symmetric $\Lambda$
defines a normalizable Gaussian density if and only if
$Q^{-1}+\Lambda\succ0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The exponent has quadratic precision $P=Q^{-1}+\Lambda$ and linear term
$Q^{-1}m+b$. When $P\succ0$, completing the square gives covariance
$P^{-1}$ and mean $P^{-1}(Q^{-1}m+b)$ and a finite Gaussian integral.
If $P$ has a negative eigenvalue, the integral grows exponentially along
that eigendirection. If it is positive semidefinite and singular, integration
along a null direction diverges whether the corresponding linear coefficient
is zero or nonzero. These exhaust the symmetric cases.
\end{proof}

Thus $\Lambda\succeq0$ is sufficient but unnecessarily restrictive. The
optional unprojected quadratic candidate must pass the actual total-precision
check at every consumed covariance. Failure rejects the candidate; it does
not authorize clipping or silently changing the target. Product-moment and
unprojected-quadratic settings are evaluated separately, so that changed
representation and changed fitting objective can be distinguished.

\subsection{Executable training and evaluation order}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Declare the exact model law, parameter coordinates and training region;
choose moment or score loss and its frozen scaling. Partition simulation seeds
into calibration, validation and test sets before fitting.
\item Simulate $(X,Y)$ and compute fixed-path labels. Pass only $Y$, the
declared parameter and independent fit randomness to cSMC/KDM. Latent paths
are retained solely in the label calculation.
\item Fit backward messages on observations; construct each declared guide
candidate. For each candidate run the shared full analytical LEDH executor
with independent particle noise, Contract E and OT $(1,5)$. Include the
unchanged and unguided comparators. Compute losses from the actual consumed
proposal and actual final score, rather than a detached guide-only prediction.
\item Select guide settings on calibration/validation data, with numerical
validity checked before loss. Freeze all choices and evaluate independent
test sequences. Keep latent labels and particle randomness independent
conditionally on the observations and parameter throughout.
\item Report paired score-loss differences and their uncertainty, separately
from moment error and message fit. A finite bank implements supervised
selection; it does not establish globally optimal controls, neural capacity
training, or canonical tuning admission.
\end{enumerate}

Offline fitting parameters remain fixed in the analytical score recursion.
If a future implementation makes guide coefficients explicit functions of
$\theta$ inside the value program, their derivatives must be included; a
frozen-guide partial derivative must not be called that program's total
derivative. Optimization over guide settings does not require differentiating
the score: a finite candidate bank already supplies a valid first experiment.
For gradient optimization, the required mixed derivatives concern training
parameters and must be distinguished from the analytical model-score path.

\subsection{The first SIR implementation and its limits}
\label{sec:sg-pilot}

The first implementation selects a finite bank of guide settings using the
analytical score produced by the shared LEDH executor. It includes the UKF
covariance lifecycle, flow, Contract E reset and both diagonal and pairwise
GenUT corrections. The kernel alternative uses the fitted positive-RBF
message described above. It does not train a new neural KDM. Direct moment
supervision is implemented as a loss, but the executed bank selection uses
score loss; terminal first- and second-moment errors are explanatory only.

The short SIR experiments use 64 particles at $\theta=(0,0,0)$, two
calibration sequences, two validation sequences and four untouched test
sequences, each evaluated with two independent particle streams. OT remains
$(1,5)$, with 32 terminal balancing updates also held fixed. Computation uses
double precision on the GPU as a diagnostic
exception. These small experiments test the learning signal and the actual
consumer, rather than establish settings for a longer horizon or a different
parameter region.

At $T=2$, the unprojected quadratic guide was initially rejected by an
implementation check requiring $\Lambda\succeq0$. Proposition
\ref{prop:sg-quadratic-domain} shows why that condition is unnecessarily
restrictive. An explicit optional setting now checks positive total precision
instead, while preserving the old default. Tests cover both an admissible
indefinite guide and a nonnormalizable proposal. The failed rows are retained
as implementation failures; they are not evidence against unprojected guidance.

\subsection{Why a valid future guide can lose its intended effect in the flow}
\label{sec:sg-flow-coordinates}

The preceding risk identity supplies an objective; it does not establish that
the current proposal family can reduce it. There is a further distinction
between fitting a future message and applying that message in the coordinates
where it was fitted. The backward message $h_t(x)$ is a function of the
physical state after the current observation update. Our implemented adapter
instead multiplies a Gaussian density before the LEDH transformation by a
quadratic approximation to this message. This is the pre-flow algorithm
specified in Section~\ref{sec:backward-guidance}; its importance correction
remains valid. It is generally different from tilting the distribution after
the flow by the same future message.

\begin{proposition}[Transporting the argument of a guide]
\label{prop:sg-guide-coordinates}
Condition on an incoming cloud and one ancestor's fixed invertible affine
flow $F(z)=Bz+c$. Let $r(z)$ be its unguided pre-flow density, and let
$q_F(x)=r(F^{-1}(x))|\det B|^{-1}$. For a positive guide $h$ with finite,
nonzero normalizers, drawing $Z$ from $r(z)h(z)/C_{\rm pre}$ gives
\begin{equation}
 q_{\rm pre}(x)
 =\frac{q_F(x)h(F^{-1}(x))}{C_{\rm pre}},
 \qquad C_{\rm pre}=\int r(z)h(z)\,\dd z.
 \label{eq:sg-preflow-tilt}
\end{equation}
To obtain $q_{\rm post}(x)\propto q_F(x)h(x)$ instead, the pre-flow guide
must be $h(F(z))$. For symmetric $\Lambda$ and
$h(x)=\exp\{-\tfrac12x^\top\Lambda x+b^\top x\}$, its coefficients
in the pre-flow coordinates are
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde\Lambda=B^\top\Lambda B,
 \qquad \widetilde b=B^\top(b-\Lambda c).
 \label{eq:sg-pulled-guide}
\end{equation}
For $r=\mathcal N(m,Q)$ with $Q\succ0$, this defines a normalizable Gaussian proposal
when $Q^{-1}+\widetilde\Lambda$ is positive definite.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Changing variables from $z$ to $x=F(z)$ in $r(z)h(z)/C_{\rm pre}$ gives
\eqref{eq:sg-preflow-tilt}. Replacing $h(z)$ by $h(F(z))$ gives the
stated post-flow tilt, with normalizer $\int r(z)h(F(z))\,\dd z$.
Expanding the quadratic yields
\[
 -\tfrac12(Bz+c)^\top\Lambda(Bz+c)+b^\top(Bz+c)
 =-\tfrac12z^\top B^\top\Lambda Bz
   +z^\top B^\top(b-\Lambda c)
   -\tfrac12c^\top\Lambda c+b^\top c.
\]
The last two terms are constant in $z$. Adding the Gaussian log density
leaves precision $Q^{-1}+B^\top\Lambda B$, proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

This calculation concerns each ancestor conditional on the incoming cloud.
The shared executor supplies the affine matrix from the actual auxiliary
trajectory, as established in Proposition~\ref{prop:bg-flow-jacobian}.
Its nonlinear observation model therefore does not invalidate this argument.
If $B$ and $c$ depend on $\theta$, an aligned analytical score must include
their derivatives in \eqref{eq:sg-pulled-guide}, even when the fitted
$\Lambda,b$ are held fixed. Alignment would reproduce the tilt of $q_F$;
it would equal the exact smoothing conditional only if $q_F$ were the
appropriate exact current-observation conditional and $h$ were exact.
Gaussian moment closure introduces an additional approximation for mixture
guides, so the exact-tilt statement is not a claim about that closure.

A scalar example shows that this distinction can matter even with a perfect
message. Let one static latent state have prior $X\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$,
and let $Y_1,Y_2$ be conditionally independent with
$Y_j\mid X\sim\mathcal N(X,1)$.
After observing the first value $2$, the posterior is
$\mathcal N(1,1/2)$. Its exact affine transport from the prior is
$F(z)=1+z/\sqrt2$. If the second observation is $1$, its exact message is
$h(x)\propto\exp\{-(x-1)^2/2\}$ and the two-observation posterior is
$\mathcal N(1,1/3)$. Multiplying the prior by $h(z)$ first gives
$\mathcal N(1/2,1/2)$; applying $F$ then gives
$\mathcal N(1+1/(2\sqrt2),1/4)$. The mean moves away from the correct
value, although the unguided mean was already correct. In fact,
$D_{\rm KL}(p_{\rm target}\Vert q)$ increases from $0.03607$ for the
unguided proposal to $0.27283$ for the unaligned guide. Applying $h(F(z))$
instead gives the exact target. This is a counterexample to a guaranteed
benefit from the pre-flow construction, not a measurement of its causal
contribution to the SIR errors.

There is also no general implication from likelihood accuracy to score
accuracy. Write a positive differentiable likelihood approximation as
$\widehat Z_\theta=Z_\theta(1+e_\theta)$. Direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_\theta\log\widehat Z_\theta-\nabla_\theta\log Z_\theta
 =\frac{\nabla_\theta e_\theta}{1+e_\theta}.
 \label{eq:sg-value-versus-score}
\end{equation}
For example, $e_\theta=\delta\sin(\theta/\delta^2)$, with
$0<\delta<1$, has uniformly small amplitude but score error $1/\delta$
at $\theta=0$. Lower weight variance in a separate controlled-SMC fit
therefore cannot establish accuracy of the derivative of the LEDH program.

The saved SIR results expose this gap. Mean ESS in the separate 512-particle
controlled-SMC pilot rises descriptively from about 181 to 505. Yet the
guides in the consumer were selected from a small bank: their coefficients
were not optimized against the supervised score objective. The kernel fits
the already fitted cSMC future message, adding another approximation. In
the $T=5$ comparison, more than $99.3\%$ of every method's validation and
test loss comes from the scaled observation-noise coordinate. This fraction
depends on the frozen calibration scales; it does not mean the other
unscaled coordinate errors are small. Every guide loses to unguided LEDH
on test sequence 261029508. An aligned guide and stronger supervision remain
testable repairs, while neither better message fits nor this structural
calculation establishes a score improvement. The derivation, consumer check,
and exact arithmetic over the saved results are recorded in
\path{docs/plans/artifacts/ledh-supervised-guidance-20261019/failure-review/review.md}.

\subsection{Training guide coefficients against terminal moment loss}
\label{sec:sg-moment-training}

The moment-training implementation makes the simulated-moment proposal operational:
guide coefficients change in response to the moment error of the full LEDH
calculation. The latent state supplies the training label, while the guide
itself receives observations only. This distinction permits supervision by
simulation without requiring posterior moments for any observed dataset.
The experiment uses the continuous 18-dimensional SIR model at
$\theta=(0,0,0)$, $T=5$, and $N=64$ particles.

The conditioning set matters because the backward guides use future
observations. At an intermediate time $t<T$, an estimate computed with all
$Y_{1:T}$ would be supervised toward a smoothing moment conditional on
$Y_{1:T}$, even if the output were named a filtering estimate. We therefore
train the terminal cloud, where the available information is exactly the
filtering information $C=(\theta,Y_{1:T})$. Training a proposal conditional on
its parent and future observations would be a separate conditional-moment
problem, as described in Proposition~\ref{prop:sg-moment-risk}.

To compare susceptible and infectious coordinates on useful scales, we first
simulate 256 independent calibration trajectories. Their terminal states
determine a fixed center $m$, coordinate standard deviations $d_j>0$, and
feature standard deviations $s_j>0$; standard deviations are floored at
$10^{-6}$. Put
\begin{equation}
 z_j(x)=\frac{x_j-m_j}{d_j},\qquad
 \phi(x)=\bigl(z(x),\operatorname{vech}\{z(x)z(x)^\top\}\bigr).
 \label{eq:sg-calibrated-features}
\end{equation}
There are 18 first features and 171 second features. If the terminal equal
weight cloud after Contract E and its higher-moment correction is
$\{\widehat X_T^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^N$, its estimate is
$A_\eta=N^{-1}\sum_i\phi(\widehat X_T^{(i)})$. A simulated terminal state
$X_T$ gives one noisy label $\phi(X_T)$. The implemented loss gives the two
feature blocks equal total weight:
\begin{equation}
 \ell_\eta=\frac{1}{2}\left[
 \frac{1}{18}\sum_{j=1}^{18}
     \left\{\frac{A_{\eta,j}-\phi_j(X_T)}{s_j}\right\}^2
 +\frac{1}{171}\sum_{j=19}^{189}
     \left\{\frac{A_{\eta,j}-\phi_j(X_T)}{s_j}\right\}^2
 \right].
 \label{eq:sg-terminal-training-loss}
\end{equation}
Thus the much larger second-feature block does not dominate simply by having
more entries. Centering also prevents the large susceptible-population level
from obscuring variation around that level. The same calibration is used for
every guide and every evaluation dataset.

\begin{proposition}[The implemented loss estimates a fixed moment risk]
\label{prop:sg-implemented-moment-risk}
Condition on the calibration sample and on the data used to learn and select
$\eta$. On a fresh model trajectory, suppose the guide-fitting and particle
randomness is conditionally independent of $X_T$ given $Y_{1:T}$, and the
required second moments are finite. The expectation of
\eqref{eq:sg-terminal-training-loss} equals the weighted MSE of $A_\eta$ for
$\mathbb E[\phi(X_T)\mid Y_{1:T}]$, plus a term independent of $\eta$.
It is also a fixed quadratic risk in the first and raw second moments of
the original state coordinates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on calibration, the diagonal weights in
\eqref{eq:sg-terminal-training-loss} are fixed and positive. Proposition
\ref{prop:sg-moment-risk} therefore applies to $V=\phi(X_T)$.
To identify the moments in the original coordinates, expand a second feature:
\[
 z_j(x)z_k(x)=\frac{x_jx_k-m_jx_k-m_kx_j+m_jm_k}{d_jd_k}.
\]
Consequently $\phi(x)=B\Phi(x)+c$ for a fixed matrix $B$ and vector $c$,
where $\Phi(x)=(x,\operatorname{vech}(xx^\top))$. The constant cancels in
an estimation error, and its quadratic weight in raw coordinates is
$B^\top W B$. This proves the asserted risk relation. Centering introduces
cross terms between first- and second-moment errors; it does not change the
conditioning information.
\end{proof}

For example, improving the estimated susceptible mean can change the centered
second-moment loss even if the estimated raw second moment stays fixed.
We therefore retain unscaled first- and raw-second-moment label errors as
separate descriptive measurements. None of these losses is directly the MSE
of a covariance estimate, which also contains the estimated mean's outer
product.

The learned coefficients adapt two existing observation-fitted guide families.
For a quadratic guide with precision $P_t$ and linear coefficient $b_t$,
let $a_t$ be a log precision multiplier and let $\delta_t$ repeat one
susceptible and one infectious displacement across the nine regions. The
adapted coefficients are
\begin{equation}
 P_t(\eta)=e^{a_t}P_t,\qquad
 b_t(\eta)=e^{a_t}(b_t+P_t\delta_t).
 \label{eq:sg-quadratic-adaptation}
\end{equation}
Indeed, expanding $e^{a_t}\log h_t(x-\delta_t)$ gives these coefficients,
up to an $x$-independent constant. For the positive-RBF mixture, with weights
$\pi_{tk}$, centers $c_{tk}$ and covariances $V_{tk}$, the adaptation is
\begin{equation}
 h_{t,\eta}(x)=\sum_k\pi_{tk}\,\mathcal N
       (x;c_{tk}+\delta_t,e^{-a_t}V_{tk}).
 \label{eq:sg-rbf-adaptation}
\end{equation}
The mixture weights stay fixed. This covariance rescaling is not the same
operation as raising the entire mixture density to a power. Both adaptations
reduce to the original guide at $\eta=0$, and future guidance is inactive at
the terminal time. These RBF coefficients are a KDM-inspired message adapter;
the experiment does not retrain the original neural KDM.

One candidate ties the three coefficients across the four active times;
another gives each active time its own three coefficients. The resulting
three- and twelve-parameter families test a limited form of temporal
adaptation. Log multipliers stay within $[-\log4,\log4]$, and each displacement
stays within two process-noise standard deviations. Region-specific
adaptation and larger neural families remain outside this experiment.

Optimization evaluates the actual shared LEDH program, including its UKF
covariance lifecycle, flow, Contract E and dual-cap GenUT correction. At each
update it draws four training datasets and holds their particle randomness
fixed while comparing $\eta$, $\eta+\rho u$ and $\eta-\rho u$ for a random
unit direction $u$. The sign of the symmetric loss difference determines
the descent direction. Up to three successively halved step lengths are
tested on that same minibatch; a step is accepted only when it lowers the
minibatch loss and all numerical checks pass. Fresh minibatches and particle
streams are used at the next update. This is continuous coefficient
optimization using moment loss. It does not require an observed score label
or an autodifferentiated model-score computation.

The initial protocol uses 48 updates, $\rho=0.1$ and initial step length
$0.25$, with two optimizer seeds for each family and capacity. Initialization
and every sixteenth update are evaluated on validation data. Failure to make
at least two accepted updates or to improve validation loss triggers a
predeclared 96-update retry with $\rho=0.025$ and step length $0.0625$.
All attempts are retained. The fixed analytical model-score recursion remains
available for separate explanatory measurements; it never selects an update
or a checkpoint.

Training uses 64 simulated datasets, validation uses 16, and final evaluation
uses 64 further datasets. Calibration, data partitions, fitting randomness
and particle streams have separate seed namespaces. Validation and final
evaluation each use two particle replicates per dataset. Within each guide
family, capacity, optimizer seed and checkpoint are frozen using validation moment
loss before final evaluation. The comparison includes unguided LEDH,
half-strength quadratic guidance and both original guides, all evaluated
afresh with identical datasets, particle streams and fixed numerical controls.
OT remains at $\epsilon=1$ with five Sinkhorn iterations and 32 terminal
balancing updates; all runs use GPU/XLA double precision as a scoped
diagnostic.

The independent unit for uncertainty is the simulated dataset. We average its
two particle replicates before forming paired differences. Four comparisons
contrast each trained family with its original guide and with unguided LEDH;
10,000 paired bootstrap resamples give nominal 98.75\% two-sided intervals,
corresponding to a Bonferroni family-wise level of 95\%. Unknown posterior
moments are unnecessary for this comparison, but label noise remains in a
finite test sample and can make the intervals inconclusive. Low and high
terminal-observation regimes, separated by the calibration median, also test
whether a favorable pooled mean hides failure against a simple guide.
Conditional means are descriptive; underperformance against any of the four
simple comparators prevents promotion under the declared experiment plan.

An exact diagnostic replay reproduced the failure with identical observations,
latent labels, particle randomness and the ten captured runtime source hashes.
The first three filtering times remained finite. At time four, the UKF,
transport and Contract E reset checks passed, but the subsequent higher-moment
correction returned nonfinite values in all $64\times18=1152$ particle
coordinates and their analytical directional tangents. Its recorded post-cap
cloud was still finite. Further replay isolated the first nonfinite operation:
the Cholesky factorization of the current covariance inside affine restoration.
The largest importance weight rounded to one, although all 64 weights remained
positive. The weighted source covariance had trace $4.77\times10^{-29}$,
while the largest mean coordinate was about $433.68$. The capped offsets from
that mean still had covariance trace $2.35\times10^{-29}$. Adding the mean
rounded all particles to the same stored FP64 vector. Recomputing their
covariance therefore gave zero, as did the existing ridge proportional to its
trace. Cholesky of this zero matrix failed and contaminated the analytical
tangents. The earlier pre-cap diagonal RMS of about $9.57\times10^{39}$
indicates a severe shape correction, but it was not the first nonfinite
operation. The failure concerns loss of representable spread, not whether
an acceptance tolerance should reject an error of order $10^{-13}$.

The repair delays addition of the common mean until after centering and
affine restoration. This retains information already available in the
calculation; it does not inject artificial particle variation. Write the
pre-restoration particles as $p_i=m+o_i$, where $m$ is the common translation
and $o_i$ are the retained offsets. Both may depend on the model parameters.
Let bars denote uniform particle averages and let $\dot{\ }$ denote one
parameter-direction derivative.

\begin{proposition}[Translation-free evaluation of affine restoration]
\label{prop:sg-offset-restoration}
In real arithmetic, centering the offsets instead of the translated particles
leaves the regularized affine-restoration map and its total directional
derivative unchanged, wherever its covariance factors are positive definite.
This statement includes the derivative of the existing trace-scaled ridge.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\bar p=m+\bar o$, the centered particles and their derivatives are
\[
 c_i=p_i-\bar p=o_i-\bar o,\qquad
 \dot c_i=\dot p_i-\dot{\bar p}=\dot o_i-\dot{\bar o}.
\]
Thus the current covariance and its derivative can both be computed without
adding and subtracting $m$:
\[
 C=\frac1N\sum_i c_i c_i^\top,\qquad
 \dot C=\frac1N\sum_i
 (\dot c_i c_i^\top+c_i\dot c_i^\top).
\]
For the fixed existing ridge coefficient $\delta$, the regularized covariance
and derivative are $C_\delta=C+\delta\operatorname{tr}(C)I/d$ and
$\dot C_\delta=\dot C+\delta\operatorname{tr}(\dot C)I/d$. They are
therefore unchanged by the representation. With
$L_C=\operatorname{chol}(C_\delta)$, target factor $L_\star$ and target
mean $\mu_\star$ computed from the same weighted source cloud, restoration
returns $\mu_\star+L_\star L_C^{-1}c_i$. Every argument of this map and
its directional derivative is identical under the two representations.
\end{proof}

In floating-point arithmetic the order of evaluation matters: subtracting a
mean cannot recover offsets already lost when that mean was added. The shared
implementation now carries the offsets and their analytical tangents into
restoration. The covariance ridge, OT settings $(1,5)$ and balancing count
remain unchanged. In the three saved pre-failure clouds, the largest scaled
change in particles or tangents was $9.82\times10^{-11}$, below the declared
$10^{-10}$ local non-harm tolerance. The failed endpoint and a separate
healthy endpoint then returned finite states, tangents, values and all three
score components on GPU/XLA. All twelve central-difference checks, using
steps $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-5}$, passed the declared normalized-error limit
$10^{-3}$; the largest error was $7.54\times10^{-5}$. These are numerical
checks on the implemented finite calculation, not tests against a true score.

One limitation remains. The final addition of the target mean still collapses
the exceptional cloud to one stored vector. Its materialized covariance is
zero, and its standardized third and fourth moments are undefined. A tiny
absolute covariance residual therefore hides a 100 percent loss of the
target variance in this case. The calculation now explicitly reports that
these shape diagnostics are unavailable; it neither replaces them with zero
nor treats them as passing. Finite value and score computation is repaired,
but complete moment fidelity and the stronger all-diagnostics-finite check
have not been established.

The distinction matters scientifically. The simulated-label risk identity in
Proposition~\ref{prop:sg-implemented-moment-risk} remains correct under its
stated conditioning and independence assumptions. The implemented optimizer
can reduce its minibatch objective and select nontrivial guides. Nevertheless,
this interrupted experiment does not show that those guides improve heldout
moments, still less that they improve the likelihood score. The repair removes
the identified factorization failure without resolving the lost physical
spread. A wider comparison must first address that representation limit
under a reviewed numerical scope, then use fresh independent test data.
The failed comparison is preserved; no numerical controls or promotion
criteria were relaxed to turn it into a successful experiment.

\subsection{Why the proposal concentrated, and what a flow-aware guide must express}
\label{sec:sg-proposal-collapse}

The restoration failure is downstream of a severe importance-weight
imbalance. Replaying the failed sequence with the existing moment-descent
safeguard enabled still produces zero materialized covariance at time four.
The endpoint now rejects the unavailable standardized moments explicitly;
previously it collected their availability flag without using it to reject
the result. A second diagnostic measures covariance error relative to the
target covariance itself, rather than to the maximum of that scale and one.
It reports relative error one on the collapsed cloud. These changes expose
the failure; they do not create missing particle spread.

To identify the preceding mechanism, fix one parent and write its transition
mean as $a$. The SIR transition and observation at that step have the form
\[
 X\mid a\sim\mathcal N(a,Q),\qquad Y\mid X\sim\mathcal N(HX,R).
\]
Here $Q=I_{18}$, $R=100I_9$ at the parameter used in the diagnostic, and $H$
extracts the nine infectious coordinates. The UKF predictive covariance $P$
is a different quantity: it also propagates uncertainty around the parent.
The shared LEDH coefficients use $P$, whereas the zero-guide pre-flow draw
and its importance-density correction use the conditional covariance $Q$.
This is a proposal choice, not a missing Jacobian or an incorrect density
evaluation. An invertible proposal can have a correct importance correction
and still produce almost unusable weights.

For linear observations the implemented flow, conditional on its incoming
cloud and covariance, is affine. Write it as $F(z)=b+Bz$ with nonsingular
$B$. The actual zero-guide output is then
$\mathcal N(b+Ba,BQB^\top)$. Completing the square in the conditional
transition times the observation density gives
\begin{align}
 V&=(Q^{-1}+H^\top R^{-1}H)^{-1},\\
 m&=V(Q^{-1}a+H^\top R^{-1}y),\\
 p(x\mid a)g(y\mid x)&=\mathcal N(y;Ha,HQH^\top+R)\,\mathcal N(x;m,V).
\end{align}
This Gaussian is exact conditional on the one parent. It is not the exact
nonlinear filtering distribution, which also averages over uncertain parents.
It nevertheless supplies a decisive check on where the conditional proposal
places its mass, without knowing any likelihood score.

At time four of the failed sequence, the mean trace of $P$ across particles
is $1038.57$, compared with $\operatorname{tr}(Q)=18$. The mean displacement
of the flow's proposal mean from its parent transition mean is $32.81$. Its
mean conditional Gaussian KL divergence to $\mathcal N(m,V)$ is $529.98$,
and its realized ESS is one. Replaying only this fixed incoming cloud with
$Q$ in the flow coefficients gives conditional KL $8.13\times10^{-9}$ and
ESS $29.0407$; the exact conditional proposal gives ESS $29.0408$. This
reference calculation does not replace the prescribed UKF lifecycle. It
locates the mechanism: the actual flow moves too far for these conditional
transition proposals. The winning particle's prior/proposal logit advantage
is $69.784$, while the observation contributes $-0.808$. Thus the immediate
weight collapse is not caused by a more concentrated observation update.

The shared-flow replay agrees with saved children to $1.14\times10^{-13}$,
proposal log densities to $6.04\times10^{-14}$, and the covariance carry to
$3.46\times10^{-16}$ relative error across both five-step fixtures. The
diagnostic therefore identifies a real proposal mismatch, not a different
reimplementation accidentally being compared with the saved experiment.

Can a guide correct this mismatch without discarding the UKF flow? It must
place pre-flow mass where that flow will carry it, rather than treating its
input coordinates as if they were already physical output coordinates.
\begin{proposition}[Gaussian preimage of the desired conditional proposal]
\label{prop:sg-gaussian-preimage}
Fix $a,Q,m,V$ and an invertible affine map $F(z)=b+Bz$, with $Q,V$ positive
definite. If
\[
 Z\sim\mathcal N(\mu_z,\Sigma_z),\qquad
 \mu_z=B^{-1}(m-b),\quad \Sigma_z=B^{-1}VB^{-\top},
\]
then $F(Z)\sim\mathcal N(m,V)$. Relative to the original pre-flow density
$\mathcal N(a,Q)$, the required multiplicative quadratic guide has precision
and linear coefficient
\[
 \Lambda=B^\top V^{-1}B-Q^{-1},\qquad
 \ell=B^\top V^{-1}(m-b)-Q^{-1}a.
\]
The guide need not have positive semidefinite $\Lambda$, although the product
density has positive definite precision $Q^{-1}+\Lambda$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An affine Gaussian transformation has mean $b+B\mu_z=m$ and covariance
$B\Sigma_z B^\top=V$. The log ratio of its preimage density to
$\mathcal N(a,Q)$ is, up to a constant independent of $z$,
\[
 -\tfrac12 z^\top(\Sigma_z^{-1}-Q^{-1})z
 +z^\top(\Sigma_z^{-1}\mu_z-Q^{-1}a).
\]
Substitution of $\Sigma_z^{-1}=B^\top V^{-1}B$ gives the displayed
coefficients. Their sum with $Q^{-1}$ is positive definite because $B$ is
invertible and $V$ is positive definite. No corresponding sign restriction
holds for their difference. Finally, change of variables gives
$q_z(z)/|\det B|=\mathcal N(F(z);m,V)$. When $m,V$ are the conditional
posterior moments defined above, the corrected conditional weight is exactly
$\mathcal N(y;Ha,HQH^\top+R)$.
\end{proof}

The saved failed step makes the capacity restriction concrete. The required
$\Lambda$ has a minimum eigenvalue of about $-0.477$. The trained quadratic
adapter cannot represent this exact correction: its positive-information
conditioning can only decrease the pre-flow covariance in the Loewner order,
while this preimage requires expansion in some directions. The more general
quadratic class has an optional indefinite-information switch, but the trained
adapter does not supply the parent- and flow-dependent coefficients above.
Merely flipping that switch therefore does not implement this repair.

The trained kernel-mixture consumer has a different covariance calculation.
It uses Gaussian-mixture product moments, retaining the covariance between
component means; that term can expand covariance. The negative eigenvalue
therefore does not establish an expressiveness obstruction for this consumer.
Whether its fitted components and restricted coefficient adapter can realize
the desired preimage has not been checked. What both consumers lack is an
explicit transformation of their proposal through the completed flow.

Applying the computed preimage samples through the actual shared flow
recovers the desired samples within $4.55\times10^{-13}$ on the failed
sequence; its conditional weight identity holds within $1.08\times10^{-12}$.
This verifies a fixed-cloud repair, not an integrated filter or a trained
guide. The next implementation must express guides in the coordinates of
the completed flow, differentiate both the map and the density through their
parameter dependence, and then repeat independent moment evaluation. The
UKF covariance lifecycle, Contract E and OT $(1,5)$ need not be removed to
test that design. The present results do not rank the learned guides, and
they do not invalidate the simulated-label moment-risk identity.

\subsection{Repairing the proposal in physical output coordinates}
\label{sec:sg-physical-repair}

The failed cloud suggests a specific repair, rather than a stronger penalty
on the same pre-flow guide. The UKF covariance describes uncertainty carried
by the filter; the process covariance describes the distribution conditional
on one parent. We retain the former in the canonical LEDH flow and use the
latter when designing the conditional proposal. The proposal must be corrected
by its actual density. Neither a plausible covariance nor a finite numerical
score substitutes for that correction.

Fix a parent, the incoming particle cloud and the auxiliary flow construction.
Write the completed shared affine flow as $F(z)=b+Bz$, with nonsingular $B$.
The transition is $N(a,Q)$, where $a=f_\theta(x_{t-1})$. For a linear Gaussian
observation $y=Hx+e$, $e\sim N(0,R)$, assume $Q\succ0$ and $R\succ0$.
Then $S\succ0$, so its inverse exists; Gaussian conditioning also gives
$V_0\succ0$. Define
\begin{align}
 S&=HQH^\top+R, & K&=QH^\top S^{-1},\label{eq:sg-physical-conditional}\\
 m_0&=a+K(y-Ha), & V_0&=Q-KHQ.\nonumber
\end{align}
These are the moments of $p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{t-1},y_t)$, not filtering
moments pooled across parents. For nonlinear $h$, we instead use
$H=Dh(a)$ and innovation $y-h(a)$. That construction is a local Gaussian
proposal, not the exact conditional law. Its importance correction still
uses the original observation density. SIR's linear Gaussian observation
allows the exact identity to be tested without this additional approximation.

The controlled-SMC message represents future information. Its current-time
observation factor must not be applied a second time. A quadratic message
with coefficients $A,c$ gives physical proposal moments
\[
 V=(V_0^{-1}+A)^{-1},\qquad m=V(V_0^{-1}m_0+c),
\]
provided the total precision is positive definite. A positive kernel mixture
instead conditions $N(m_0,V_0)$ by each component, then retains the weighted
component means and covariances, including between-component variation.
We draw from the Gaussian $N(m,V)$ having these product moments. Accordingly,
the proposal density used below is that Gaussian, not the uncollapsed kernel
mixture. This is the existing KDM moment consumer used in new coordinates;
its moment matching remains an approximation to the future-informed law.

\begin{proposition}[Cancellation and a genuine mixture augmentation]
\label{prop:sg-flow-cancellation}
Let $m,V$ be independent of the particular affine flow, with $V\succ0$.
Drawing $x=m+L\xi$, where $LL^\top=V$ and $\xi\sim N(0,I)$, and setting
$z=B^{-1}(x-b)$ is equivalent to drawing from the preimage proposal and
applying $F$. The resulting physical density is exactly $N(m,V)$ and does
not depend on $F$. Thus this construction alone replaces, rather than
improves, the LEDH proposal.

Let $q_L$ be the actual physical density of the unchanged LEDH proposal
and $q_O=N(m,V)$. For a fixed $0<\beta<1$, draw an independent uniform
$U$, use $q_O$ if $U<\beta$ and $q_L$ otherwise, and correct by
\begin{equation}
 q_\beta(x)=(1-\beta)q_L(x)+\beta q_O(x).
 \label{eq:sg-physical-mixture}
\end{equation}
This is a genuine mixture retaining the LEDH proposal. For any integrable
test function $\varphi$,
\[
 \mathbb E_{q_\beta}\!\left[
   \frac{f_\theta(x\mid x_{t-1})g_\theta(y_t\mid x)}{q_\beta(x)}
   \varphi(x)\right]
 =\int f_\theta(x\mid x_{t-1})g_\theta(y_t\mid x)\varphi(x)\,dx.
\]
If $q_O$ is the exact current-observation conditional, the importance
weight is at most $p_\theta(y_t\mid x_{t-1})/\beta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The affine change of variables gives
\[
 q_z(z)=N(F(z);m,V)|\det B|,\qquad
 \frac{q_z(F^{-1}(x))}{|\det B|}=N(x;m,V).
\]
Using the same $\xi$ gives the stated
pathwise equality. Independent component selection gives
\eqref{eq:sg-physical-mixture}; cancellation of this full density inside
the expectation proves the integral identity. Finally,
$fg=p_\theta(y_t\mid x_{t-1})q_O$ and $q_\beta\geq\beta q_O$ prove the
bound. Predictive likelihoods differ between parents, so this does not
make all particle weights equal. The bound does not apply unchanged to a
future-conditioned or locally linearized $q_O$.
\end{proof}

The mixture selects one draw; it does not average two positions. Its mixing
weight is distinct from the existing identity/global/local covariance-design
weights. Holding $\beta=1/2$ is an explicit hypothesis for the present repair,
not an optimized default. The $\beta=1$ no-future conditional sampler is an
essential cancellation control. If only that control works, we have evidence
for replacing the proposal, not for a beneficial LEDH augmentation.

\begin{proposition}[Analytical differentiation of the repaired proposal]
\label{prop:sg-physical-tangent}
Assume differentiable model quantities and flow coefficients, positive
definite proposal covariances and nonsingular $B$ in an open parameter
neighborhood. Freeze guide coefficients, $\beta$ and all exogenous random
numbers for the finite-program derivative. For the preimage moments,
\begin{align}
 \mu_z&=B^{-1}(m-b), & \Sigma_z&=B^{-1}VB^{-\top},\\
 d\mu_z&=B^{-1}(dm-db-dB\mu_z),\label{eq:sg-physical-preimage-tangent}\\
 d\Sigma_z&=B^{-1}dVB^{-\top}-B^{-1}dB\Sigma_z
                  -\Sigma_z dB^\top B^{-\top}.\nonumber
\end{align}
For $r=x-m$ and $P=V^{-1}$, the moving-point Gaussian density satisfies
\begin{equation}
 d\log N(x;m,V)=-r^\top P(dx-dm)
 +\tfrac12 r^\top P(dV)Pr-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(P\,dV).
 \label{eq:sg-physical-density-tangent}
\end{equation}
At the selected point, set
$\rho_j=\beta_jq_j(x)/q_\beta(x)$, with
$(\beta_L,\beta_O)=(1-\beta,\beta)$. Then
\begin{equation}
 d\log q_\beta(x)=\rho_Ld\log q_L(x)+\rho_Od\log q_O(x).
 \label{eq:sg-physical-mixture-tangent}
\end{equation}
These are total derivatives, including movement of the selected particle,
the incoming parent, the conditional moments and the completed flow.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate $B\mu_z=m-b$ and $B\Sigma_zB^\top=V$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:sg-physical-preimage-tangent}. Differentiating
$-\tfrac12(r^\top V^{-1}r+\log\det V+d\log(2\pi))$, using
$dV^{-1}=-V^{-1}(dV)V^{-1}$, gives
\eqref{eq:sg-physical-density-tangent}. Differentiating the two-term density
sum gives \eqref{eq:sg-physical-mixture-tangent}. The sampled component is
constant as a function of model parameters because both $U$ and $\beta$
are fixed. There is no parameter-dependent discrete selection to differentiate.
For $L=\operatorname{chol}(V)$, write
$M=L^{-1}(dV)L^{-\top}$. Then
$dL=L(\operatorname{tril}(M)-\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(M))$ and
$dx=dm+dL\xi$ supply the sample tangent. All density terms are evaluated
at that same moving point.
\end{proof}

For completeness, the conditional moments in \eqref{eq:sg-physical-conditional}
also move with the parameter. With the observation held fixed, let
$e=y-h_\theta(a)$ and let $dh_\theta(a)$ include both the explicit parameter
derivative and the derivative through $a$. Product differentiation gives
\begin{align}
 dS&=(dH)QH^\top+H(dQ)H^\top+HQ(dH)^\top+dR,\nonumber\\
 dK&=\bigl((dQ)H^\top+Q(dH)^\top-K\,dS\bigr)S^{-1},\nonumber\\
 dm_0&=da+(dK)e-K\,dh_\theta(a),\label{eq:sg-physical-conditional-tangent}\\
 dV_0&=dQ-(dK)HQ-K(dH)Q-KH(dQ).\nonumber
\end{align}
The second line follows by differentiating $KS=QH^\top$ and solving for
$dK$; the other lines differentiate the definitions directly.
If the frozen future message is quadratic, its coefficients $A,c$ have zero
parameter differential. Write $P_0=V_0^{-1}$. Then
\[
 dP_0=-P_0(dV_0)P_0,\qquad
 dV=-V(dP_0)V,\qquad
 dm=(dV)(P_0m_0+c)+V\bigl((dP_0)m_0+P_0\,dm_0\bigr).
\]
For the kernel product, each component obeys the same Gaussian product rule.
Its normalized product weight also changes: if its unnormalized weight is
$w_k$ and $\alpha_k=w_k/\sum_jw_j$, then
$d\alpha_k=\alpha_k(d\log w_k-\sum_j\alpha_jd\log w_j)$.
Consequently both component-moment derivatives and weight derivatives enter
the mixture's first and raw second moments before recovering its covariance.
Holding these weights fixed would omit a required term.

In implementation, $F(a)$ and $B$ come from the existing shared flow loop.
The LEDH component density at any selected $x$ is evaluated through
$\delta=B^{-1}(x-F(a))$:
\[
 q_L(x)=N(\delta;0,Q)/|\det B|,\qquad
 d\delta=B^{-1}(dx-dF(a)-dB\delta).
\]
This avoids a second flow solver. Directly drawing the physical Gaussian is
algebraically equivalent to the preimage-and-flow calculation while avoiding
an unnecessary inverse/forward round trip. For nonlinear observations the
conditional-moment tangent includes $dH$ and $dh(a)$; dropping either when
nonzero is wrong relative to the total-derivative claim. These derivatives
establish the derivative of the finite estimator, not equality to the exact
model score or differentiability through later offline guide refitting.

\paragraph{Algorithm and learning experiment.}
The following order separates proposal design from the filter's covariance
lifecycle and moment-restoring reset.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\arabic*.]
\item Propagate each parent's state, tangent and UKF covariance using the
unchanged canonical recursion. Compute $a,Q$ and their tangents.
\item Run the shared LEDH flow once on the baseline process draw; retain
$x_L,dx_L,F(a),dF(a),B,dB$ and its log determinant and tangent.
\item Form the current-observation conditional Gaussian in physical
coordinates. Condition its moments with the frozen future-only cSMC or KDM
message. Check positive definiteness and finite analytical tangents.
\item Draw $x_O,dx_O$ from those moments. Select exactly one component using
an independent, fixed uniform draw. Evaluate both densities and their total
tangents at the selected point, including the full mixture denominator.
\item Form $\log f+\log g-\log q_\beta$ and its analytical derivative;
combine with incoming ancestor weights. Record component counts, density
validity and weight concentration without changing acceptance criteria.
\item Continue the shared UKF update, dual-cap GenUT correction and
Contract E--Chol OT reset, including all direct moment and weight derivatives.
The diagnostic experiment retains OT $(1,5)$, terminal balance32 and $K=N$.
\end{enumerate}

Offline guide adaptation still minimizes simulated-label moment risk. For
$C=(\theta,Y_{1:T})$, $U$ independent of $X_T$ conditional on $C$, fixed
calibration matrix $W\succeq0$ and $\mu(C)=\mathbb E[\Phi(X_T)\mid C]$,
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widehat\mu_\eta(C,U)-\Phi(X_T)\|_W^2
 =\mathbb E\|\widehat\mu_\eta(C,U)-\mu(C)\|_W^2
  +\mathbb E\|\Phi(X_T)-\mu(C)\|_W^2.
\]
The cross term vanishes by conditioning on $(C,U)$. This justifies learning
without posterior-moment or score labels. It does not make a particular
adapter expressive or its optimizer successful. New calibration, training,
validation and untouched evaluation partitions are required after the failed
campaign; its exposed holdout cannot become a tuning set.

The comparison must distinguish three questions: whether the analytical
implementation is correct, whether the physical conditional repair prevents
the observed collapse, and whether future-trained guides reduce independent
moment risk beyond the no-future repair. Plain LEDH, the cancellation control,
the no-future mixture, untrained guides and trained guides answer different
parts of that question. Prediction-only, observation-only and recursive
Gaussian moment estimators provide cheap external checks in both low- and
high-observation regimes. Heldout paired uncertainty, not training loss or
ESS alone, is needed to establish improvement. None of these moment checks
alone establishes exact model-score accuracy, HMC readiness or a new default.

\subsection{A neural conditional KDM proposal for SIR}
\label{sec:sir-neural-kdm-adaptation}

The preceding RBF message was fitted from a controlled-SMC approximation and
then adjusted by a few scalar coefficients. It did not train the replicated
neural KDM filter. That replicated filter estimates a three-dimensional
position-and-angle filtering distribution. SIR has eighteen state coordinates,
and a filtering density is not a future-likelihood message. Substituting one
for the other would change the stated conditional distribution. We therefore
define an explicit SIR adaptation: a neural mixture used as an evaluable
proposal inside the existing LEDH filter.

For a fixed parent particle \(a\), let \(m_t(a),C_t(a)\) denote the
current-observation conditional Gaussian used by the physical proposal, and
write \(C_t=L_tL_t^\top\) with a positive-definite covariance. The observation
sequence \(Y=y_{1:T}\) is the network input. A network with parameters
\(\eta\) supplies vectors \(\delta_{t,k}(Y)\) and \(s_{t,k}(Y)\), and we set
\begin{align}
 m_{t,k}&=m_t+L_t\delta_{t,k},&
 B_{t,k}&=L_t\operatorname{diag}(e^{s_{t,k}}),&
 C_{t,k}&=B_{t,k}B_{t,k}^{\top},\label{eq:sir-neural-components}\\
 q_\eta(x\mid a,Y)&=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K
       \mathcal N(x;m_{t,k},C_{t,k}),&
 q_{\beta,\eta}&=(1-\beta)q_{\mathrm{LEDH}}+\beta q_\eta.
       \label{eq:sir-neural-proposal}
\end{align}
Here \(m_t,\delta_{t,k},s_{t,k}\in\mathbb R^{18}\) and
\(L_t,B_{t,k},C_{t,k}\in\mathbb R^{18\times18}\). The network returns two
arrays of shape \(T\times K\times18\). Its standardized displacements and
bandwidth multipliers are shared across parents; each parent's own \(m_t(a)\)
and \(L_t(a)\) turn them into different component means and covariances.
The implementation preserves the parent and component axes separately,
with component means of shape \(N\times K\times18\). This restricts the
learned family: the network does not receive a separate parent-state input.
The current Gaussian anchors the proposal to the physical model; the learned
displacements and bandwidths can adapt it to the observations. Setting both
network outputs to zero recovers that Gaussian exactly. The mixture is sampled
by selecting one component, not by averaging particle positions. Its complete
density, including all components, enters the importance denominator.

This first adaptation fixes the inner component probabilities at \(1/K\)
and freezes \(\beta\) within each candidate. It trains every neural location
and bandwidth parameter. These restrictions permit ordinary reparameterized
training without omitting derivatives of learned categorical probabilities.
They also limit the family and must be tested empirically. This differs from
the original learned KDM posterior and its IWSG update
\citep[Section~4, equations~(14)--(15)]{younis2023}.
The original sequence NLL is also replaced by the terminal moment objective
below; the adaptation is not a source-faithful SIR replication.

\begin{proposition}[Physical correction of a future-informed proposal]
\label{prop:sir-neural-physical-correction}
Fix the incoming cloud and all proposal maps. Suppose
\(q_{\beta,\eta}(x\mid a,Y)>0\) wherever
\(h_t(x,a)=f_\theta(x\mid a)g_\theta(y_t\mid x)>0\).
For any feature \(\phi\) for which \(\int |\phi(x)|h_t(x,a)\,dx<\infty\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{q_{\beta,\eta}}\!\left[
 \frac{h_t(X,a)}{q_{\beta,\eta}(X\mid a,Y)}\phi(X)\right]
 =\int h_t(x,a)\phi(x)\,dx.
\]
Thus future observations may inform the proposal without changing the
unnormalized current filtering target.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write the expectation as an integral against
\(q_{\beta,\eta}(x\mid a,Y)\,dx\) and cancel that density pointwise on
the support of \(h_t\). Integrability justifies the expectation. Summing this
identity over parents with their incoming weights gives the corresponding
finite-cloud predictive mixture identity. If parents are randomly selected,
their selection probabilities must also appear in the joint denominator.
The one-to-one consumer used here does not introduce that extra selection.
\end{proof}

This is ordinary proposal correction. It does not introduce controlled-SMC
twisted intermediate targets or claim an unbiased normalized moment estimator
at finite particle count. Contract E and the finite particle approximation
remain part of the estimator whose error is measured.

\begin{proposition}[Analytical model derivative of the neural proposal]
\label{prop:sir-neural-total-derivative}
Fix \(Y,\eta,\beta\), all component-selection uniforms and all Gaussian draws.
Assume the physical maps are differentiable, the relevant covariances are
positive definite, and the LEDH affine maps are invertible. The network has no
\(\theta\) input in this experiment. A dot denotes a directional derivative
with respect to \(\theta\). Then
\begin{align*}
 \dot m_{t,k}&=\dot m_t+\dot L_t\delta_{t,k},&
 \dot B_{t,k}&=\dot L_t\operatorname{diag}(e^{s_{t,k}}),&
 \dot C_{t,k}&=\dot B_{t,k}B_{t,k}^{\top}
                    +B_{t,k}\dot B_{t,k}^{\top}.
\end{align*}
For a selected Gaussian draw, \(x=m_{t,k}+B_{t,k}z\) and
\(\dot x=\dot m_{t,k}+\dot B_{t,k}z\). If \(\ell_j\) denotes the log
density of any component of the full physical mixture, evaluated at this
moving point, and \(\pi_j\) its fixed mixture probability, then
\[
 \dot{\log q}_{\beta,\eta}(x)
 =\sum_j r_j(x)\dot\ell_j(x),\qquad
 r_j(x)=\frac{\pi_j e^{\ell_j(x)}}{\sum_i\pi_i e^{\ell_i(x)}}.
\]
For a Gaussian component with residual \(v=x-m\), precision \(P=C^{-1}\)
and force \(b=Pv\), the required moving-point derivative is
\[
 \dot\ell=-b^\top(\dot x-\dot m)
       +\tfrac12 b^\top\dot C b-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(P\dot C).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiating \(C=LL^\top\) and multiplying by \(L^{-1}\) and
\(L^{-\top}\) gives \(A=H+H^\top\), where
\(A=L^{-1}\dot C L^{-\top}\) and \(H=L^{-1}\dot L\) is lower triangular.
Hence \(H\) contains the strict lower triangle of \(A\) and half its
diagonal, and \(\dot L=LH\). Differentiating
\eqref{eq:sir-neural-components} now gives the displayed component derivatives.
The selection probabilities are fixed, so a fixed uniform selects the same
component during differentiation. Differentiating the log of the sum of
weighted densities gives the responsibilities formula. Finally,
\(\dot P=-P\dot C P\) and
\(\dot{\log\det C}=\operatorname{tr}(P\dot C)\) give the Gaussian expression.
Both the point and density parameters move; neither term may be dropped.
\end{proof}

These formulas supply the proposal terms to the existing analytical LEDH
recursion, including the direct source-moment and transport contributions to
Contract E. Autodiff of neural training parameters does not replace that
model-score calculation. Agreement with finite differences of this finite
program checks its derivative, not accuracy against the true model score.

\begin{proposition}[Training the corrected estimator with simulated labels]
\label{prop:sir-neural-moment-training}
Let \(C=(\theta_0,Y)\), let \(\Phi(X_T)\) contain the fixed scaled first
and raw second features, and let \(\widehat\mu_\eta(C,U)\) be the features
averaged over the actual final corrected LEDH cloud. Suppose particle
randomness \(U\) is conditionally independent of the simulated latent path
given \(C\), all second moments are finite, and the scaling matrix \(W\)
is fixed independently of the training examples. Writing
\(\mu(C)=\mathbb E[\Phi(X_T)\mid C]\),
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widehat\mu_\eta-\Phi(X_T)\|_W^2
 =\mathbb E\|\widehat\mu_\eta-\mu(C)\|_W^2
  +\mathbb E\|\Phi(X_T)-\mu(C)\|_W^2.
\]
If the finite estimator is almost-everywhere differentiable in \(\eta\)
and a locally integrable bound permits interchange of differentiation and
expectation, its reparameterized loss gradient is a gradient of the first term.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand the square after inserting \(\mu(C)\). Conditional independence gives
\(\mathbb E[\Phi(X_T)-\mu(C)\mid C,U]=0\), so the cross term vanishes.
The second term contains no \(\eta\). Fixed mixture probabilities make all
selection uniforms independent of \(\eta\); Gaussian draws have the same
property. Under the stated regularity and domination assumptions, differentiate
the finite estimator along those fixed draws and interchange derivative and
expectation. This proves the final assertion. It does not establish those
regularity assumptions across an inadmissible covariance or a failing reset.
\end{proof}

The trained loss must therefore be computed after the shared flow, complete
importance correction, OT and Contract-E reset. Loss on the neural proposal
alone would optimize a different estimator. Numerical validity, nonzero
gradients to every trainable tensor, fresh held-out moments and a separate
model-score reference are distinct checks. The reporting and provenance
repairs of 20 October passed their regressions without changing healthy
values or scores; they provide no evidence that this new learner improves
either quantity. The neural adaptation requires its own execution results.

The first SIR experiment fixes \(\theta=\theta_0\), uses five observation
times and 64 particles, and learns from independent simulated paths. The
network receives the standardized observations at all five times. Two hidden
\(\tanh\) layers produce the component displacements and log bandwidths at
each time; all six weight and bias tensors are trainable. We compare two
capacities, \((K,\text{width})=(2,16)\) and \((4,32)\), the two fixed mixture
probabilities \(\beta=0.5,0.9\), two Adam learning rates, and two independent
initializations. Each update averages losses from four newly simulated paths
and independent particle draws. The original numerical filtering controls,
including OT parameters \((1,5)\), remain fixed for this diagnostic comparison.

Thirty-two separate validation paths select checkpoints after the initial
32 updates and the 128-update continuation; a 512-update continuation is
conditional on the measured computational budget. Validation selects the
moment loss above, never an unavailable model score. After selection, 64
untouched paths with four paired particle draws compare the trained network
with the same untrained network, plain LEDH, the conditional-only proposal
and the physical mixture without neural adaptation. Paired uncertainty is
computed by resampling whole datasets, preserving their four particle draws.
First- and raw-second-moment label losses are reported separately. Simple predictive,
observation-only and recursive Gaussian estimates also test whether a learned
proposal offers useful moment estimates in each observation regime and in
the relative lower tail of compartment counts. A lower validation loss alone
does not settle that comparison. These fixed-parameter, short-horizon results
will concern the moment estimator; model-score improvement requires its own
independent reference comparison.

The distinction between label loss and posterior-moment error matters when
interpreting a percentage reduction. For two estimators \(a,b\), the
proposition gives
\[
 \mathbb E[L_a-L_b]
 =\mathbb E\|\widehat\mu_a-\mu(C)\|_W^2
  -\mathbb E\|\widehat\mu_b-\mu(C)\|_W^2.
\]
Thus the paired loss difference estimates the difference in moment-estimation
MSE without observing \(\mu(C)\). A percentage reduction in the total label
loss has a different denominator, because that loss also contains the
irreducible conditional variance. It must not be reported as the same
percentage reduction in posterior-moment MSE. A finite paired sample also
retains uncertainty from its latent labels, which is why the independent
comparison resamples datasets rather than treating particle replications as
independent observations.

The observation regimes and latent-compartment regimes also require different
interpretations. Let \(R\) denote a reported subgroup, let
\(A_a,A_b\) be two moment estimators, and write
\(M_j=\|A_j-\mu(C)\|_W^2\) and \(L_j=\|A_j-V\|_W^2\).
\begin{proposition}[Risk differences within a selected subgroup]
\label{prop:sg-subgroup-risk}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sg-separation}, for a subgroup of positive probability,
\[
 \mathbb E[L_a-L_b\mid R]
 =\mathbb E[M_a-M_b\mid R]
 -2\mathbb E[(A_a-A_b)^\top W(V-\mu(C))\mid R].
\]
If membership in \(R\) is determined by \(C\), the last term vanishes.
If membership depends on the latent label beyond \(C\), it can be nonzero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand both label losses around \(\mu(C)\) and subtract. The common
\(\|V-\mu(C)\|_W^2\) term cancels, giving the displayed expression before
taking conditional expectations. For \(R\) measurable with respect to \(C\),
condition first on \((C,U)\); the residual \(V-\mu(C)\) has zero conditional
mean by Assumption~\ref{ass:sg-separation}. This argument need not hold after
conditioning on an event that also reveals the latent label.
\end{proof}
For example, take \(W=1\), let \(C\) contain no information, let \(V\) take values
\(-1,1\) with equal probability, and compare \(A_a=1\) with \(A_b=0\).
The true conditional mean is zero and \(M_a-M_b=1\). Within the subgroup
\(R=\{V=1\}\), however, \(L_a-L_b=-1\). Thus the pooled and
observation-defined comparisons retain the original moment-MSE interpretation.
The latent-compartment comparisons measure prediction loss in selected latent
situations and supply the predeclared conservative heuristic veto. They do not
establish a posterior-moment MSE ranking against \(\mu(C)\) in those subgroups.

parameters and Adam state only after completing a training stage and its
validation. Although its log contained 362 accepted updates, its last saved
state contained only 128. The repair therefore resumed that exact state and
saved all neural parameters, Adam moments and iteration count, and the next
simulation and particle seeds every eight updates. These intermediate saves
permit recovery; only separately completed validation supplies a candidate's
validation loss. The numerical training kernel, proposal, loss and random
streams remained unchanged.

The continuation reached 512 updates. All 384 updates after the saved
128-update state were accepted, with finite losses and six finite nonzero
gradient norms. For all 234 updates overlapping the interrupted run, the
recorded loss, block losses, gradient norms and optimizer flags agreed exactly.
The lost terminal tensors of the original run were unavailable for comparison.
Separate interrupted-versus-uninterrupted tests checked restored parameters,
Adam state and the next update, while the new validated saves agreed with their
corresponding recovery states. These checks establish correct continuation of
the implemented optimization procedure; they do not establish that its learned
proposal improves estimation.

Table~\ref{tab:sg-neural-continuation} shows a lower observed validation loss at
256 updates, followed by increases at 384 and 512. Longer training thus did not
give a monotone improvement on this validation set. Repeated use of this small
set and the single optimization trajectory prevent a generalization ranking;
the pattern alone does not identify overfitting, an unsuitable learning rate,
or convergence. Both screened initializations completed 128 updates, but only
this initialization reached 512. The other optional continuation required more
time than the remaining campaign allowance under the recorded timing rule.

No later checkpoint was evaluated on the already exposed independent data.
Consequently, the failed improvement criterion and recursive-Gaussian comparison
in Section~\ref{sec:sg-neural-result} still describe the original frozen
candidate. Establishing a benefit from a later fit requires a frozen selection
based on validation, sufficient replication, and a fresh independent comparison
with the same baselines and conditional heuristic checks. Successful recovery
does not supply that missing evidence or a model-score accuracy result.
