# q20 staged campaign budget repair

The owner requests a realistic executable master after the 2,198-hour quote.
That number prices simultaneous maximum work counts and duplicates a complete
tuning scope as repair. It ignores the existing eight-hour tuning deadline and
two-hour repair allocation. It is wrong as a required-time estimate.

## Intent and evidence contract

Repair allocation and interruption handling for the existing q20/T30 float64
UKF-approximate posterior. Plain NeuTra HMC remains first; only a completed
candidate failure may lead to the tempered NeuTra ensemble. Identity latent
mass, the full declared tuning cohort, fresh verification, sequential posterior
checks and independent reference criteria remain unchanged. There is no method
ranking. The engineering baseline is the preserved training-admission r3 tree.

Passing means affordable stages can start without funding every maximum,
cumulative stage and campaign spending cannot reset on replay/retry, partial
work is preserved, and a resource pause cannot become scientific rejection or
admission. Corrupt checkpoints, changed numerical kernels, failed focused
regressions or exhausted verification allowance stop this repair. Measured
costs are explanatory forecasts, not posterior evidence or guaranteed runtimes.
Artifacts are the changed source, CPU-reference regressions, a saved-price
spending report, source/import audit and settled allowance under
`docs/plans/artifacts/ssl-lstm-q20-staged-budget-repair-2026-09-20/`.

## Allocation policy and numerical provenance

- Use actual remaining campaign time after every stage. Hard deadlines are
  spending limits; never multiply them by the forecast safety factor.
- Initially cap training at its measured, factor-two floor quote. After every
  full cohort rung at or above the floor, hand off when enough independently
  rooted maps meet the existing trial criteria at every needed temperature.
  Otherwise advance only within the funded allocation; preserve unfinished
  training. In an ensemble, process each required temperature explicitly.
- Each tuning scope receives at most the inherited 28,800-second deadline,
  clipped to remaining funds after protected downstream work. Retain the public
  frozen-transport tuner and its complete-cohort selection rule. Smaller staged
  deadlines change resource availability, not numerical admission.
- Hold the inherited 7,200-second repair allowance once across the campaign.
  Actual failed infrastructure attempts debit that hold as well as actual
  campaign time. Neither method fallback nor retry renews it. Existing attempt
  limits and cumulative stage limits remain binding.
- Before tuning, protect the earliest posterior assessment, the first two reference
  rungs (two are required for the existing stability test), and assessment.
  The posterior placeholder uses the shortest measured L and is explicitly
  conditional. This is 2,000 warm-up plus 1,000 retained transitions for the
  saved protocol; it is a first opportunity to assess, not a convergence promise.
  After tuning, use the selected L's measurement or the next
  measured larger L as a conservative cost hypothesis, never as kernel evidence.
- Sampling then receives available time after reference, assessment and repair
  holds, bounded by the measured sampling cap. Reference may grow through its
  existing ladder within the actual balance. Checksums and deterministic chunk
  inputs permit completed chunks to replay without redoing numerical work.
- Before a new sample/reference chunk, check its measured forecast against the
  worker's remaining allocation; update the forecast with observed chunk time.
  Preserve incomplete receipts and checkpoints on a budget pause. An external
  timeout remains the backup for underestimated or stuck calls.

All numerical controls above are inherited or derived from saved measurements:
512 training updates per scope, factor two, 1,000-transition posterior chunks,
reference rungs 1,024 and 4,096 rows per bank, eight banks, existing worker
termination grace, and measured initialization/analysis. No new accuracy
threshold, compute allowance, hardware assumption or unmeasured parallel speedup
is introduced. Factor two and nearest-larger-L cost coverage are engineering
hypotheses; per-chunk timing and external deadlines expose underestimation.

## Skeptical audit before implementation

The previous all-cap baseline is not a realistic lower bound. Reserving only a
first reference rung would be another error because qualification needs a
second rung. Selecting a verified member from an incomplete tuning cohort would
silently change the declared procedure, so that remains forbidden. A timeout or
`partial_budget` result must pause the campaign, not select the ensemble.
Training must complete the floor for all declared seeds before early handoff,
and the ensemble must cover all its temperatures. Saved updates retain their
existing current-scope credit rules. Actual stage time must be charged even
when a process fails or a checkpoint replays. The saved GPU prices belong to
unchanged numerical kernels; concurrency changes in the main workspace must
not enter the isolated execution source. With these corrections the plan
passes the audit. No convergence or 21-hour completion promise follows.

The terminal implementation audit refined the initial one-chunk hold: it could
let an eight-hour tuner consume time needed for the first posterior assessment.
Protect the earliest assessment instead. This is an affordable finite first
stage, not a return to reserving both 10,000-transition maxima. If training uses
its full reserve, the resulting tuning allocation will be smaller; that may
leave an inconclusive campaign and must be reported honestly.

every maximum. `forecast_campaign` emits v2 with separate work forecasts and
allocation limits; the misleading `minimum_complete_seconds` and all-caps
reservation fields are removed. Training hands off only after a full cohort rung
at or above the floor and enough eligible independently rooted maps across all
needed temperatures. Unresolved maps do not force continued training after that
handoff. If training needs more work than funded, it remains incomplete.

Tuning receives at most eight hours per scope and less when necessary to protect
the first posterior assessment. Its initial controller deadline is clipped to
the worker allocation after initialization and binding. Checkpoint resume keeps
that original controller deadline; the supervisor additionally enforces the
remaining cumulative stage allowance. A partial tuning result, including one

Shared target invalidity also cannot trigger a sampler fallback.

Sampling and reference use the existing numerical checkpoint inputs and seeds.

the master and leaves its completed predecessors intact. Process timeout remains
the fallback when compilation or execution exceeds its forecast. A resource
pause provides no scientific rejection and grants no new allowance or attempt.
