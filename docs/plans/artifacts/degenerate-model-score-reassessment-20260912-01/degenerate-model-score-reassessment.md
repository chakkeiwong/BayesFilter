# Reassessing the score for a degenerate state-space model

Date: 2026-09-12
Status: conceptual decision record; no production implementation or stochastic campaign

## Decision

The score problem should be reformulated in disturbance coordinates. The
ordinary all-ancestor smoother, which requires evaluating a density
`p(x_t | x_{t-1}, theta)` between arbitrary particles, is the wrong object for
a deterministic or rank-deficient transition. Adding a small process-noise
ridge, using a pseudoinverse, or reusing the regular-transition backward
weights would define a different model or an unproved measure change.

The original observed-data score is still meaningful. It is the derivative of
the likelihood obtained by integrating the model over its actual initial and
innovation variables. The implementation must therefore keep those variables
explicit, propagate the state sensitivity through the deterministic model
map, include the initial-law derivative, and account for the derivative of
any parameter-dependent proposal or resampling law. A finite-particle score
from that construction is a valid estimator candidate, but it is not exact
merely because the state map is differentiable.

There are three different quantities that must remain separately named:

1. The **model score**, `s(theta) = grad_theta log p_theta(y_{1:T})`.
2. The **finite-filter derivative**, such as `grad_theta log Z_hat_N` or the
   derivative of the executed particle program.
3. A **proposal force**, a field that may be useful for transport or tuning
   even when it is not the gradient of the posterior target.

Only the first is the score of the original DSGE likelihood. The current
LEDH analytical score remains the derivative of its finite value program; it
cannot be relabeled as the exact DSGE score without the disturbance-coordinate
identity and the required initial-law and resampling terms.

## The target in the correct coordinates

Write the model using an initial variable `v_0`, innovations `u_1:T`, and a
deterministic state map:

```text
x_0 = F_{0,theta}(v_0)
x_t = F_theta(x_{t-1}, u_t),       t = 1,...,T
y_t ~ g_theta(y_t | x_t)
```

Let `rho_theta(v_0)` and `r_theta(u_t | x_{t-1})` be the densities of the
variables actually sampled by the model. For independent shocks, the second
density is just `r_theta(u_t)`. The joint integrand in disturbance coordinates
is

```text
pi_theta(v_0, u_{1:T}; y)
  = rho_theta(v_0)
    prod_t r_theta(u_t | x_{t-1}) g_theta(y_t | x_t),
```

where every `x_t` is obtained by the deterministic recursion. The observed
likelihood is

```text
Z_theta(y) = integral pi_theta(v_0, u_{1:T}; y) d v_0 d u_{1:T}.
```

This expression does not require an ambient transition density. It remains
valid when the transition support is a lower-dimensional manifold and when
the map from shocks to states is many-to-one. Murray, Jones, and Parslow,
*On Disturbance State-Space Models and the Particle Marginal Metropolis-Hastings
Sampler* (2013), Section 2 and equations (3)--(9), make precisely this
disturbance-state-space replacement: the tractable noise density appears in
the weight in place of an unavailable transition density. They explicitly
prefer this general formulation to a change-of-variables argument, since the
state map need not be one-to-one. The local source is
`.localresources/papers/murray-jones-parslow-2013-disturbance-state-space.txt`,
especially lines 42--61, 93--127, and 189--249.

For a fixed disturbance path, define the total state sensitivity
`dx_t_dtheta = d x_t / d theta`. It obeys

```text
dx_0_dtheta = d F_{0,theta}(v_0) / d theta
dx_t_dtheta = partial_theta F_theta(x_{t-1}, u_t)
          + J_x F_theta(x_{t-1}, u_t) dx_{t-1}_dtheta.
```

The complete-path derivative is then

```text
H_theta = d log rho_theta(v_0) / d theta
          + sum_t d log r_theta(u_t | x_{t-1}) / d theta
          + sum_t [partial_theta log g_theta(y_t | x_t)
                   + (grad_x log g_theta(y_t | x_t))^T dx_t_dtheta].
```

The derivative of `r_theta` must include its dependence through
`x_{t-1}` when the shock law is state dependent. If the shock law is
parameter independent, its direct term is zero, but the state-map and
observation terms remain. If the initial state is generated by a parameterized
innovation, `dx_0_dtheta` carries the initial-law contribution. If the initial law
is represented by a density instead, its log-density derivative must be
included directly. A stationary initial distribution is not optional: the
exact reference check in `verify_score_identities.py` shows that freezing an
AR(1) stationary variance at `a = 1/2` retains only `3/4` of the correct score.

Under the usual differentiation-under-the-integral conditions,

```text
grad_theta log Z_theta(y)
  = E_theta[ H_theta(v_0, u_{1:T}) | y_{1:T} ].
```

This is the support-valid score identity. It is a conditional expectation
under the disturbance posterior, not a backward expectation formed with a
Lebesgue-density transition between arbitrary state particles.

DSGE-specific regularity must be checked before using this identity globally.
An implicit equilibrium map can use an implicit derivative when the relevant
Jacobian is nonsingular. Rank changes, occasionally binding constraints,
regime switches, or discontinuous solution branches require a separate
piecewise or weak-derivative treatment. A smooth automatic derivative through
one selected branch is not evidence that the global likelihood is
differentiable.

## What a particle implementation may estimate

A disturbance particle filter samples an ancestor and then samples an
innovation. For a proposal `q_t(u_t | x_{t-1}, y_t)`, the incremental factor
has the form

```text
w_t = r_theta(u_t | x_{t-1})
      g_theta(y_t | F_theta(x_{t-1}, u_t))
      / q_t(u_t | x_{t-1}, y_t),
```

with the usual previous or auxiliary-stage factor. This is the route in
Murray et al., Section 2. It propagates actual shocks and deterministic states;
it never asks whether an arbitrary child state lies on an ancestor's
lower-dimensional transition support.

The filter can carry `x_t`, `dx_t_dtheta`, and an accumulated `H_theta` along each
sampled ancestry. That gives a direct disturbance-path estimator. It is a
useful reference construction because it exposes the terms that the current
ambient-state implementation cannot represent. It still has the usual
genealogical variance problem, and a self-normalized finite-particle estimate
is not automatically unbiased.

There is a second derivative issue at resampling. If ancestor probabilities
depend on `theta`, differentiating only the continuous state map while
silently treating the sampled ancestor as fixed omits the derivative of the
resampling law. Scibior and Wood, *Differentiable Particle Filtering without
Modifying the Forward Pass* (2021), Section 3.1 and equation (13), derive a
stop-gradient weight-ratio correction for this case; Section 4.1 extends the
same idea to reparameterized proposals. The local paper copy is
`.localresources/papers/scibior-wood-dpf-forward-pass.txt` and the downloaded
source page is `scibior-arxiv.html` in this artifact directory. The correction
changes the derivative calculation, not the forward particle values.

That result does not make a small finite-particle log score exact. If
`Z_hat_N` and `D_hat_N` estimate `Z` and `d Z / d theta`, respectively, then

```text
score_hat_N = D_hat_N / Z_hat_N
```

is a ratio estimator and generally has finite-sample bias even when both
numerator and denominator estimators are unbiased. The exact check in
`verification.json` enumerates every `N=2, T=2` discrete particle-filter
outcome and finds:

* the expected unnormalized value is `Z = 33/128`;
* the expected corrected derivative is `dZ = 1/32`;
* the exact score is `4/33`; and
* the mean of the single-run normalized score is not `4/33`.

Therefore the implementation should expose `Z_hat`, `D_hat`, and their ratio
as separate outputs. It should not describe the ratio as an unbiased model
score. Obtaining an exactly unbiased score rather than unbiased value and
derivative is a separate debiasing problem; it would need a specified
randomized construction, tail/work conditions, and its own validation.

## Where variance reduction belongs

Variance reduction is worth pursuing only after the disturbance-coordinate
identity has passed an exact score check. The useful order is:

1. **Conditional integration.** If a DSGE block is linear-Gaussian conditional
   on a smaller nonlinear disturbance path, integrate that block with a
   Kalman-style calculation and differentiate the resulting conditional
   likelihood. This is the strongest first option because it removes both
   particle variance and unnecessary state dimensions.
2. **Observation-informed disturbance proposals.** Use an auxiliary or
   locally adapted proposal for `u_t`, with the correction `r/q` retained in
   the weight. The proposal may use a UKF or local linearization, but it must
   remain a proposal in shock coordinates; it is not permission to evaluate a
   singular state transition density.
3. **Conditional-mean control variates.** For a path contribution `H`, replace
   it by `H - c` only when the conditional expectation of `c` is known under
   the same conditioning sigma-field, or when an exact compensating term is
   retained. A local Kalman score, an analytically integrated tangent block,
   or a martingale increment can be a candidate. A learned or arbitrary
   baseline may reduce observed variance, but without a proved zero-mean
   property it changes the estimand.
4. **Resampling and replication.** Stratified/systematic resampling, better
   ancestor proposals, and independent filter replicates can reduce variance.
   They do not repair a missing initial-law term, an omitted resampling score,
   or the ratio bias above.
5. **Shock-history smoothing.** A backward or PaRIS-like method is admissible
   only after deriving a conditional law for shock histories under the
   disturbance measure. Reusing an ambient-state backward kernel is invalid
   for the degenerate model.

The DiCE paper (Foerster et al., 2018, Theorem 1 and the baseline discussion)
supports the general score-function/pathwise distinction and the fact that a
valid baseline can reduce variance without changing an expectation. It does
not, by itself, establish that a proposed baseline is conditionally
mean-zero for this DSGE likelihood. The local source is
`.localresources/papers/foerster-2018-dice.txt`.

## Consequences for HMC and the project architecture

Standard HMC needs the gradient of the declared target potential. A finite-N
particle ratio, even one built from a valid disturbance filter, is a noisy
force and is not a drop-in exact HMC gradient. An unbiased likelihood estimate
supports pseudo-marginal or particle-MCMC constructions with their own
correctness argument; it does not turn noisy-gradient HMC into exact HMC.

The project therefore has three honest architecture choices:

* For parameter inference, use a disturbance-coordinate particle likelihood
  inside particle marginal Metropolis-Hastings or particle Gibbs, or use a
  model-specific deterministic likelihood solver when the DSGE structure
  permits one.
* For small or reduced models, build an exact or high-accuracy disturbance
  reference and use it to validate any particle score before considering
  gradient-based inference.
* If the finite-filter force is useful for proposal adaptation or transport,
  bind it as a position-only proposal field and label it accordingly. It must
  not be passed to an HMC consumer that expects the exact target score. The
  HMC tuning interface in `docs/reference/hmc-tuning-interface.md` requires a
  typed target/force binding for such a distinction.

This means the KDM/Fisher smoothing route should be closed for the degenerate
DSGE score question. Further bandwidth or control-variate tuning of that
route cannot fix the measure mismatch. A disturbance-coordinate reference
implementation is the next useful engineering object; it is a correctness
oracle and candidate estimator, not a production default yet.

## Bounded next sequence

The next work should be a sequence of small discriminating checks rather than
another performance campaign:

1. **Specify the target.** Write the DSGE transition, shock law, initial law,
   observation law, constraints, and regime assumptions in disturbance form.
   State whether the initial state is fixed, stationary, or generated from an
   initial innovation.
2. **Construct an exact oracle.** Use a linear-Gaussian rank-deficient model
   with the same support pattern and compare the disturbance score, including
   the initial-law derivative, with a Kalman or analytic score. Retain the
   moving-support and initial-law checks already passed in
   `verification.json`.
3. **Implement a diagnostic disturbance filter.** Keep the forward value,
   unnormalized derivative, normalized ratio, path sensitivity, and resampling
   correction as separately inspectable quantities. CPU/reference execution is
   sufficient for this small check; no GPU campaign is implied.
4. **Test resampling dependence.** Compare the corrected derivative with a
   finite-difference derivative of the same finite-N value program and with an
   exact enumeration on a tiny discrete fixture. A pathwise-only derivative
   is a failure for a parameter-dependent resampling law.
5. **Add one variance reduction at a time.** First conditional integration,
   then an observation-informed shock proposal, then a proved conditional
   control variate. Report bias and variance separately as `N` and `T` change.
6. **Choose the inference architecture.** Only after the preceding checks
   decide whether the required use is deterministic-solver HMC, particle
   MCMC, or a typed proposal field. Do not promote a particle ratio to the
   canonical HMC score.

## Source coverage and limits

The decision rests on inspected local primary texts: Murray et al. (2013) for
disturbance-state-space particle filtering; Scibior and Wood (2021) for the
resampling derivative correction; and Foerster et al. (2018) for pathwise,
score-function, and baseline distinctions. Poyiadjis, Doucet, and Singh
(2011) remains useful for the regular-transition Fisher/backward recursion but
does not remove the support issue identified here. The web search service was
unavailable during this pass, so no claim of exhaustive literature coverage is
made. The local papers and exact reference script are the evidence used for
this decision.
