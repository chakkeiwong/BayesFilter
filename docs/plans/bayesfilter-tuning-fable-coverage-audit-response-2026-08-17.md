## F1 Correction and Rebuttal

The audit is correct that collection-only evidence cannot support migration.
It is not correct to compress the 13 failures into three uniform BayesFilter
contract-drift families.

### 1. CCMA acceptance evidence: eight failures

The historical CCMA artifacts use
`bayesfilter.hmc_acceptance_evidence.v3`. The live parser at
`bayesfilter/inference/hmc_verification.py:910` requires v5. Historical v3
migration views already exist and are intentionally non-promoting.

The repair must be either regenerated v5 evidence or an explicit historical,
non-promoting reader. Weakening the live v5 parser or silently upgrading v3
would violate the plan's artifact rules. These failures block the active
migration gate, but they do not prove that the live v5 parser is defective.

### 2. MacroFinance staged-timeout identity: two failures

MacroFinance constructs the default `HMCStagedTimeoutPolicy()`. The live and
committed BayesFilter default at
`bayesfilter/inference/hmc_kernel_tuning.py:770` is
`bayesfilter_hmc_emergency_stage_caps_v2`, while the consumer tests expect
`ccma_phase4y_stage_budget_v1`. The tuning config correctly serializes the
identity of the policy object it was given.

The likely repair is to update the stale consumer expectation. If the older
CCMA identity is genuinely required, MacroFinance must instantiate that policy
explicitly. The plan must not rewrite a bound policy's identity during payload
projection.

### 3. MacroFinance redaction assertion: one failure

The L10d test asserts that the string `0.25` appears nowhere in the full public
JSON. Legitimate timeout/budget-policy constants also equal `0.25`, so the test
can fail even when covariance arrays are absent. This is a brittle substring
false positive, not staged-timeout drift.

The plan now requires structural assertions that private covariance/mass arrays
and fields are absent. It records this separately so fixing it cannot be
misrepresented as restoring a BayesFilter timeout contract.

### 4. dsge_hmc fixed-transport policy: two failures

The committed BayesFilter policy at
`bayesfilter/inference/fixed_transport_hmc_grid_policy.py:243` uses
`eligible_trajectory_acceptance_in_band_then_rhat_convergence_then_ess`, and
BayesFilter's own policy tests require 63 candidates. The dsge_hmc relaunch
contract expects the older rule and 49 candidates.

Current source evidence therefore points to a stale consumer contract, subject
to final adjudication during the dsge_hmc migration. The plan correctly blocks
migration until that decision and rerun are recorded; it does not weaken the
repository policy merely to satisfy an old count.

### Owner-sign-off wording

I do not accept a blanket owner-sign-off requirement for each drift repair.
Source-anchored producer/consumer authority can resolve a demonstrably stale
test without escalating routine compatibility maintenance into a product
decision. The revised plan requires owner direction only when inspected
authorities leave a material intended-contract choice unresolved.

## F3 Correction and Rebuttal

I agree that the mass holdout needs a valid but deliberately mismatched SPD arm.
Using analytic precision in the position-covariance role is a useful example:
it is structurally valid, geometrically wrong for that declared role, and
distinguishable through deterministic analytic mass-distance/whitening checks.

I do not accept the statement that repair diagnostics must necessarily fire.
For a fixed correct target, any valid SPD mass can leave HMC target-correct, and
fresh epsilon/`L` retuning can compensate for poor geometry. Requiring a repair
solely because the mass differs from the analytic covariance would incorrectly
turn a geometry comparator into a target-validity veto.

The revised requirement is stricter and more precise:

- deterministic mismatch diagnostics must distinguish the arm;
- every arm gets fresh mass-specific epsilon and `L` tuning;
- untouched posterior validity is tested independently;
- repair fires only under a predeclared health, validity, or efficiency
  condition; and
- efficiency differences remain descriptive unless uncertainty supports a
  ranking.

## Changed Plan

The revised plan now contains these concrete changes:

1. Phase 0 has a focused run-level baseline and four-entry drift-adjudication
   ledger, not collection-only evidence.
2. Phase 1 permits structural extraction without new regressions but does not
   call unexplained baseline failures compatible.
3. Phases 4-6 require adjudicated repairs; 90/11 and 24/2 are not passing
   migration outcomes.
4. The mass procedure is explicitly staged and conditional:
   mass -> fresh epsilon -> `L` -> untouched verification. It distinguishes the
   monolith's one-epsilon-then-`L` policy from robust per-`L` dual averaging.
5. `mass_matrix.py` is declared NumPy migration debt on the admitted path.
6. Both dedicated mass test modules are included in an unfiltered command.
7. The Gaussian holdout includes identity, exact, adapted, and mismatched-SPD
   arms with fresh retuning and uncertainty-disciplined interpretation.
8. Non-Gaussian curved/varying-Hessian evidence is required before robust
   numeric-default promotion, while remaining nonblocking for Phase 0-2
   structural extraction.
