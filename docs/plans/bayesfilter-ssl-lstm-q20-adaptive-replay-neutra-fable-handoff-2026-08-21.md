## Question being reviewed

Can a fixed-capacity experience-replay-like scheme, which continually replaces
old particle populations with fresh correctly weighted populations and combines
their forward-KL information with fresh reverse-KL NeuTra queries, target the
right transport and converge under explicit assumptions?

The intended answer is conditional mathematical viability, not a guarantee
that the existing SSL-LSTM q=20 dense IAF, its optimizer, six 100-particle SMC
populations, or finite HMC kernel succeeds.

## Required review

Please independently rederive and challenge every item below. Do not accept a
claim merely because it is internally consistent or because MathDevMCP emitted
a favorable status.

1. Target and transport identities: check the directions of `T_phi`,
   `q_phi`, and the pulled-back target, every Jacobian determinant, and the
   equality `KL(q_phi||pi) = KL(rho||pi_phi^z)`.
2. Gradient identities: check which quantities are held fixed in the
   forward-KL score and which total derivatives appear in the fresh
   reverse-KL estimator. Identify any missing regularity or boundary term.
3. Deterministic-mixture estimator: check conditional unbiasedness with
   adaptive-but-frozen proposal history, mixture allocation factors, target
   support, unknown normalizer `Z`, vector-valued scores, and finite variance.
4. SMC boundary: determine whether the SMC-U versus SMC-N split is correct.
   In particular, challenge the claim that endlessly averaging independent
   fixed-`N` normalized populations converges to the finite-`N` expected ratio
   estimator rather than automatically removing its bias.
5. Replacement unit: check whether whole-population replacement is required
   for the stated normalized SMC semantics and whether any row-level replay
   route could be valid only with separately bound proposal/source weights.
6. Buffer Markov chain: verify the invariant product law, uniqueness, coupling
   bound, and stationary forward-gradient mean under content-independent
   refresh.
7. Theorem 2, the highest-risk obligation: check the time indexing of the
   buffer transition; the Poisson equation and martingale/telescoping
   decomposition; dependence on `phi_t`; Lipschitz and moment requirements;
   the explicit inactive-projection assumption; the transformed iterate
   `y_t`; the summable remainder in (33d); inequality (33f); and the
   almost-supermartingale conclusion. Decide whether the eight stated
   assumptions are sufficient. If they are not, give the smallest exact
   repair or a counterexample. Also say plainly how strong and empirically
   unverified assumption (30) is for a finite dense IAF.
8. Adaptive extension: independently verify Theorem 2A, especially conditional
   unbiasedness after history-dependent proposal selection, inequality (33i),
   and whether (33h) really makes arbitrary bounded stale replay a summable
   perturbation. Check the distinction between that theorem and persistent
   nonsummable positive-weight replay of stale blocks generated by a changing
   learned proposal. Decide whether the controlled-Markov, increasing-buffer,
   and cross-fitting routes are correctly classified as additional proofs
   rather than consequences of Theorem 2 or 2A.
9. Exact minimizer: verify KL nonnegativity/equality, the role of positive
   `a`, `b`, and `Z`, and whether exact membership `q_phi=pi` really makes the
   hybrid global minimum zero. Separate existence from optimizer convergence.
10. Expressivity: review the Rosenblatt-map existence argument and its
    assumptions. Confirm or reject the statement that it establishes existence
    in an unrestricted triangular diffeomorphism class but says nothing about
    membership in the implemented finite dense IAF.
11. Gaussianization and HMC: check exact pullback Gaussianity and the
    `tau=pi/2` ideal quadratic-Hamiltonian calculation. Confirm that this is not
    evidence about a finite leapfrog kernel, tuned integration time, numerical
    errors, or the current sign-mode crossing rate.
12. Counterexamples: try to construct failures for content-dependent eviction,
    missing proposal support, fixed finite replay, pure reverse KL, and
    normalized row deletion. Report any counterexample that also defeats a
    theorem under all of its stated assumptions.
