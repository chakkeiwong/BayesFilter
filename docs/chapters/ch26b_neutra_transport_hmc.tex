\chapter{Neural Transport Hamiltonian Monte Carlo}
\label{ch:bf-neutra}

This chapter gives a self-contained account of neural-transport Hamiltonian
Monte Carlo (NeuTra HMC) and documents the BayesFilter implementation and
experiments available in July 2026.  The construction was introduced by
\citet{hoffman2019neutra}; the derivation and implementation below do not
require the reader to consult that paper.  The HMC and transport mathematics,
diagnostics, and five common truth-tail targets are derived explicitly.  Eight
additional historical configurations are reported under their preserved
protocols.  Four of those are inherited DSGE targets whose complete economic
model systems belong to their dedicated chapters and repositories; they are
identified here at the resolution needed to interpret the NeuTra evidence, but
are not presented as self-contained re-derivations of those economic models.

\section{Motivation: geometry is often the HMC bottleneck}

Let $\vartheta$ denote scientifically meaningful parameters and let $y$ denote
the observations.  Bayesian computation targets
\begin{equation}
 p(\vartheta\mid y)=\frac{p(y\mid\vartheta)p(\vartheta)}{p(y)}.
\end{equation}
The evidence $p(y)$ is normally unavailable, but HMC needs only an unnormalized
log posterior and its gradient.  Constraints are first removed by a model
chart $\vartheta=C(\theta)$, $\theta\in\R^d$.  Thus the source-coordinate target
used by computation is
\begin{align}
 \widetilde\pi_\Theta(\theta)
 &=p(y\mid C(\theta))p(C(\theta))
   \left|\det J_C(\theta)\right|, \\
 \ell_\Theta(\theta)&=\log\widetilde\pi_\Theta(\theta).
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-source-target}
\end{align}
The tilde emphasizes that normalization is unnecessary.

Euclidean HMC is efficient when typical posterior directions have comparable
scales and curvature.  It can be inefficient when a single global mass matrix
cannot accommodate strong correlations, changing curvature, funnels, or
nonlinear ridges.  A step size small enough for the stiffest region then makes
movement elsewhere slow.  An affine whitening map repairs constant covariance
but cannot generally straighten changing geometry.  NeuTra learns an explicit
nonlinear coordinate map and runs ordinary HMC in the learned coordinates.

Three facts motivate the method and delimit its claims.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item The learned variational distribution need not be exact.  Its purpose is
   to provide useful coordinates, not final posterior draws.
 \item The Jacobian-corrected HMC target is exact for the declared
   source-coordinate posterior whenever the frozen map is a bijection and HMC
   is implemented correctly.
 \item A poor map can still reduce efficiency or hide a mode from finite runs.
   Exact invariance is not a finite-time convergence guarantee.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Hamiltonian Monte Carlo from first principles}

For a target $\widetilde\pi(q)$ on $q\in\R^d$, introduce momentum
$p\sim\calN(0,M)$ with positive-definite mass $M$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 U(q)=-\log\widetilde\pi(q),\qquad
 K(p)=\tfrac12p^\top M^{-1}p,\qquad H(q,p)=U(q)+K(p).
\end{equation}
Hamilton's equations are
\begin{equation}
 \dot q=M^{-1}p,\qquad \dot p=\nabla_q\log\widetilde\pi(q).
\end{equation}
Along an exact trajectory,
\begin{align}
 \frac{dH}{dt}
 &=\nabla_qH^\top\dot q+\nabla_pH^\top\dot p\\
 &=\nabla U(q)^\top M^{-1}p
   +(M^{-1}p)^\top[-\nabla U(q)]=0.
\end{align}
The phase-space velocity is
$v(q,p)=(\nabla_pH,-\nabla_qH)$, whose divergence is
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_q\!\cdot\nabla_pH-\nabla_p\!\cdot\nabla_qH=0
\end{equation}
because the mixed derivatives cancel.  Liouville's formula therefore gives a
unit Jacobian determinant for the exact flow, so phase-space volume is
preserved.  If $(q(t),p(t))$ solves the equations, then
$(q(-t),-p(-t))$ does also, proving reversibility after a momentum flip.

Numerically, one leapfrog step of size $\epsilon$ is
\begin{align}
 p_{n+1/2}&=p_n+\tfrac\epsilon2\nabla\log\widetilde\pi(q_n),\\
 q_{n+1}&=q_n+\epsilon M^{-1}p_{n+1/2},\\
 p_{n+1}&=p_{n+1/2}+\tfrac\epsilon2
   \nabla\log\widetilde\pi(q_{n+1}).
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-leapfrog}
\end{align}
Each substep is a shear: it changes one block while holding the other fixed,
and therefore has block-triangular Jacobian determinant one.  Their composition
is volume-preserving.  Replacing $\epsilon$ by $-\epsilon$ and reversing the
substep order recovers the starting state; because the kick--drift--kick order
is symmetric, a momentum flip supplies the required proposal reversibility.
After $L$ steps, the proposal $(q',-p')$ is accepted with probability
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=\min\{1,\exp[-H(q',p')+H(q,p)]\}.
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-accept}
\end{equation}
Indeed, for the joint density $\rho(q,p)\propto e^{-H(q,p)}$ and the
deterministic reversible proposal map $F$, the accepted forward probability
mass is
\begin{equation}
 \rho(x)\min\!\left\{1,\frac{\rho(Fx)}{\rho(x)}\right\}
 =\min\{\rho(x),\rho(Fx)\}
 =\rho(Fx)\min\!\left\{1,\frac{\rho(x)}{\rho(Fx)}\right\},
\end{equation}
where $x=(q,p)$.  This is detailed balance; the unit Jacobian means no proposal
volume factor is missing.  The Metropolis correction therefore removes
leapfrog discretization bias.  A high
acceptance rate alone is not evidence of good exploration: $\epsilon$ can be so
small that proposals are nearly unchanged.

\section{Exact neural change of variables}
\label{sec:bf-neutra-change-of-variables}

Let $z\in\R^d$ be latent HMC coordinates and let
\begin{equation}
 \theta=T_\phi(z)
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-map}
\end{equation}
be a differentiable bijection with frozen parameters $\phi$.  For a measurable
set $A$ in source space,
\begin{align}
 \Pr(\Theta\in A)
 &=\int_A\pi_\Theta(\theta)\,d\theta\\
 &=\int_{T_\phi^{-1}(A)}
   \pi_\Theta(T_\phi(z))|\det J_{T_\phi}(z)|\,dz.
\end{align}
Therefore the pulled-back target is
\begin{align}
 \widetilde\pi_Z(z)
 &=\widetilde\pi_\Theta(T_\phi(z))
   |\det J_{T_\phi}(z)|,\\
 \ell_Z(z)&=\ell_\Theta(T_\phi(z))+\log|\det J_{T_\phi}(z)|.
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-transported-target}
\end{align}
If a Markov kernel leaves $\pi_Z$ invariant, pushing its stationary draws
through $T_\phi$ produces draws from $\pi_\Theta$.  Hence approximation error
in training does not change the stationary posterior.  It changes only the
geometry, initialization, and finite-compute efficiency.

By the chain rule, the latent score is
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_z\ell_Z(z)=J_{T_\phi}(z)^\top
 \nabla_\theta\ell_\Theta(T_\phi(z))+
 \nabla_z\log|\det J_{T_\phi}(z)|.
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-score}
\end{equation}
Both terms are derivatives of the same scalar target.  Dropping the log
Jacobian changes the probability measure; dropping its derivative gives the
wrong force for that measure.  During HMC, $\phi$ is fixed, so no optimizer
state participates in \eqref{eq:bf-neutra-score}.

Artifacts distinguish latent $z$, unconstrained source $\theta$, and physical
$\vartheta=C(\theta)$.  HMC integrates $z$; the filter target consumes $\theta$;
scientific summaries use $\vartheta$.

\section{The BayesFilter dense IAF}
\label{sec:bf-neutra-iaf}

The tested map is a stack of three MADE-style dense inverse autoregressive flow
(IAF) stages, reverse-order permutations between stages, and a fixed full
affine lift.  This section derives that exact implementation.

For one stage, masked dense networks produce vectors $a(z)$ and $b(z)$ such
that coordinate $i$ depends only on $z_{1:i-1}$.  BayesFilter bounds the log
scale by
\begin{equation}
 s_i(z_{1:i-1})=s_{\max}\tanh\!\left(
   \frac{a_i(z_{1:i-1})}{s_{\max}}\right),\qquad s_{\max}=1,
\end{equation}
and applies
\begin{equation}
 u_i=z_i\exp\{s_i(z_{1:i-1})\}+b_i(z_{1:i-1}).
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-iaf-stage}
\end{equation}
Because $s_i$ and $b_i$ do not depend on $z_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial z_i}=e^{s_i},\qquad
 \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial z_j}=0\quad(j>i).
\end{equation}
The Jacobian is lower triangular, so
\begin{equation}
 \det J_f(z)=\prod_{i=1}^d e^{s_i(z_{1:i-1})},\qquad
 \log|\det J_f(z)|=\sum_{i=1}^d s_i(z_{1:i-1}).
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-iaf-logdet}
\end{equation}
Invertibility follows constructively.  The first coordinate satisfies
$u_1=e^{s_1}z_1+b_1$, where $s_1,b_1$ are constants, so
$z_1=e^{-s_1}(u_1-b_1)$.  After $z_{1:i-1}$ have been recovered, evaluate
$s_i(z_{1:i-1})$ and $b_i(z_{1:i-1})$ and set
\begin{equation}
 z_i=e^{-s_i(z_{1:i-1})}
 \{u_i-b_i(z_{1:i-1})\}.
\end{equation}
This recursion gives a unique inverse for every $u\in\R^d$.  The positive
diagonal derivative agrees with that inverse construction.  The scale bound
prevents a single stage from creating arbitrarily large or small local volume
changes.

Let $f_1,\ldots,f_K$ denote the IAF stages and $P$ the reverse permutation.
For $K=3$, define
\begin{equation}
 h_1=f_1(z),\quad h_2=f_2(Ph_1),\quad h_3=f_3(Ph_2),
 \quad T_\phi(z)=c+Lh_3,
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-stack}
\end{equation}
where $L$ is a fixed nonsingular full matrix.  Since $|\det P|=1$,
\begin{equation}
 \log|\det J_T(z)|=\log|\det L|+
 \sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{i=1}^d s_{k,i}.
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-stack-logdet}
\end{equation}
The affine $(c,L)$ supplies target-specific location and scale; only the neural
stages are trained.  Output-layer weights are initialized at zero, so the
residual stack begins as identity.  Hidden weights use small stateless-normal
initialization, and reverse permutations let later stages model dependencies
hidden by a single ordering.  These are tested implementation choices, not
universal optimal defaults.

\section{Reverse-KL training and its gradient}
\label{sec:bf-neutra-training}

Let $r(z)=\calN(0,I)$ and let $q_\phi$ be the pushforward distribution of
$\theta=T_\phi(z)$.  Change of variables gives
\begin{equation}
 \log q_\phi(T_\phi(z))=\log r(z)-\log|\det J_T(z)|.
\end{equation}
The normalized posterior is
$\pi_\Theta(\theta)=\widetilde\pi_\Theta(\theta)/Z$.  Thus
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{KL}(q_\phi\Vert\pi_\Theta)
 &=\E_{z\sim r}\left[
 \log r(z)-\log|\det J_T(z)|-\ell_\Theta(T_\phi(z))
 \right]+\log Z.
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-reverse-kl-full}
\end{align}
Neither $\log r(z)$ nor $\log Z$ depends on $\phi$, so the optimized loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L(\phi)=-\E_{z\sim r}\left[
 \ell_\Theta(T_\phi(z))+\log|\det J_T(z)|\right].
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-reverse-kl}
\end{equation}
For a batch $z^{(1:B)}$, the unbiased Monte Carlo estimator is the corresponding
sample mean.  Reparameterization yields
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_\phi\mathcal L=-\E_r\left[
 J_{T,\phi}(z)^\top\nabla_\theta\ell_\Theta(T_\phi(z))+
 \nabla_\phi\log|\det J_T(z)|\right].
\end{equation}
This is differentiated through the same declared target used later by HMC.

Reverse KL penalizes mass placed where the posterior density is low but may
underrepresent separated modes or tails.  Consequently, heldout loss can
select a training recipe, and nonfinite loss or gradients can veto training,
but loss cannot establish posterior convergence.  HMC corrects density error
only if finite computation actually explores all relevant latent regions.

BayesFilter uses stateless Gaussian batches, one leading-batch target call,
float64 TensorFlow/TFP, XLA, GPU memory growth, global gradient clipping, and
Adam with bias correction and linear learning-rate decay.  The full training
loop is TensorFlow control flow.  No NumPy or Python sample-axis loop belongs
to the active training/HMC route.  A valid segmented resume restores weights,
both Adam moments, completed step, seed schedule, and learning-rate schedule.

\section{Geometry induced in source space}

NeuTra may also be understood as a learned position-dependent metric.  With
identity latent mass, its Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
 H_Z(z,p)=-\ell_\Theta(T(z))-\log|J(z)|+\tfrac12p^\top p.
\end{equation}
Let $J=J_T(z)$ and define source momentum $p_\theta=J^{-\top}p$.  Then
$p=J^\top p_\theta$ and
\begin{align}
 \tfrac12p^\top p&=\tfrac12p_\theta^\top JJ^\top p_\theta,\\
 -\log|J|&=\tfrac12\log|(JJ^\top)^{-1}|.
\end{align}
Hence the same scalar Hamiltonian in $\theta$ has metric
\begin{equation}
 G(\theta)=(J(z)J(z)^\top)^{-1},
\end{equation}
which explains how a nonlinear map adapts effective scale and orientation with
position.  Unlike general Riemannian HMC, computation remains an explicit,
separable Euclidean HMC problem in $z$.  The benefit exists only where the
learned $J$ approximates useful posterior geometry.

\section{Complete BayesFilter algorithm}

For a valid batched target, the operational algorithm is:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Define the physical model, prior, source chart, likelihood or
 filter-defined approximate likelihood, scalar log target, score, and target
 health telemetry.  Verify value/score and batch identity before training.
 \item Choose a fixed affine lift $(c,L)$ and a small, target-specific candidate
 set of IAF widths and learning rates.  Train each screen candidate with
 \eqref{eq:bf-neutra-reverse-kl}; compare on common heldout latent noise.
 \item Train a fresh final transport from independent initialization using the
 nominated recipe.  Freeze and serialize its exact component order, weights,
 biases, masks, affine lift, target signature, and training-state hash.
 \item Construct \eqref{eq:bf-neutra-transported-target} from the frozen map.
 Check trainable/frozen parity, finite values, scores, and target telemetry.
 \item Screen fixed HMC pairs $(\epsilon,L)$ with disjoint seeds.  Acceptance
 and short-chain ESS nominate candidates; finite states, target health, energy
 errors, and modern R-hat may veto them.
 \item Retain warm-up chunks separately until the warm-up gate passes or the
 cap is reached.  Never pool warm-up into posterior summaries.
 \item Accumulate fresh retained chunks until the joint R-hat, ESS, and health
 gates pass or the retained cap is reached.
 \item Transform retained $z$ draws to source and physical coordinates and
 apply the predeclared scientific diagnostic.  Archive all three coordinates,
 seeds, settings, telemetry, hashes, and wall time.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Convergence and scientific diagnostics}
\label{sec:bf-neutra-hmc-protocol}

For $M$ chains of length $N$, each chain is split in half, producing $2M$
sequences of length $n=N/2$.  Pool the draws, replace ranks $r$ by normal
scores
\begin{equation}
 z=\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\frac{r-3/8}{S+1/4}\right),
\end{equation}
where $S=MN$.  If $\bar z_m$ and $s_m^2$ are split-chain means and variances,
\begin{align}
 W&=\frac1{2M}\sum_m s_m^2,\\
 B&=\frac{n}{2M-1}\sum_m(\bar z_m-\bar z)^2,\\
 \widehat V&=\frac{n-1}{n}W+\frac1nB,\qquad
 \widehat R_{\rm rank}=\sqrt{\widehat V/W}.
\end{align}
Folded R-hat repeats the calculation for
$|x-\operatorname{median}(x)|$, detecting scale discrepancies.  BayesFilter
reports
\begin{equation}
 \widehat R_{\rm modern,j}=\max\{\widehat R_{\rm rank,j},
 \widehat R_{\rm folded,j}\}.
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-modern-rhat}
\end{equation}
Thus folded R-hat is required, but it is not the sole reported R-hat.

For an estimand with autocorrelations $\rho_t$, the conceptual effective sample
size is
\begin{equation}
 N_{\rm eff}=\frac{MN}{1+2\sum_{t\ge1}\rho_t},
\end{equation}
with a stable positive-sequence estimator used in computation.  Bulk ESS uses
rank-normalized draws; tail ESS evaluates lower and upper tail indicators and
takes the conservative tail value.  These are finite-run diagnostics, not
proofs of convergence.

The current serious protocol uses four chains, warm-up modern R-hat at most
$1.05$, retained modern R-hat at most $1.01$, minimum bulk ESS $1000$, minimum
tail ESS $400$, and caps of $10{,}000$ warm-up and retained draws per chain.
The value $1000$ is a conservative convenience rule, not a theorem.  The
structural result below missed it narrowly and is labeled with the disclosed
post-result owner adjudication rather than called an automated pass.

TFP's fixed HamiltonianMonteCarlo kernel did not expose native divergence flags
for these runs.  The preserved hard screen defines an energy failure as a
nonfinite log acceptance ratio or one below $-1000$.  This narrower telemetry
is reported honestly and is not renamed as a native divergence diagnostic.

For synthetic data with known truth $\vartheta_j^*$ and $S$ pooled retained
draws, define
\begin{equation}
 \widehat F_j(\vartheta_j^*)=
 \frac{n_{<,j}+\tfrac12n_{=,j}+\tfrac12}{S+1},\qquad
 p_{{\rm truth},j}=2\min\{\widehat F_j,1-\widehat F_j\}.
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-truth-tail}
\end{equation}
This posterior-tail diagnostic is not a frequentist p-value.  The cost-bounded
rule runs one dataset seed first: pass if every value is at least $0.05$;
repeat one fresh seed for a marginal $[0.003,0.05)$ value; classify and
investigate immediately below $0.003$.  A sampler veto makes truth recovery
inconclusive.  Truth at the prior mean is deliberately favorable; noncentral
fixtures are labeled.

\section{Filter-defined posterior targets used below}
\label{sec:bf-neutra-filter-targets}

The nonlinear experiments do not use an unspecified ``UKF likelihood'' or
``SGQF likelihood.''  This section defines the scalar approximation inserted
into \eqref{eq:bf-neutra-source-target}.

Suppose a filtering rule represents $x_{t-1}\mid y_{1:t-1},\vartheta$ by mean
$m_{t-1}$ and covariance $P_{t-1}$.  Let standardized points $\xi_r$ and
weights $w_r^{(m)},w_r^{(c)}$ be fixed.  Given a square-root factor $S$ with
$SS^\top=P_{t-1}$, place $x_{t-1}^{(r)}=m_{t-1}+S\xi_r$.  For an
additive-noise transition $f_\vartheta$ and observation $h_\vartheta$, form
\begin{align}
 \widetilde x_t^{(r)}&=f_\vartheta(x_{t-1}^{(r)}),\\
 m_t^-&=\sum_rw_r^{(m)}\widetilde x_t^{(r)},\\
 P_t^-&=Q_t+\sum_rw_r^{(c)}
   (\widetilde x_t^{(r)}-m_t^-)(\widetilde x_t^{(r)}-m_t^-)^\top.
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-filter-predict}
\end{align}
When process noise enters nonadditively, the rule is placed on the block
Gaussian $(x_{t-1},\varepsilon_t)$ and the transition consumes both parts; this
is the structural-UKF route used for
\eqref{eq:bf-neutra-structural-model}.  No artificial variance is then added
to deterministic completion coordinates.

Refactor $P_t^-=S_t^-(S_t^-)^\top$, place
$\widehat x_t^{(r)}=m_t^-+S_t^-\xi_r$, and set
\begin{align}
 y_t^{(r)}&=h_\vartheta(\widehat x_t^{(r)}),\\
 \mu_t&=\sum_rw_r^{(m)}y_t^{(r)},\\
 F_t&=R_t+\sum_rw_r^{(c)}(y_t^{(r)}-\mu_t)(y_t^{(r)}-\mu_t)^\top,\\
 C_t&=\sum_rw_r^{(c)}(\widehat x_t^{(r)}-m_t^-)
       (y_t^{(r)}-\mu_t)^\top,\\
 K_t&=C_tF_t^{-1},\qquad m_t=m_t^-+K_t(y_t-\mu_t),\\
 P_t&=P_t^--K_tF_tK_t^\top.
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-filter-update}
\end{align}
The filter-defined log likelihood is the Gaussian prediction-error scalar
\begin{equation}
 \ell_{\rm filt}(\vartheta)=-\frac12\sum_{t=1}^T\left[
 d_y\log(2\pi)+\log|F_t|+v_t^\top F_t^{-1}v_t\right],
 \quad v_t=y_t-\mu_t.
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-filter-likelihood}
\end{equation}
For any scalar source coordinate $a$, differentiation of one increment gives
\begin{equation}
 \partial_a\ell_t=-\frac12\left[
 \operatorname{tr}(F_t^{-1}\partial_aF_t)
 +2(\partial_av_t)^\top F_t^{-1}v_t
 -v_t^\top F_t^{-1}(\partial_aF_t)F_t^{-1}v_t\right].
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-filter-score}
\end{equation}
Propagating derivatives through
\eqref{eq:bf-neutra-filter-predict}--\eqref{eq:bf-neutra-filter-update}, including
the square-root derivative, supplies the score of the same scalar
\eqref{eq:bf-neutra-filter-likelihood}.  BayesFilter uses this checked manual
or custom-gradient recursion rather than a different force.

For the principal-square-root UKF, in dimension $d$ the unscented rule is
\begin{align}
 \lambda&=\alpha^2(d+\kappa)-d,\\
 \xi_0&=0,\quad \xi_i=\sqrt{d+\lambda}\,e_i,\quad
 \xi_{i+d}=-\sqrt{d+\lambda}\,e_i,\\
 w_0^{(m)}&=\frac{\lambda}{d+\lambda},\quad
 w_0^{(c)}=w_0^{(m)}+1-\alpha^2+\beta,\\
 w_i^{(m)}&=w_i^{(c)}=\frac1{2(d+\lambda)}\quad(i>0).
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-ukf-rule}
\end{align}
The tested rule fixes $(\alpha,\beta,\kappa)=(1,2,0)$ and uses the unique
symmetric positive-definite principal square root.  Here $\lambda=0$, so the
mean center weight is zero and the covariance center weight is two.

For the fixed level-2 SGQF in dimension $d$, the merged Smolyak/Gauss--Hermite
cloud has the closed form
\begin{equation}
 \xi_0=0,\quad w_0=1-\frac d3,\qquad
 \xi_{j,\pm}=\pm\sqrt3e_j,\quad w_{j,\pm}=\frac16,\quad j=1,\ldots,d.
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-sgqf-rule}
\end{equation}
The normalization follows from
\begin{equation}
 w_0+\sum_{j=1}^d(w_{j,+}+w_{j,-})
 =1-\frac d3+2d\frac16=1.
\end{equation}
Symmetry makes the weighted mean zero.  Off-diagonal second moments vanish,
and coordinate $j$ has second moment
\begin{equation}
 \frac16(\sqrt3)^2+\frac16(-\sqrt3)^2=1,
\end{equation}
so the weighted covariance is $I_d$.  Predator--prey has $d=2$, hence five
points and center weight $1/3$.  SIR has $d=18$, hence 37 points and center
weight $-5$; that negative weight is part of the declared rule and is retained.
Both filters use
\eqref{eq:bf-neutra-filter-predict}--\eqref{eq:bf-neutra-filter-likelihood}; only
their fixed point clouds and square-root branches differ.

All bounded-uniform physical priors below use the explicit probit chart
\begin{align}
 \vartheta_i&=l_i+(u_i-l_i)\Phi(\theta_i),\\
 \frac{d\vartheta_i}{d\theta_i}&=(u_i-l_i)\phi(\theta_i),\\
 \log p(\vartheta_i)+\log\left|\frac{d\vartheta_i}{d\theta_i}\right|
 &=\log\phi(\theta_i),\qquad
 \partial_{\theta_i}\log\!\left[
 p(\vartheta_i)\left|\frac{d\vartheta_i}{d\theta_i}\right|\right]
 =-\theta_i.
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-probit-chart}
\end{align}
The interval width cancels the uniform density exactly.  These chart terms are
part of the source target before the NeuTra Jacobian is added.

\section{Models in the common truth-tail experiment}
\label{sec:bf-neutra-common-models}

Five retained archives are comparable under
\eqref{eq:bf-neutra-truth-tail}.  Each has four chains and $4000$ retained
draws per chain, hence $16{,}000$ pooled draws.  Warm-up is separate.

\subsection{Four-dimensional exact LGSSM}

The model is
\begin{equation}
 x_t=Ax_{t-1}+\eta_t,\quad \eta_t\sim\calN(0,Q),\qquad
 y_t=x_t+\epsilon_t,\quad \epsilon_t\sim\calN(0,R),
\end{equation}
with $T=120$, stationary Gaussian initialization, diagonal $Q,R$, and lower
triangular
\begin{equation}
 A=\begin{pmatrix}.62&0&0&0\\.18&.48&0&0\\-.10&.14&.30&0\\
 .06&-.08&.11&.16\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The process standard deviations are $(.30,.26,.22,.18)$ and observation
standard deviations $(.12,.11,.10,.09)$.  The 18 inferred quantities are ten
free elements of $A$ and eight standard deviations.  The likelihood is the
exact Kalman prediction-error likelihood.  In raw coordinates, the prior is
independent Gaussian and centered at the frozen truth, with standard deviation
$.5$ for the four diagonal transition coordinates, $.6$ for the six strict
lower-triangular coordinates, and $.35$ for each log process or observation
standard deviation.  Warm-up/retained counts were
$2000/4000$ per chain; maximum modern R-hat was $1.00215$, minimum bulk/tail
ESS $4571.6/3977.0$, and the energy-error count was zero.

\subsection{Predator--prey with UKF and SGQF likelihoods}

For prey $X$ and predator $Y$, the continuous dynamics are
\begin{align}
 \dot X&=rX(1-X/K)-s\frac{XY}{a+X},\\
 \dot Y&=u\frac{XY}{a+X}-vY.
\end{align}
They are integrated by RK4 over intervals of length two using step $0.1$.
The initial state is $\calN((50,5)^\top,I_2)$; process and observation covariance
are both $4I_2$; both state coordinates are observed; and $T=20$.  Independent
uniform priors use the box
\begin{equation}
 r\in[.1,1.1],\ K\in[110,130],\ a\in[20,30],\ s\in[.1,1.1],\
 u,v\in[0,1],
\end{equation}
implemented by six probit charts with their Jacobians.  The generating vector
is
\begin{equation}
 (r,K,a,s,u,v)=(.6,114,25,.3,.5,.5).
\end{equation}
Thus $K$ and $s$ are not at prior means, requiring
the noncentral label.  The two posterior targets differ only in the declared
approximate filter likelihood: principal-square-root UKF versus fixed SGQF.
Both used $2000/4000$ warm-up/retained draws per chain and zero recorded energy
failures.  UKF had maximum modern R-hat $1.00081$ and minimum bulk/tail ESS

\subsection{Parameterized spatial SIR with SGQF likelihood}

Nine regions have susceptible-infectious pairs $(S_j,I_j)$, giving an
18-dimensional state.  With graph adjacency $A_{jk}$ and degree $d_j$, the
continuous dynamics are
\begin{align}
 \dot S_j&=-\kappa S_jI_j+\tfrac12\sum_kA_{jk}S_k-\tfrac12d_jS_j,\\
 \dot I_j&=\kappa S_jI_j-\nu I_j+\tfrac12\sum_kA_{jk}I_k-\tfrac12d_jI_j.
\end{align}
All nine infectious coordinates are observed.  The frozen $T=20$ fixture uses
unit process covariance and base observation covariance $100I_9$.  Three
source parameters have independent $\calN(0,.5^2)$ priors and scale
\begin{equation}
 \kappa=.1e^{\theta_1},\qquad \nu=18e^{\theta_2},\qquad
 \operatorname{sd}(e)=10e^{\theta_3}.
\end{equation}
The truth $(.1,18,10)$ is therefore prior-centered.  The target uses a fixed
level-2, 37-point SGQF observed-data likelihood.  Warm-up/retained counts were
$2000/4000$; maximum modern R-hat was $1.00007$, minimum bulk/tail ESS
$16358.5/14568.5$, and the energy-error count was zero.

\subsection{Structural deterministic dynamics with UKF likelihood}

The structural model is
\begin{align}
 m_t&=\rho m_{t-1}+\sigma\varepsilon_t,\quad\varepsilon_t\sim\calN(0,1),\\
 k_t&=\phi k_{t-1}+\gamma m_t^2,\\
 y_t&=m_t+k_t+e_t,\quad e_t\sim\calN(0,R).
 \label{eq:bf-neutra-structural-model}
\end{align}
There is no artificial innovation in $k_t$.  The initial state has mean zero
and covariance $\operatorname{diag}(.04,.09)$.  A principal-square-root
structural UKF supplies the approximate likelihood for $T=100$.  Independent
uniform priors, written in the reporting order
$(\rho,\sigma,\phi,\gamma,R)$, are
\begin{equation}
 \rho\in[.05,.98],\quad \sigma\in[.05,1.25],\quad
 \phi\in[.05,.98],\quad
 \gamma\in[.02,1],\quad R\in[.02,1],
\end{equation}
again represented by probit charts.  The truth
$(\rho,\sigma,\phi,\gamma,R)=(.8,.5,.7,.4,.25)$ differs from prior mean
$(.515,.65,.515,.51,.51)$ in the same order, so this is noncentral.

The final transport was a fresh 5000-step, three-stage dense IAF with hidden
widths $(30,30)$ and learning rate $5\times10^{-3}$, selected from four
500-step screens.  Heldout reverse KL was $137.734$, versus $195.591$ for the
identity/source-prior baseline.  This is training evidence only.  The admitted
fixed HMC kernel used step size $.05$ and 12 leapfrog steps.  Verification had
zero energy failures and modern R-hat $1.03240$ under the $1.05$ tuning gate.
Warm-up stopped at $4000$ draws per chain when the last 1000-draw window reached
$1.04742$.  Retained maximum rank/folded/modern R-hat were
$1.00668/1.00156/1.00668$; minimum bulk/tail ESS were $971.06/2354.53$.
The original convenience gate of bulk ESS $1000$ was missed by $2.9\%$.
After seeing the result, the owner accepted it under an explicitly disclosed
threshold of $900$.  The result is therefore qualified and owner-adjudicated,
not an automated pass of the original rule.

\section{Additional historical model tests}
\label{sec:bf-neutra-historical}

The broader ledger counts a family once for shared scientific dynamics and a
configuration separately when its posterior likelihood or solution/filter
construction changes.  Seeds, dimensions, transports, and tuning candidates
do not inflate the count.  Including the five configurations above, learned
NeuTra reached transformed HMC in ten families and thirteen configurations.
The following eight configurations used their original protocols and must not
be interpreted as common truth-tail tests.

The historical terminology is fixed as follows.  ``Second-moment R-hat'' and
``second-moment ESS'' apply the run's original convergence estimators to the
coordinate-wise estimands $x_j^2$; they were intended to emphasize scale and
tail exploration and are not the modern rank/folded diagnostics defined above.
A ``quality pass'' in the local paper-suite required finite selected chains and
median-over-seed second-moment R-hat below the threshold declared for that
run.  The synthetic dimension-100 ladder used two seeds, four chains, 2048
retained draws per chain, threshold 2.0, and a retained-draw budget ratio
$0.001$.  The ratio is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{(\text{local chains})(\text{local retained draws per chain})}
 {16384\times500},
\end{equation}
because the source paper ran 1000 transitions and discarded the first 500.
Thus ``paper-scale'' below means the same target family and dimension, not the
same sampling budget.  A historical configuration is called ``clean/strong''
only when its own declared finite, sampler, and completion screens passed; it
is ``qualified/marginal'' when a named budget, convergence, telemetry, or
parent-artifact condition remained open.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
 \item[Neal funnel, dimension 100.] The exact unnormalized density was
 \begin{equation}
  \log\widetilde\pi(y,x)=-\tfrac12y^2-\tfrac12e^{-2y}
  \sum_{j=1}^{99}x_j^2-99y,
 \end{equation}
 equivalently $y\sim\calN(0,1)$ and $x_j\mid y\sim\calN(0,e^{2y})$.
 It was tested with
 diagonal, triangular-affine, and three-stage dense-IAF transports.  At the
 preserved local budget, IAF gave acceptance $.838$, second-moment R-hat
 $1.01867$, and minimum second-moment ESS $376.3$, whereas affine funnel
 controls had second-moment ESS below 10.  This passed its preserved quality
 screen; its
 diagnostics and budget differ from the modern common protocol.

 \item[Ill-conditioned Gaussian, dimension 100.] The target was
 $\calN(0,\Sigma)$.  A seed-10 Gaussian matrix supplied an orthogonal $Q$;
 sorted draws $\lambda_i\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(.8,1)$ defined
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma=Q\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i^{-1})Q^\top.
\end{equation}
It tested constant and
 neural geometry.  Triangular affine and IAF were
 both strong: second-moment R-hat $1.00277/1.00244$ and minimum second-moment ESS
 $2716.5/2724.5$.  This shows that nonlinear transport was not necessary for a
 target whose geometry a full affine map could remove.

 \item[German-credit sparse logistic regression, dimension 51.] The dataset
 had 1000 observations and 25 raw columns; 24 predictors were range-scaled,
 mapped by $2x-1$, and augmented by an intercept, giving $d=25$.  Binary
 outcomes obeyed
\begin{equation}
 y_n\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\!\left(
 \operatorname{logit}^{-1}(x_n^\top[\tau z\circ\lambda])\right),
\end{equation}
with
 $z_j\sim\calN(0,1)$ and independent Gamma$(.5,.5)$ global $\tau$ and local
 $\lambda_j$ scales.  Log scales make the unconstrained dimension
 $2d+1=51$.  A three-stage IAF with
 step $.05$ and 32 leapfrogs achieved acceptance $.918$, second-moment R-hat
 $1.01564$, and minimum second-moment ESS $414.7$ across three seeds, six chains,
 and 768 retained draws per chain.  Its quality threshold was 1.2.  It is
 qualified because budget ratio $.0005625$ remained below the predeclared
 $.001$ gate, not because the chain exhibited a hard numerical failure.

 \item[NK-like analytic surrogate.] In local coordinates $x\in\R^9$, let
\begin{equation}
 r=\|x\|,\qquad a=.35\tanh(x_1)s(r),\qquad
 \lambda_s=1+.25(r/3)^2,
\end{equation}
and let $\lambda_w$ be the smooth lower-bounded version of
$.15[1-.6(r/3)^2]$.  If $R(a)$ rotates the first two coordinates and
 $P_2(x)=R(a)\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_w,\lambda_s)R(a)^\top$, the target is
 \begin{equation}
  \log\widetilde\pi(x)=-\tfrac12x_{1:2}^\top P_2(x)x_{1:2}
  -\tfrac12\sum_{j=3}^9x_j^2.
 \end{equation}
 Here $s(r)$ smoothly caps $r/3$ at one and the weak eigenvalue is smoothly
 floored at $10^{-3}$, with softplus scales $.05$ and $.002$.  This
 nine-dimensional changing-Hessian target used three IAF stages with $(9,9)$ hidden
 layers, 5000 reverse-KL steps, batch 4096, and a 49-candidate fixed HMC grid.
 The selected $.5\times3$ kernel had acceptance $.757$, zero recorded
 divergences, preserved $x$-coordinate R-hat $1.00158$, and minimum preserved
 $x$-coordinate ESS $2541.5$.  These preserved diagnostics are not silently
 promoted to the modern rank/folded definitions.  This is one-seed
 strong diagnostic evidence, not a method ranking.

 \item[Small/real NK local posterior.] This is a nine-parameter Small-NK
 state-space posterior: the model solution and observed data define an exact
 linear-Gaussian Kalman likelihood, combined with the model's declared prior
 in unconstrained coordinates.  It is a real local posterior, not the analytic
 surrogate in the preceding item.  It used a
 3000-step three-stage IAF and 49 CPU HMC candidates.  All worker results were
 recovered; the selected $.5\times4$ kernel had acceptance $.918$, zero
 divergences, preserved $x$-coordinate R-hat $1.00077$, and minimum preserved
 $x$-coordinate ESS $9011.3$.  These are not asserted to be modern rank/folded
 diagnostics.  The parent timed out before
 canonical reduction, so the result is qualified despite strong worker-level
 diagnostics.  ``Canonical reduction'' means the parent process did not write
 its normal aggregate JSON after all 49 workers; the selected row was rebuilt
 by the same deterministic key from hash-checked worker outputs.

 \item[Small/real NK principal-square-root SVD--UKF.] The same economic model
 was paired with a nonlinear principal-square-root SVD--UKF approximate
 prediction-error likelihood rather than the linear Kalman likelihood.  A
 fixed diagonal transport trained with batch 4096 for 5000 steps and an HMC
 kernel with step $.5$, 16 leapfrogs, four chains, and 1536 retained draws per
 chain reached acceptance $.703$, preserved per-parameter bulk/tail ESS
 $550.4/615.8$, but had preserved per-parameter R-hat $1.01774>1.01$ and lacked
 native divergence telemetry.  The artifact does not establish that this
 R-hat was the modern rank/folded maximum.  It remains a marginal
 continuation candidate, not a clean pass.

 \item[Rotemberg NK linear-Kalman posterior.] The linearized 15-parameter
 economic solution with exact Kalman likelihood completed calibrated
 learned-NeuTra HMC.  Four chains used 1536 burn-in and 1536 retained draws;
 the selected step $.65$ and 25-leapfrog kernel had acceptance $.743$, preserved
 maximum R-hat $1.00250$, minimum preserved per-parameter bulk/tail ESS
 $3793.4/1484.5$, and finite samples.  The record does not identify that R-hat
 as the modern rank/folded maximum.
 It is a clean historical baseline, not a claim about the nonlinear Rotemberg
 posterior.

 \item[Rotemberg NK second-order SVD posterior.] A four-shock minimal
 principal-square-root second-order state construction, coupled to an SVD
 sigma-point approximate likelihood, produced a 15-dimensional HMC state.
 Training and a 612-candidate fixed grid selected step $.72917$ and two
 leapfrogs, with acceptance $.732$, zero selected-candidate energy failures,
 maximum preserved $x$-coordinate R-hat $1.00324$, and minimum preserved
 $x$-coordinate ESS $2248.3$.  These are not asserted to be modern rank/folded
 diagnostics.  This is a completed-valid
 serious baseline; no comparative ranking or economic-model adequacy follows.
\end{description}

Nine of the thirteen configurations are clean or strong under their preserved
screens.  Four are qualified or marginal: German budget ratio, real-NK parent
timeout, NK SVD--UKF convergence/telemetry, and structural post-result ESS
adjudication.  Exact/KSC stochastic volatility and several Zhao--Cui cells
stopped before retained learned-NeuTra HMC and are therefore not counted.

\section{Interpretation, failure modes, and nonclaims}

The evidence supports the bounded statement that learned NeuTra has produced
viable transformed-HMC sampling across a broad set of difficult geometries,
including exact linear-Gaussian, nonlinear biological, epidemic, structural,
hierarchical, regression, and DSGE-derived targets.  In the five comparable
synthetic configurations, all 38 inspected truths were non-extreme and inside
empirical 95\% intervals for one frozen dataset each.

The strongest alternative explanations are favorable fixture choice, reverse
KL missing a mode not visited by finite HMC, approximate filter likelihoods
defining a different posterior from the exact scientific model, and
heterogeneous historical protocols.  The results therefore do not establish:
\begin{itemize}
 \item repeated-sampling calibration or 95\% frequentist coverage;
 \item exactness of UKF, SGQF, or SVD-filter posteriors relative to the latent
 data-generating model;
 \item universal mode or tail recovery by reverse KL;
 \item statistical superiority over plain HMC, NUTS, RMHMC, or another map;
 \item convergence merely because R-hat and ESS cross finite thresholds;
 \item universal architecture, optimizer, HMC, production, or default policy.
\end{itemize}

The engineering, sampler, and scientific ledgers remain separate.  A correct
map can train poorly; a low loss can yield bad HMC; valid HMC can place truth
in an extreme tail; and one-fixture success can fail under repeated datasets.
That separation is not procedural caution: it is the mathematical distinction
between implementing the intended density, exploring it adequately, and
learning something scientifically defensible from the resulting posterior.
