\section{Marginalizing the ancestor}
\label{sec:bf-pfpf-marginal-weights}

The ancestor-conditioned identity is correct under its stated assumptions.
Nevertheless, a child moved near a different ancestor's predictive mass can
receive an extreme weight when its correction uses only its own ancestor.
In the range--bearing example, narrow transition distributions separate nearby
predictive components while nonlinear observations move children across them.
Marginalization evaluates how much predictive and proposal density all
ancestors assign to the same child. It changes the finite estimator; it does
not repair an algebraic error in the original identity.

Condition on the incoming cloud, observation, and offline controls. Let
$\omega_j$ be normalized weights and $m_j$ zero-innovation transition means.
The implemented extension requires additive full-rank Gaussian transitions:
\begin{equation}
 f_j(x)=\mathcal N(x;m_j,Q_\theta),\qquad
 p_N(x)=\sum_j\omega_j f_j(x),\qquad
 Z_N=\int g_\theta(y_t\mid x)p_N(x)\,dx.
 \label{eq:bf-marginal-predictive}
\end{equation}
Here $Q_\theta$ is the transition-noise covariance, not the UKF predicted
covariance $P^j$ used to construct the flow. Substituting one for the other
would change the proposal density.

The auxiliary path depends on the ancestor and observation but not on the
child's current innovation. Consequently its finite Euler steps define an
affine map $F_j(u)=B_ju+c_j$. For $M_{jr}=I+\epsilon_r A_{jr}$, accumulate
\begin{equation}
 B_{j,0}=I,\ c_{j,0}=0,\qquad
 B_{j,r+1}=M_{jr}B_{j,r},\quad
 c_{j,r+1}=M_{jr}c_{j,r}+\epsilon_r b_{jr}.
 \label{eq:bf-marginal-affine}
\end{equation}
This is the same ordered sequence that moves the actual particle. The
auxiliary endpoint is $a_j=F_j(m_j)$, so
\begin{equation}
 q_j(x)=\mathcal N(x;a_j,S_j),\quad S_j=B_jQ_\theta B_j^\top,\qquad
 q_N(x)=\sum_j\omega_jq_j(x).
 \label{eq:bf-marginal-proposal}
\end{equation}
The determinant correction is already present through $S_j$; adding a separate
determinant would count it twice. Every component covariance must be positive
definite. A singular transition, including an AR(2) state representation with
deterministic coordinates, requires a derivation in the innovation measure
and is unsupported by this full-state Gaussian formula.

For one child $X_i\sim q_i$ per ancestor, compare
\begin{align}
 V_i^{\rm anc}&=\omega_i g_\theta(y_t\mid X_i)f_i(X_i)/q_i(X_i),\notag\\
 V_i^{\rm mar}&=\omega_i g_\theta(y_t\mid X_i)p_N(X_i)/q_N(X_i).
 \label{eq:bf-marginal-weight}
\end{align}
The outer $\omega_i$ is necessary because every component supplies one child.
If instead $M$ labels were sampled independently from $\omega$, the estimator
would average $M$ mixture ratios with coefficient $1/M$.

Assume $g_\theta p_N$ is integrable and $q_N>0$ wherever $g_\theta p_N>0$.
For random children having the stated conditional marginal densities,
\begin{align}
 \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_iV_i^{\rm mar}\mid\hbox{incoming cloud}\right]
 &=\sum_i\omega_i\int q_i(x)g_\theta(y_t\mid x)
                                  \frac{p_N(x)}{q_N(x)}\,dx\notag\\
 &=\int g_\theta(y_t\mid x)p_N(x)\,dx=Z_N.
 \label{eq:bf-marginal-identity}
\end{align}
Independence between children is unnecessary for this expectation calculation.
Cancellation of $q_i$ gives the same expectation for the old weights. Both
therefore target the incoming cloud's predictive mixture; their distribution
of finite-sample error differs.

This applies the marginal-particle-filter construction
\citep[Section~3]{klaas2005marginal} to the actual affine LEDH proposals.
For a random label $J\sim\omega$ and $X\mid J\sim q_J$, conditioning
$g(X)f_J(X)/q_J(X)$ on $X$ gives $g(X)p_N(X)/q_N(X)$.
The law of total variance gives a Rao--Blackwell variance inequality if second
moments exist. That argument concerns a random label, and does not establish
smaller variance for one fixed child per ancestor, smaller log-likelihood
error, or a more accurate score. Fixing the random design and composing
deterministic resets over time also removes any justification for reading
\eqref{eq:bf-marginal-identity} as an unbiasedness theorem for that finite
log likelihood. For an identity flow, $q_j=f_j$ and both weight laws reduce
to $\omega_i g(X_i)$, providing a useful algebraic check.

\section{The analytical total score}
\label{sec:bf-pfpf-marginal-total-score}

Fix base innovations, reset designs, and offline controls, and write the
finite log likelihood as $\widehat\ell_N(\theta)$. The analytical recursion
computes its total derivative. It does not hold particle locations fixed or
substitute the derivative of the exact model likelihood. Let $D$ denote a
parameter-direction derivative on a neighborhood where the program is valid
and differentiable; it includes previous particles, weights, UKF covariances,
both flow paths, and the current evaluation point.

For $u=C^{-1}(x-\mu)$, direct differentiation of a Gaussian log density gives
\begin{equation}
 D\log\mathcal N(x;\mu,C)
 =u^\top(D\mu-Dx)+\tfrac12u^\top(DC)u
                         -\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1}DC).
 \label{eq:bf-marginal-gaussian-tangent}
\end{equation}
The first term includes the moving child; the others differentiate the
quadratic form and covariance determinant. In a proposal component,
\begin{equation}
 DS_j=(DB_j)QB_j^\top+B_j(DQ)B_j^\top+B_jQ(DB_j)^\top,\qquad
 DB_{j,r+1}=(DM_{jr})B_{j,r}+M_{jr}DB_{j,r}.
 \label{eq:bf-marginal-covariance-tangent}
\end{equation}
The code obtains $Da_j$ from the existing auxiliary-path tangent, not from
a separately constructed flow.

For FP32 execution, the covariance products receive a selective precision
check. Write $\widetilde S_j$ for the covariance obtained by the ordinary
matrix products, and use the scale-free symmetry measure
\begin{equation}
 \rho_j=\max_{a,b}\left|
 \frac{(\widetilde S_j)_{ab}}{\|\widetilde S_j\|_{\max}}-
 \frac{(\widetilde S_j)_{ba}}{\|\widetilde S_j\|_{\max}}\right|,
 \qquad \tau_d=8d\,2^{-23}.
 \label{eq:bf-marginal-symmetry-precision}
\end{equation}
The zero matrix is assigned $\rho_j=0$ and still fails the subsequent
positive-definiteness check. When $\widetilde S_j$ is finite and
$\rho_j>\tau_d$, the code recomputes both $S_j$ and its differential in
\eqref{eq:bf-marginal-covariance-tangent} using FP64 matrix products, then
casts them back to FP32. All other component products retain their original
values. The same final symmetry, finiteness and positive-definiteness checks
apply after recomputation. No ridge, eigenvalue clipping or relaxed tolerance
is introduced, and FP64 inputs keep their original calculation.

The analytical tangent follows the selected arithmetic branch. At a boundary
where that branch changes, smoothness is not asserted. This numerical repair
therefore needs separate assessment near switching boundaries before an HMC
force claim. The filter trace records the number of recomputed component
covariances at every observation; the batch diagnostic consumer preserves
these counts. The statistical weighting identity is unchanged by this
precision policy, while its finite numerical evaluation is changed on
otherwise rejected components.

For either mixture $h_N=\sum_j\omega_jh_j$, define responsibilities
$r_j^h=\omega_jh_j(X_i)/h_N(X_i)$. Differentiating the sum then dividing gives
\begin{equation}
 D\log h_N(X_i)=\sum_j r_j^h
                   \{D\log\omega_j+D\log h_j(X_i)\}.
 \label{eq:bf-marginal-mixture-tangent}
\end{equation}
These responsibilities are coefficients of the first derivative.
Differentiating them again here would incorrectly add second-derivative terms.
Consequently,
\begin{align}
 D\log V_i^{\rm mar}
 &=D\log\omega_i+D\log g_\theta(y_t\mid X_i)
                    +D\log p_N(X_i)-D\log q_N(X_i),\notag\\
 \widehat\ell_N&=\sum_t\log\sum_iV_{ti},\qquad
 D\widehat\ell_N=\sum_t\sum_i w_{ti}D\log V_{ti},\notag\\
 Dw_{ti}&=w_{ti}\left(D\log V_{ti}-\sum_jw_{tj}D\log V_{tj}\right),
 \qquad w_{ti}=V_{ti}/\sum_jV_{tj}.
 \label{eq:bf-marginal-filter-score}
\end{align}
These finite-program identities determine the quantity the implementation
must compute. Accuracy relative to the model score is a separate question.
Angle-branch boundaries and failed factorizations remain explicit validity
conditions.

\section{The complete October 2026 marginal-extension recursion}
\label{sec:bf-pfpf-current-recursion}

This section names the earlier marginal-extension implementation explicitly.
It is preserved as a derivation and historical diagnostic snapshot.  The
current covariance-guided shared consumer used by the five-model HMC campaign
is the three-branch recurrence documented in Chapter~\ref{ch:bf-ukf-covariance-proposal}.

The executable filter performs the following sequence at each observation.
Its score uses analytical recursive tangents; automatic differentiation and
finite differences are independent diagnostic checks.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Carry incoming particles, weights, particle-local UKF covariances and
 their parameter tangents. The initial distribution includes its parameter
 dependence.
 \item Predict each UKF covariance. Form its zero-innovation anchor and actual
 transition draw. Evolve both through the same local affine LEDH steps, carrying
 their tangents and the discrete determinant.
 \item Use \eqref{eq:bf-pfpf-alg1-weight} for the default ancestor policy.
 For the optional marginal policy, also carry $B_j,DB_j$ in that loop and use
 the complete mixtures and tangents in
 \eqref{eq:bf-marginal-weight}--\eqref{eq:bf-marginal-filter-score}.
 No component truncation or nearest-ancestor approximation is used.
 \item Accumulate the log normalizer and score, normalize the weights, and
 perform the UKF observation covariance update.
 \item Apply entropic transport and the existing Contract E--Chol reset,
 carrying covariances through the same transport. The total derivative includes
 direct source-moment/weight dependence and dependence through transport.
 A transported-cloud-only derivative would be wrong.
 \item Apply the existing GenUT higher-moment correction, including diagonal
 and pairwise controls, trust-region constraints and coordinate caps, with its
 analytical derivative and validity checks. Carry the equally weighted
 outgoing cloud and reset covariances into the next observation.
\end{enumerate}
The statistical change is step 3. Its changed weights also alter subsequent
moments, transport, correction, and future flow paths. Those algorithms are
unchanged, but their inputs and outputs generally differ.

The consumer \path{canonical_batch_fused_value_score} forwards
\path{importance_weight_policy} to the shared executor
\path{ledh_canonical_score_tf.py}. That executor calls
\path{marginal_prior_ratio_tangent} in
\path{ledh_marginal_weights_tf.py}. The default is \texttt{ancestor};
the extension requires \path{marginal_mixture}. Unsupported transition
callbacks, innovation geometries, and incompatible modes fail explicitly.
Marginal weighting, OT reset, and GenUT correction are BayesFilter extensions;
they are not claimed to be unchanged Li--Coates Algorithm~1.

Both mixtures sum every component using log-sum-exp and exact equal
row/column tiles. The extent is $K=N$ for $N\leq3000$; otherwise it is the
largest divisor of $N$ at most 3000. Thus $N=4096$ uses $K=2048$.
Tiling bounds intermediate storage but retains quadratic pair count, so
runtime must be compared alongside error.

For stationary LGSSMs, the initial covariance and its directional derivative
satisfy
\begin{align}
 P_0-FP_0F^\top&=Q,\notag\\
 DP_0-F(DP_0)F^\top
   &=(DF)P_0F^\top+FP_0(DF)^\top+DQ.
 \label{eq:bf-marginal-stationary-initialization}
\end{align}
The derivative includes both transition and process-noise dependence.
The implementation uses $\lVert F\rVert_2<1$ as a sufficient stability
certificate; this deliberately rejects some stable nonnormal matrices.
For FP32 inputs, its small Gram matrix, equation residual, and tangent
right-hand side now use FP64 products. The Lyapunov solves and returned
covariances remain FP32, and the filter retains TF32. This avoids evaluating a
full-FP32 residual tolerance with lower-precision TF32 products, without
changing the stationary law or loosening the validity check.
