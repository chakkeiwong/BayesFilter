\chaptermark{UKF Proposals and Covariance Resets}

Chapter~\ref{ch:bf-ledh-pfpf-ot-custom-gradient} requires the reset to preserve
importance-weighted moments and the score to differentiate the same finite
filtering computation. We now examine how a UKF covariance can guide the
proposal while respecting those requirements. The distinction is between
uncertainty conditional on one ancestor, uncertainty across the predictive
mixture, and uncertainty represented by the importance-weighted posterior.
Each covariance has a different role in the algorithm.

The construction below is the implemented covariance-guided proposal route used by the five-model surrogate-HMC campaign. It combines a common UKF guide, conditional local flows, one marginal importance correction, a Cholesky moment reset that preserves the first two weighted moments under the stated assumptions, and a bounded optional higher-moment correction. The original three-branch proposal is the baseline for the observation-conditioned extension described at the end of this chapter. That extension uses observation-weighted parents by default, while uniform parent selection remains a competitive alternative. Cross-model likelihood, score and ESS comparisons, including adverse results, are presented in Chapter~\ref{ch:bf-dpf-observation-parent-failures}; the evidence supports the default as a weight-balance choice, not as a guarantee of score accuracy or HMC suitability. The shared consumer is \path{make_batched_score_filter}, in \path{bayesfilter/highdim/covariance_proposal_score_batch_tf.py}. Every model adapter reaches that recurrence. The derivations establish the finite value and analytical total score that the code evaluates, while the case study in Chapter~\ref{ch:bf-surrogate-hmc-five-models} reports the separate endpoint, Metropolis correction and posterior diagnostics.
