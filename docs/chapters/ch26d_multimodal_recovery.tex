\section[Posterior mode coverage]{Covering posterior modes before fitting the transport}
\label{sec:bf-is-multimode-recovery}

For this application, posterior annealing followed by weighted forward-KL
fitting of the existing IAF is a candidate for further study. The modern
mutation, retention and flow-transport methods in
Sections~\ref{sec:bf-is-craft-detail}--\ref{sec:bf-is-modern-smc-dimension}
provide alternatives to a fixed, locally mutating SMC implementation. The
sampler must find relevant regions and estimate their probabilities; the IAF
then learns from those probabilities. This separates three tasks that were
previously too easily conflated: discovering basins, estimating their relative
mass, and making the transformed posterior easy for HMC. It also removes the
current flow density from the denominator of the sampling target. This is a
proposed change of training objective, not a repaired alpha-two FAB estimator
or an empirically established winner among methods.

Historical BayesFilter tests motivate this choice within a limited scope.
Defensive importance-weighted forward-KL fits represented known two- and
three-component targets, but their proposals already covered the components
and their neural architectures are now retired as unfaithful to the author's
code. Earlier q20 SMC supplied useful mass evidence for two known sign regions;
no mutation crossed between those signs. Earlier replica exchange achieved
temperature round trips without a converged cold posterior. None of those
results establishes unknown-mode coverage or validates the current IAF. They
suggest which mechanisms deserve a fresh, correctly scoped investigation.\footnote{\raggedright

\subsection{Use a path ending at the posterior}

Write the intended unnormalized posterior as $\gamma(x)=r(x)L(x)$ with
normalized prior $r$, and assume for the following derivations that its
likelihood is a well-defined function with $0<L(x)\le M<\infty$. In the
ordinary UKF value model, the fixed scalar observation variance floor gives
$M=(2\pi R)^{-T/2}$; time-varying floors give the corresponding product.
The executable's numerical-domain issues must be resolved before applying
these identities to its output.

The usual likelihood-annealing path is
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_\beta(x)=r(x)L(x)^\beta,\qquad
 Z_\beta=\int r(x)L(x)^\beta\,dx,\qquad
 p_\beta=\gamma_\beta/Z_\beta,\quad 0\le\beta\le1.
 \label{eq:bf-is-posterior-path}
\end{equation}
Every $Z_\beta$ is finite and positive since $L^\beta\le M^\beta$; the path
starts at the prior and ends at the desired posterior. By contrast, the FAB
path in \eqref{eq:bf-fab-bridge} continues past the posterior to an auxiliary
law containing $1/q_\phi$. Ordinary posterior annealing therefore avoids
the specific integrability failure just proved, without assuming that the
sampler will explore efficiently.

For a fixed ladder $0=\beta_0<\cdots<\beta_J=1$, SMC begins with prior
draws and updates normalized weights by
\begin{equation}
 u_{ki}=L(X_{k-1,i})^{\beta_k-\beta_{k-1}},\qquad
 \bar W_{ki}=
 \frac{\bar W_{k-1,i}u_{ki}}{\sum_j\bar W_{k-1,j}u_{kj}}.
 \label{eq:bf-is-posterior-smc-weight}
\end{equation}
Resampling uses these probabilities, and subsequent mutation must preserve
$p_{\beta_k}$. The importance factors estimate changes in probability mass;
mutation explores locations. Neither operation can compensate for an
incorrect likelihood, a missing Metropolis correction, or silently discarded
invalid evaluations. \citet[Algorithm 2.1 and Sections 2.3--2.4]{dai2022smc}
describe this construction and distinguish adaptive tuning from fixed-design
identities.

A pilot can choose a temperature increment by controlling the concentration
of its incremental weights. Replacing expectations under $p_\beta$ by the
current weighted empirical measure gives the conditional ESS diagnostic
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{CESS}(\Delta\beta)=
 N\frac{\{\sum_i\bar W_i L(X_i)^{\Delta\beta}\}^2}
          {\sum_i\bar W_i L(X_i)^{2\Delta\beta}}.
 \label{eq:bf-is-weighted-cess}
\end{equation}
For equal weights this reduces to the ESS expression in
\citet[Equation 2.7]{dai2022smc}. It measures the incremental discrepancy
under the represented distribution, not the number of independent particles
or the mass of unseen modes. The threshold and mutation effort require
target-specific pilot checks. For subsequent fixed-design evidence, freeze
the ladder and all proposal or kernel parameters, then use independent
populations. Freezing the ladder alone while estimating a mass matrix from
the same population does not produce a fixed kernel. Resampling according
to a declared ESS rule can remain adaptive under the usual unbiased
resampling construction.

The finite-population estimates remain approximate even with correct weights.
Independent populations, surviving ancestral lineages, region occupation and
sensitivity to particle count and mutation effort are needed to assess them.
The ancestry-based variance analysis in \citet[Section 4.1]{dai2022smc}
explains why resampled copies are not independent observations. Its favorable
dimensional scaling arguments assume strong, sometimes perfect, intermediate
mixing; those assumptions must not be transferred to a difficult local kernel.

\subsection{Search for basins, but infer their masses from weighted sampling}

This target has four free parameters, so a broad multistart search is a
plausible aid despite its much larger latent state. Optimizing $\log\gamma$
from dispersed starts can identify candidate basins, with local curvature
helping to distinguish duplicate solutions. \citet[Section 2.2 and
Algorithm 3]{pompe2020adaptive} use this idea with an adaptive multimodal
MCMC construction. A mode is a density maximum; its height and the number of
optimizer starts reaching it are not its posterior probability. Curved ridges,
unequal widths and small attraction basins can defeat a naive count of modes.
The paper's asymptotic discovery conditions do not certify a finite search.

Candidate basins can instead guide evaluable proposals. One useful family at
temperature $\beta$ is
\begin{equation}
 m_\beta(x)=\lambda r(x)+(1-\lambda)
              \sum_{k=1}^{K_{\rm loc}}v_k t_k(x),\qquad
 0<\lambda\le1,\quad v_k\ge0,\quad\sum_kv_k=1,
 \label{eq:bf-is-mode-mixture}
\end{equation}
where $t_k$ are normalized Gaussian or Student components fitted around the
discovered basins. Their $v_k$ are proposal allocations, not assumed posterior
masses. The prior component preserves support while the fitted components
spend more proposals in plausible regions. GRAMIS supplies gradient and
curvature-based ways to move such components; safe adaptive importance
sampling supplies domination arguments
\citep{elvira2023gramis,delyon2021safe}. A Hessian approximation is a proposal
construction here, not an assertion that a nonlinear posterior is Gaussian.

For a frozen $m_\beta$, the independence Metropolis move $Y\sim m_\beta$
accepts with
\begin{equation}
 a_\beta(x,y)=1\wedge
 \frac{\gamma_\beta(y)m_\beta(x)}{\gamma_\beta(x)m_\beta(y)}.
 \label{eq:bf-is-mode-mh}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the accepted flux is
$Z_\beta^{-1}\min\{\gamma_\beta(x)m_\beta(y),
\gamma_\beta(y)m_\beta(x)\}$, which is symmetric. This establishes
invariance and the relative weighting of global moves without knowing
$Z_\beta$. Composing this kernel with any $p_\beta$-invariant local kernel
preserves $p_\beta$: $p_\beta K_{\rm local}K_{\rm global}=p_\beta$.
The composition need not itself be reversible.

The defensive component also gives a quantitative, although possibly weak,
protection. Since $m_\beta\ge\lambda r$ and
$p_\beta\le M^\beta r/Z_\beta$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_x\frac{p_\beta(x)}{m_\beta(x)}
 \le\frac{M^\beta}{\lambda Z_\beta},\qquad
 K_{\rm global}(x,A)\ge
 \frac{\lambda Z_\beta}{M^\beta}\,p_\beta(A).
 \label{eq:bf-is-defensive-mh-bound}
\end{equation}
The second inequality follows by bounding both ratios in the accepted flux.
It is a minorization for the frozen kernel, not a useful runtime guarantee
when $Z_\beta/M^\beta$ is tiny. Domination makes a mathematical argument
possible; it does not make the initial proposal accurate.

Optimizer points must enter through such a corrected proposal, rather than
being inserted into an SMC population with unit weights. If initialization
draws from a fitted mixture $m_0$ instead of $r$, its initial importance
weight is $r/m_0$. Proposals may be calibrated in pilot runs, but any subsequent
adaptation using the same evidence population requires its own justification.
Mixture weights, component widths and the safe share $\lambda$ are tuning
hypotheses to check, not universal constants supplied by the literature.

\subsection{Fit the IAF to the posterior weights}

For a weighted posterior population $(X_i,\bar W_i)$, the forward-KL
training objective and its empirical approximation are
\begin{align}
 D_{\rm KL}(p\Vert q_\phi)
   &=\E_p[\log p(X)]+\mathcal C(\phi),
   &\mathcal C(\phi)&=-\E_p[\log q_\phi(X)],\\
 \widehat{\mathcal C}(\phi)
   &=-\sum_i\bar W_i\log q_\phi(X_i),
   &\nabla_\phi\widehat{\mathcal C}
   &=-\sum_i\bar W_i\nabla_\phi\log q_\phi(X_i).
 \label{eq:bf-is-posterior-fkl}
\end{align}
The samples and weights are held fixed when differentiating. The inverse
$T_\phi^{-1}(X_i)$ still depends on $\phi$ and must be differentiated as in
\eqref{eq:bf-fab-inverse-derivative}. The IAF, its triangular Jacobian, its
canonical scale parameterization and the posterior evaluator can all remain
unchanged. Evaluating the inverse costs more than sampling this IAF; batched
inverse and gradient measurements must therefore precede a training budget.

These weights target $p$, which does not change when $\phi$ changes.
Consequently a frozen posterior dataset does not require FAB's
$q_{\rm old}/q_\phi$ replay correction. It does require an adequate posterior
sample in the first place. Repeated fitting can overfit a finite dependent
cloud; heldout validation should use independent sampler populations, with
lineage-aware uncertainty inside each population. If a weighted minibatch
draws indices with probabilities $s_i$, an unbiased gradient for the fixed
empirical objective uses coefficient $\bar W_i/s_i$ in its minibatch mean.
Sampling with $s_i=\bar W_i$ gives ordinary mean negative log density;
uniform sampling needs coefficient $N\bar W_i$.

Forward KL also has a less demanding integrability requirement here. For a
fixed finite IAF parameter vector, each ELU conditioner has at most linear
growth. Its complete log scale is bounded because its free bias is finite
and its conditional part is capped. The inverse recursion
$z_j=(u_j-\mu_j(z_{<j}))e^{-s_j(z_{<j})}$ then has at most linear growth by
induction on coordinates, and a finite composition preserves that property.
The log determinant is bounded. Thus there are finite map-dependent constants
such that
\begin{equation}
 |\log q_\phi(x)|\le C_\phi(1+\|x\|^2),\qquad
 \E_p\|X\|^2\le\frac{M}{Z}\E_r\|X\|^2<\infty.
 \label{eq:bf-is-fkl-finite}
\end{equation}
The cross-entropy is finite even when $\int p^2/q_\phi$ is infinite.
Moreover, $u|\log u|$ is bounded for $u\in[0,M]$, so
$\E_p|\log L|=Z^{-1}\int rL|\log L|<\infty$; together with the Gaussian
prior this makes $\E_p|\log p|$ finite as well. Hence the forward KL itself
is well-defined under these assumptions. This local derivation proves
existence, not low gradient variance, sufficient IAF capacity or successful
optimization. Constants may grow as parameters change.

To see why relative masses matter, partition the space into disjoint regions
$A_k$, with $a_k=p(A_k)>0$ and $p_k=p(\cdot\mid A_k)$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C(\phi)=-\sum_k a_k\E_{p_k}\log q_\phi(X),\qquad
 \widehat a_k=\sum_i\bar W_i\mathbf1_{A_k}(X_i).
 \label{eq:bf-is-region-fkl}
\end{equation}
An equal number of unweighted local samples from unequal-mass modes trains
the wrong mixture. If region $k$ is deliberately sampled with probability
$s_k$, its loss contribution needs multiplier $a_k/s_k$, or the corresponding
estimated weight with its uncertainty retained. Broad regions should include
an unassigned remainder; a discovered set of maxima need not exhaust the
posterior. Region counts and optimizer attraction frequencies cannot replace
the weighted mass estimates.

\subsection{What the other methods can contribute}

\citet[Equation 8, Algorithm 1 and Appendix G.1]{gabrie2022adaptiveflows}
combine forward-KL fitting with local moves and flow-based independence
Metropolis proposals. Their experiments explicitly distinguish three cases:
local chains trapped in separate modes do not rebalance their masses; chains
started only in one mode may never discover another; representative starts
in both modes allow the learned global proposals to rebalance them. This is
support for coupling sampling with learning, together with a direct warning
against making discovery depend solely on the initially narrow flow. Their
continuous-time analysis and finite experiments using unadjusted Langevin
steps do not establish exactness of an arbitrary adaptive implementation.
The corrected, frozen-kernel construction above states the finite-step
invariance argument needed here.

Parallel tempering provides a different way to transport information between
regions. Adjacent likelihood temperatures can exchange states with probability
\begin{equation}
 a_{\rm swap}=1\wedge
 \exp\{(\beta'-\beta)[\log L(x)-\log L(y)]\},
 \quad x\sim p_\beta,\ y\sim p_{\beta'}.
 \label{eq:bf-is-posterior-swap}
\end{equation}
The prior cancels in this ratio. Non-reversible even/odd exchange schedules
can improve temperature communication
\citep[Section 2.3 and assumptions A1--A3]{syed2022nrpt}; their analysis
requires stationarity and efficient local energy exploration. A replica can
make round trips without estimating all cold-mode probabilities correctly.
The existing tempered NeuTra ensemble remains a possible final estimator,
with cold-chain convergence and region-mass uncertainty assessed directly.

A defensive FAB variant is mathematically possible but must be described
accurately. Let $m_\phi=(1-\lambda)q_\phi+\lambda r$. Then
\begin{equation}
 J_\lambda(\phi)=\int\frac{(rL)^2}{m_\phi}\,dx
 \le\frac1\lambda\int rL^2\,dx\le\frac{M^2}{\lambda}.
 \label{eq:bf-is-defensive-fab}
\end{equation}
Under valid differentiation, with $g_\lambda=\gamma^2/(m_\phi J_\lambda)$,
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_\phi\log J_\lambda
 =-\E_{g_\lambda}\left[
 \frac{(1-\lambda)q_\phi(X)}{m_\phi(X)}
 \nabla_\phi\log q_\phi(X)\right].
 \label{eq:bf-is-defensive-fab-gradient}
\end{equation}
The mixture responsibility changes the gradient. Merely drawing from a
defensive proposal while keeping the original objective $\int\gamma^2/q_\phi$
does not repair its divergence. This mixture extension has not been tested
here and is not a claim to reproduce the author's Gaussian-flow recipe.

AFT or CRAFT would be a later option if ordinary SMC requires too many
intermediate steps or ineffective local moves. Their weights must include
the stage Jacobian in \eqref{eq:bf-is-aft-weight}; learned maps reduce
transport cost only if their fitting and inverse costs justify it. The
surveyed SCLD and continuous-flow methods address related exploration
problems, but adopting them would add a larger implementation and training
problem. The present evidence supports first investigating the ordinary
posterior sampling and forward-KL mechanism while preserving the IAF.

\subsection{Evidence needed before claiming coverage or an estimate}

The first experiment should establish a common, evaluable target, then test
whether independent posterior SMC populations agree on region masses and
the quantities ultimately to be estimated. Region definitions can use
discovered basins and scientifically interpretable sign regions, while
retaining an unassigned remainder. Genealogical survival, maximum weights,
incremental ESS, actual cross-region moves and temperature sensitivity explain
failures; none individually proves coverage. A single region-level discrepancy
can reject the present sample as a training reference without rejecting
posterior SMC as a research direction.

Even independent agreement has a limit. If a basin has prior probability
$a$, then $N$ independent prior starts miss it with probability
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(\hbox{no initial hit})=(1-a)^N.
 \label{eq:bf-is-missing-basin}
\end{equation}
Mutation can subsequently enter it, but resampling alone cannot. A basin
with tiny prior mass may have important posterior mass. Repeated populations,
broader discovery searches and sensitivity checks provide evidence against
particular omissions; they are not a finite proof that all modes were found.

Only after the sampler supplies usable weights should the canonical IAF be
trained and assessed on independent populations. Keep the standard
$1{,}000$-point normal-base score-residual test, but also evaluate
$Z_i=T_\phi^{-1}(X_i)$ for independent posterior samples and inspect their
weighted latent distribution and region-specific residuals. A map that
generates only one mode may look good on its own samples while transforming
the missing mode badly. Agreement of weighted posterior region masses with
fresh IAF draws tests coverage; transformed scores test geometry. Neither
replaces the other.

Finally freeze the map and tune the supported NeuTra kernel for the exact
transformed posterior in \eqref{eq:bf-fab-neutra-target}, using identity
latent mass. Plain NeuTra HMC or the existing tempered NeuTra ensemble can
supply the estimate; completing a comparison of every method is unnecessary.
Retained-chain convergence, Monte Carlo uncertainty, consistency across
dispersed starts and agreement with the independently assessed region masses
determine whether the estimate is usable. No such posterior estimate or
validated canonical forward-KL fit is supplied by this documentation study.
