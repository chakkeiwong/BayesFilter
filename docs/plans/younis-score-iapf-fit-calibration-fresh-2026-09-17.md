# Fresh underflow-safe iAPF fit calibration

This is a new executable phase of the score master program.  It follows the
closed 16 September calibration and the completed objective-underflow repair.
It has a new output root, fresh data partitions, a new attempt budget, and a
source snapshot that contains the guard.  The earlier calibration remains
holdout evidence and is not reused as a claim result.

## Research question and evidence contract

Does target-specific recursive iAPF density fitting produce an admissible,
finite analytical score on weakly and strongly curved scalar nonlinear models,
and do any admissible tuned controls survive comparison with the frozen-control
iAPF baseline and cheap conditional heuristics?

The computed quantity is the analytical derivative of the finite particle
log-likelihood program conditional on the fitted twist, realized particle
count, and realized resampling labels.  It is not an unbiased marginal-
likelihood score and passing a fit-validity check does not establish that
property.

The frozen-control baseline is the repaired recursive iAPF implementation with
the narrow first candidate from the previous specification (`k=1`, `tau=100`,
bounded mean and standard-deviation ranges).  The candidate family is selected
using calibration and validation streams only.  The selected candidate and the
frozen baseline are both evaluated on the same untouched claim datasets and
the same two independent final particle streams, with the density fit frozen
across those streams.  EKF, UKF, bootstrap, and local-linear proposals are
evaluated on each claim regime as conditional heuristic adversaries.

Promotion requires all of the following: every claim row has finite runtime,
valid fit diagnostics, one trace for each compiled kernel, verified GPU/XLA
and memory-growth evidence, complete weak and curved heuristic tables, and the
declared independent claim replicates.  A candidate with an objective
underflow, non-finite diagnostic, invalid covariance, or boundary/shape veto
is recorded and cannot supply a score comparison.  Any heuristic that has a
lower claim MSE is a promotion veto.  With two claim replicates and three
independent datasets per regime, all numerical differences remain descriptive;
no ranking or default change is issued without the later powered uncertainty
phase.

The phase must not conclude that iAPF is unbiased, that it improves the
marginal likelihood score, that a selected control is globally optimal, or
that a scalar result transfers to DSGE or other degenerate-transition models.

## Fresh partitions and candidate contract

The phase uses data identities not opened by the prior score studies:

| regime | calibration | validation | claim |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| weak curvature | 1100, 1102 | 1110, 1112 | 1120, 1122, 1124 |
| strong curvature | 1101, 1103 | 1111, 1113 | 1121, 1123, 1125 |

The extra calibration/validation identities provide two independent datasets
for control selection; the three claim identities are paired across all
methods.  They are generated by the repository's deterministic seed contract,
not copied from the old 1000/1001/1010/1011/1020/1021 rows.

Each regime tests three candidates total, including the frozen baseline:

1. `k=1, tau=100`, the frozen-control baseline;
2. `k=1, tau=25`, wider mean/scale bounds and tighter fitting tolerance;
3. `k=2, tau=10`, the wider candidate with the longer stopping window.

Here `k` is the Algorithm-4 stopping-window parameter: the sample coefficient
of variation uses the latest `k+1` normalizer estimates. It is not a count of
look-ahead mixture components.

The candidate family is a calibration hypothesis, not a universal default.
The fit uses FP64 offline arithmetic; the particle score uses the GPU FP32,
TF32, XLA route required by the repository policy.  All rows use the same
model parameters, horizon, particle count, reference construction, and source
fingerprint within a regime.

## Skeptical audit and default audit

The prior phase had the wrong validity admission because an exactly represented
zero density loss hid a positive normalized residual.  The repaired predicate
is now part of the source snapshot and is tested through the recursive fitter
and its adapter.  The old phase also omitted the curved heuristic table and the
claim-data frozen baseline; this plan makes both mandatory.

The primary comparator is the repaired frozen-control iAPF on the identical
claim streams, not the untuned candidate.  Heuristics are vetoes only and are
not tuning targets.  Candidate selection uses no claim rows.  Two final streams
are sufficient to check frozen-fit and independent-stream mechanics, but not
to support a statistical ranking; the plan explicitly records that limitation.
The scalar reference has a finite-grid approximation error, so reference
resolution and tail checks are recorded as diagnostics and must pass before
MSE is interpreted.

The nontrivial defaults have this provenance and early check:

| choice | provenance and failure mode | early check |
|---|---|---|
| `k`, `tau`, bounds | previous target-specific pilot; may overfit the scalar regimes | complete held-out calibration/validation table and boundary/shape diagnostics |
| FP64 fitting | numerical repair requirement; costs CPU time | finite fit diagnostics and exact underflow veto |
| GPU FP32/TF32/XLA | repository production target; may expose device-specific invalidity | runtime, trace, memory-growth, and finite-output checks |
| two claim streams and three datasets | mechanics/pilot budget, not power | inference table marks ranking unsupported |
| heuristic veto | owner research-question guard | conditional per-regime table |

The pre-mortem asks how a run could pass while misleading us: the reference
could be too coarse, the fixed fit could be shared accidentally, a candidate
could win only on one dataset, or invalid fits could be silently dropped.  The
driver therefore checks reference diagnostics, fit digests and stream seeds,
complete-case presence, and explicit invalid-row accounting before any
comparison is assembled.

## Required implementation and preflight

The executable driver is copied into the fresh source checkout and must use
the new plan path, fresh partitions, 280-attempt budget, repaired fit-validity
flag, explicit baseline claim rows, and both heuristic tables.  Before GPU
execution, run the underflow fixture, healthy exact-fit, recursive-adapter,
source-digest, and driver dry-run checks with CUDA hidden.  A preflight failure
must repair the implementation or stop the phase; it must not be bypassed by
loosening tolerances or deleting a negative fixture.

The phase closes with a result note that classifies the outcome as engineering
failure, numerical/fit veto, candidate failure, descriptive heuristic veto, or
viable-but-unpowered evidence.  The master matrix and checkpoint are refreshed
before any later covariance or fitted-moment integration phase is started.

The charged-attempt budget is derived before execution rather than inferred
from a nominal row count.  The three-candidate source studies contain 24 iAPF
rows (three candidates across four calibration/validation datasets in each of
the two regimes), and the baseline-only source studies contain eight more
rows.  The two claim studies contain 24 iAPF rows (selected and frozen-control
methods, three claim datasets and two final streams in each regime).  The
conditional heuristic studies contain 24 non-iAPF rows.  Charging every iAPF
row at its conservative four-unit upper bound and reserving 32 units for
diagnostic/reporting rows gives a ceiling of 280.  A failed source study blocks
its repository-issued selection artifact; it is preserved as evidence and
does not authorize silently substituting an unselected claim configuration.
