\chapter{CIP/AFNS Case Study}
\label{ch:bf-cip-afns-case-study}

The CIP/AFNS material is useful to BayesFilter because it is a large structured
linear Gaussian application with economically meaningful observation equations,
missing-data pressure, and analytic derivative requirements.  The economics
belongs in the source CIP monograph.  BayesFilter should import only the
filtering lessons.

\section{Filtering Lessons}
\label{sec:bf-cip-filtering-lessons}

The reusable lessons are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item affine or linearized macro-finance measurement systems can become large
    LGSSMs with structured observation matrices;
  \item yield-curve blocks create dense measurement panels with heterogeneous
    maturities and noise scales;
  \item missing observations must be represented by explicit masks, not by
    silent imputation or dynamic shape changes;
  \item analytic derivatives and backend parity matter because HMC mass
    matrices and MAP curvature depend on the same likelihood being sampled.
\end{itemize}

\section{BayesFilter Boundary}
\label{sec:bf-cip-boundary}

BayesFilter should not re-derive AFNS economics, currency-basis theory, or
asset-pricing restrictions in this monograph.  It should define the state-space
objects that an application supplies:
\[
  F_\theta,\quad Q_\theta,\quad H_\theta,\quad R_\theta,\quad
  m_{0|0,\theta},\quad P_{0|0,\theta},
\]
and then validate that the filter, derivative provider, masks, and HMC
diagnostics behave as promised.  This boundary keeps BayesFilter reusable and
prevents application monographs from becoming maintenance forks of the
filtering code.

\section{Readiness Use}
\label{sec:bf-cip-readiness-use}

CIP/AFNS should be used as a large LGSSM readiness case once the BayesFilter
package exists.  The required evidence is backend parity, missing-data policy,
finite analytic gradients and Hessians, MAP/mass-matrix diagnostics, and short
HMC plumbing checks.  It is not the right first target for SVD sigma-point HMC
claims, because its main reusable strength is exact or controlled linear
Gaussian filtering.

\section{DZ5 Fixed-Center Curvature Evidence}
\label{sec:bf-dz5-curvature-evidence}

A 2026-07-16 MacroFinance diagnostic shows why score stationarity and curvature
stability must be separated.  The foreign-binding center had prior-scaled score
$0.970879$, yet at radius $0.015625$ three independent dense fits had positive
raw curvature, negligible SPD projection, independent holdout relative error
below $0.007$, and trace-normalized differences near $0.1\%$.  Small
$\pm0.01$ prior-standard-deviation center shifts preserved the geometry.  This
nominates a separate exact HMC mechanics canary; it does not establish a MAP or
convergence.

The both-binding center had the smaller score $0.094590$ but failed
reproducibility.  Raw nonpositive counts were $(2,2,1)$, projected generalized-
eigenvalue spreads were approximately $4.3\times10^{11}$ to
$1.7\times10^{12}$, and positive-subspace principal-angle disagreement reached
about $70.6$ degrees.  Its projected dense matrix is blocked for HMC use;
projection did not repair weak-direction uncertainty.

These are deterministic local diagnostics on one preserved center per regime
and three fit seeds.  They support the score-versus-curvature correction and
the structured and consensus repair.  They do not establish posterior validity,
identification, recovery, HMC behavior, or default readiness.  Applying the new
BayesFilter fixed-center API to the both-binding target and running exact HMC
remain separate reviewed experiments.
