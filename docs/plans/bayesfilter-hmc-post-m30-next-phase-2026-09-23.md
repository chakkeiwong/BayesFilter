## Question and evidence contract

Can a statistically justified validation design answer an outstanding
full-procedure question within the remaining allowance? M30 tests execution
equivalence and cost. Its replay calls do not add independent fits to coverage
or defect-power denominators. A cheaper fit alone cannot close either gap.

Keep two distinct questions. The stopped-interval study asks whether complete
fresh fits deliver their declared posterior checks and whether each declared
mean/median interval covers analytic truth. The existing screen requires a
pointwise two-sided 95% exact binomial lower endpoint of at least .90 for every
declared quantity, with missing fits counted as failures. Its N=384 planning
inventory comes from M26's operating-characteristic calculation, not from a
universal sample-size rule. The full-fit defect study asks whether a specified
test detects a declared implementation error while controlling null rejection.
Its unit is the entire statistical experiment; a dataset, candidate sibling,
profile replay, and repeated experiment are different denominators.

The baseline is the M26 count/member policy, M29 designs, M30's exact same-source
static/dynamic pairs, and the saved M25 controlled-defect costs. Keep M22's
frozen-Gaussian null result closed for its tested cell. Do not rerun it or
claim that it calibrates complete preparation, tuning and stopping.

output, hiding stopping bias with a wider interval, counting siblings as fits,
and using observed favorable p-values to choose test quantities or replication
counts. The mathematical audit and frozen denominator precede HMC launches to
expose these errors early. A failed candidate motivates the declared next
repair; invalid inference or exhausted allowance blocks that comparison.
