\chapter{UKF-Guided Particle Proposals and Covariance-Preserving Resets}
\label{ch:bf-ukf-covariance-proposal}
\chaptermark{UKF Proposals and Covariance Resets}

Chapter~\ref{ch:bf-ledh-pfpf-ot-custom-gradient} requires the reset to preserve
importance-weighted moments and the score to differentiate the same finite
filtering computation. We now examine how a UKF covariance can guide the
proposal while respecting those requirements. The distinction is between
uncertainty conditional on one ancestor, uncertainty across the predictive
mixture, and uncertainty represented by the importance-weighted posterior.
Each covariance has a different role in the algorithm.

The construction below is the covariance-guided proposal route evaluated in the
five-model surrogate-HMC campaign. It combines a common UKF guide, conditional
local flows, a marginal importance correction, a Cholesky reset that preserves
the first two weighted moments under its stated assumptions, and an optional
higher-moment correction. The original three-branch proposal is the baseline
for the observation-conditioned extension developed here. In that extension,
observation-weighted parents are the default, while uniform parents remain a
competitive alternative. Chapter~\ref{ch:bf-dpf-observation-parent-failures}
compares likelihood, score, and ESS across models, including cases where the
weighted-parent score is less accurate. Those results support the default as a
weight-balance choice; they do not establish uniformly better score accuracy
or HMC suitability.

The shared filtering recurrence is implemented in
\path{make_batched_score_filter} in
\path{bayesfilter/highdim/covariance_proposal_score_batch_tf.py}. The value and
analytical total score differentiate the same finite filtering computation.
Chapter~\ref{ch:bf-surrogate-hmc-five-models} separately reports the endpoint,
Metropolis correction, and posterior diagnostics. The deterministic
structural-completion route uses an innovation-space proposal and currently
selects parents uniformly; the weighted-parent comparison does not cover that
route. Particle matrices in this chapter have particles as columns,
\(X\in\R^{d\times N}\), transposing the row-particle convention in the
preceding chapter without changing the covariance or derivative identities.
