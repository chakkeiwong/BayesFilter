\chapter[Neural-Force HMC]{Metropolis-Corrected Neural-Force Hamiltonian Monte Carlo}
\label{ch:bf-hnn-surrogate-hmc}

This chapter studies a tempting acceleration of Hamiltonian Monte Carlo for
state-space models.  A filtering likelihood and its score may require one
complete recursion through the observations.  Ordinary HMC calls that score at
every leapfrog kick.  A learned force can make the interior trajectory cheap;
one then evaluates the declared filtering posterior at the proposed endpoint
and applies a Metropolis correction.

That summary is attractive but incomplete.  A true endpoint posterior value
does not correct an arbitrary neural proposal.  The simple HMC acceptance
formula is valid only after the proposal map has the required discrete
reversibility and volume properties, and its endpoint energy must include the
final momentum.  A stochastic filtering likelihood adds a separate target
problem: a fresh particle-filter estimate is not a deterministic potential and
cannot be inserted naively into that formula.

The chapter therefore does four things.  It derives a sufficient construction
for a frozen learned force, proves its invariant target, identifies precisely
why broader learned-Hamiltonian constructions need additional proof or
correction, states the filtering boundary for every relevant target class, and
ends with an implementation and validation contract for BayesFilter.  The
presentation is self-contained.  The proposal of
\citet{dhulipala2022latent} and its official implementation motivate the audit,
but no theorem below depends on accepting that paper's stationarity argument.

\section{The computational question}
\label{sec:bf-hnn-question}

Let $u\in\R^d$ be unconstrained sampler coordinates and let $y_{1:T}$ denote
the observations.  For a deterministic filtering backend $F$, define
\begin{equation}
  \ell_F(u)=\log p(u)+\log L_F(y_{1:T}\mid u)+J(u),
  \qquad U_F(u)=-\ell_F(u),
  \label{eq:bf-hnn-filter-potential}
\end{equation}
where $J$ contains every parameter-transform log Jacobian.  The subscript $F$
is essential.  If $F$ is a Kalman filter, $L_F$ may be the exact likelihood of
the declared linear Gaussian model.  If $F$ is a UKF or fixed SGQF,
$L_F$ is normally an approximate filtering likelihood.  In either case,
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-filter-potential} is a deterministic scalar once the model,
data, numerical branch, and filter settings are frozen.

With mass matrix $M\succ0$, ordinary HMC uses
\begin{equation}
 H_F(u,p)=U_F(u)+K(p),\qquad
 K(p)=\tfrac12p^\top M^{-1}p,
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-true-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
and repeatedly evaluates $\nabla U_F$.  Write $C_V$ for one new evaluation of
$U_F$, $C_G$ for one evaluation of $\nabla U_F$ together with whatever value
the implementation returns, and $C_S$ for one learned-force call.  For $L$
leapfrog steps, the leading proposal costs are approximately
\begin{align}
 C_{\rm ordinary}&\simeq L C_G,\label{eq:bf-hnn-cost-ordinary}\\
 C_{\rm neural}&\simeq L C_S+C_V.\label{eq:bf-hnn-cost-neural}
\end{align}
The current state's true value can be cached, so the corrected neural proposal
usually needs only one new endpoint value.  Equations
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-cost-ordinary}--\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-cost-neural} are accounting
identities, not speedup theorems: acceptance, autocorrelation, fallback,
training, compilation, batching, and device utilization determine actual ESS
per second.

\begin{proposition}[When a neural-force proposal can reduce filtering work]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-cost-condition}
Suppose ordinary and neural-force kernels use the same number $L$ of interior
steps.  Ignore one-time compilation and training, and suppose each kernel
produces the same effective sample size per accepted transition.  Then the
neural-force kernel has lower leading cost per transition exactly when
\begin{equation}
  L C_S+C_V<L C_G.
  \label{eq:bf-hnn-cost-condition}
\end{equation}
If training costs $C_{\rm train}$ and the per-transition saving is
$\Delta C=L C_G-(L C_S+C_V)>0$, at least
$C_{\rm train}/\Delta C$ transitions are required to amortize training under
these assumptions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Subtract \eqref{eq:bf-hnn-cost-neural} from
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-cost-ordinary}.  The neural route is cheaper precisely when
the difference $\Delta C$ is positive.  After $N$ transitions the accumulated
saving is $N\Delta C$; it covers training if and only if
$N\Delta C\ge C_{\rm train}$.
\end{proof}

The equal-efficiency premise in Proposition~\ref{prop:bf-hnn-cost-condition}
is deliberately strong.  A useful experiment must instead compare ESS per
wall-clock second after charging training and fallback.  The proposition only
explains why expensive filtering scores make the method worth investigating.

\section{Phase-space Metropolis correction}
\label{sec:bf-hnn-phase-space-mh}

The target of one HMC transition is the joint density
\begin{equation}
 \rho_F(u,p)=Z_H^{-1}\exp\{-H_F(u,p)\}.
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-joint-target}
\end{equation}
Marginalizing $p$ recovers the posterior proportional to $e^{-U_F(u)}$.
Before each transition, momentum is refreshed from $\calN(0,M)$.  Proposal
validity is easiest to state for a deterministic phase-space map.

Let $R(u,p)=(u,-p)$ be the momentum flip.  Let $\Phi$ be a differentiable
bijection of phase space and define the proposal map $F=R\circ\Phi$.  The
following proposition gives the exact conditions behind the familiar HMC
acceptance probability.

\begin{proposition}[Deterministic involutive Metropolis proposal]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-deterministic-mh}
Assume $F:\R^{2d}\to\R^{2d}$ is a differentiable involution,
$F\circ F=I$, and $|\det DF(x)|=1$ for every $x$.  From state $x=(u,p)$,
propose $x'=F(x)$ and accept with
\begin{equation}
 \alpha(x)=\min\{1,\rho_F(x')/\rho_F(x)\}
 =\min\{1,\exp[-H_F(x')+H_F(x)]\}.
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-true-energy-acceptance}
\end{equation}
Then the accept/reject kernel is reversible with respect to $\rho_F$ and hence
leaves $\rho_F$ invariant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any measurable sets $A,B$, the accepted flow from $A$ to $B$ is
\begin{equation}
 \int_A \rho_F(x)\alpha(x)\mathbf1\{F(x)\in B\}\,dx
 =\int_A\min\{\rho_F(x),\rho_F(Fx)\}
   \mathbf1\{F(x)\in B\}\,dx.
\end{equation}
Change variables $y=F(x)$.  Involution gives $x=F(y)$ and the unit-Jacobian
assumption gives $dx=dy$.  The expression becomes
\begin{equation}
 \int_B\min\{\rho_F(Fy),\rho_F(y)\}
   \mathbf1\{F(y)\in A\}\,dy,
\end{equation}
which is the accepted flow from $B$ to $A$.  Rejections remain at the same
state and are automatically balanced.  Thus detailed balance holds.  Taking
$A$ to be the full state space proves invariance.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Terms in the endpoint correction]
Equation \eqref{eq:bf-hnn-true-energy-acceptance} contains
$K(p')-K(p)$ as well as $U_F(u')-U_F(u)$.  A correction based only on
$\ell_F(u')-\ell_F(u)$ is generally wrong.  Momentum cancels only for a
proposal that preserves kinetic energy exactly, which ordinary leapfrog does
not guarantee.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Omitting endpoint kinetic energy is wrong in general]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-kinetic-required}
There exist volume-preserving involutive proposals for which the
position-only acceptance
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_U(x)=\min\{1,\exp[-U_F(u')+U_F(u)]\}
 \end{equation}
does not leave \eqref{eq:bf-hnn-joint-target} invariant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $d=1$, $U_F(u)=u^2/2$, $K(p)=p^2/2$, and the involution
$F(u,p)=(p,u)$.  Its Jacobian determinant is $-1$.  Because
$H_F(Fx)=H_F(x)$, the correct acceptance is one.  The position-only rule is
$\min\{1,\exp[-p^2/2+u^2/2]\}$ and rejects a set of positive Gaussian measure,
for example whenever $|p|>|u|$.  The reverse move from $(p,u)$ uses the
reciprocal position ratio.  The accepted forward mass is proportional to
\begin{equation}
 e^{-(u^2+p^2)/2}\min\{1,e^{(u^2-p^2)/2}\},
\end{equation}
while the accepted reverse mass is proportional to
\begin{equation}
 e^{-(u^2+p^2)/2}\min\{1,e^{(p^2-u^2)/2}\}.
\end{equation}
They differ whenever $|u|\ne|p|$, so detailed balance fails.  The missing
kinetic term is therefore not optional.
\end{proof}

\section{A sufficient learned-force construction}
\label{sec:bf-hnn-safe-construction}

The cleanest construction learns only a position potential
$\widehat U_\phi(u)$.  Its force
\begin{equation}
 g_\phi(u)=\nabla\widehat U_\phi(u)
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-learned-force}
\end{equation}
may be inaccurate.  It is frozen before warmup used for inference and remains
fixed throughout the retained chain.  Define a kick, drift, and one symmetric
step by
\begin{align}
 K_{a}(u,p)&=(u,p-a g_\phi(u)),\label{eq:bf-hnn-kick}\\
 D_b(u,p)&=(u+bM^{-1}p,p),\label{eq:bf-hnn-drift}\\
 \Psi_\epsilon&=K_{\epsilon/2}\circ D_\epsilon
                 \circ K_{\epsilon/2}.\label{eq:bf-hnn-kdk}
\end{align}
The rightmost map acts first.

\begin{lemma}[Kick and drift are volume-preserving]
\label{lem:bf-hnn-shear-volume}
If $g_\phi$ is continuously differentiable, both maps in
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-kick}--\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-drift} have Jacobian determinant one.
Consequently, $\Psi_\epsilon$ and every finite composition of its steps are
volume-preserving.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Their Jacobians have block form
\begin{equation}
 DK_a(u,p)=
 \begin{pmatrix}I&0\\-aDg_\phi(u)&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 DD_b(u,p)=
 \begin{pmatrix}I&bM^{-1}\\0&I\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Both are block triangular with identity diagonal blocks, hence determinant
one.  Determinants multiply under composition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Momentum-flip reversibility]
\label{lem:bf-hnn-kdk-reversible}
For the momentum flip $R(u,p)=(u,-p)$,
\begin{equation}
 R\circ\Psi_\epsilon\circ R=\Psi_\epsilon^{-1}=\Psi_{-\epsilon}.
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-kdk-reversibility}
\end{equation}
The same identity holds for $\Phi_{\epsilon,L}=\Psi_\epsilon^L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct substitution gives
$R K_a R=K_{-a}=K_a^{-1}$ and
$R D_b R=D_{-b}=D_b^{-1}$.  Therefore
\begin{align}
 R\Psi_\epsilon R
 &=K_{-\epsilon/2}\circ D_{-\epsilon}
   \circ K_{-\epsilon/2}\\
 &=\left(K_{\epsilon/2}\circ D_\epsilon
   \circ K_{\epsilon/2}\right)^{-1}.
\end{align}
For $L$ steps,
$R\Psi_\epsilon^L R=(R\Psi_\epsilon R)^L
=(\Psi_\epsilon^{-1})^L=(\Psi_\epsilon^L)^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact invariance for a frozen learned position force]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-safe-exactness}
Let $\Phi_{\epsilon,L}=\Psi_\epsilon^L$ use any fixed continuously
differentiable $g_\phi:\R^d\to\R^d$ in the symmetric construction above.
The force need not equal $\nabla U_F$.  Define
$F=R\circ\Phi_{\epsilon,L}$ and accept $F(u,p)$ with the true joint-energy
probability \eqref{eq:bf-hnn-true-energy-acceptance}.  Then the transition
leaves \eqref{eq:bf-hnn-joint-target} invariant.  After momentum refresh, its
position marginal leaves the deterministic filter-defined posterior
proportional to $e^{-U_F(u)}$ invariant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:bf-hnn-shear-volume} and the definition of $R$ give
\begin{equation}
 |\det D\Phi_{\epsilon,L}|=1,
 \qquad
 |\det DR|=1.
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:bf-hnn-kdk-reversible},
\begin{align}
 F^2
 &=R\Phi_{\epsilon,L}R\Phi_{\epsilon,L}
 =\Phi_{\epsilon,L}^{-1}\Phi_{\epsilon,L}=I.
\end{align}
Proposition~\ref{prop:bf-hnn-deterministic-mh} therefore proves joint
invariance.  Momentum refresh draws from the conditional momentum factor of
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-joint-target} and also preserves the joint density.
Marginalization over $p$ gives the stated posterior invariance.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Conservative learning is useful but not required for this proof]
Writing $g_\phi=\nabla\widehat U_\phi$ guarantees a conservative learned force
and permits scalar value/gradient training.  The shear and reversibility proof
above actually needs only a frozen position-only $g_\phi$.  The stronger
potential representation remains preferable because it constrains learning,
supports energy diagnostics, and prevents accidental momentum dependence.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Surrogate error affects efficiency, not this invariant target]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-error-efficiency}
Under Proposition~\ref{prop:bf-hnn-safe-exactness}, replacing $g_\phi$ by any
other fixed finite position-only force does not change the invariant target.
However, if the resulting proposal has large true energy error
$\Delta H_F=H_F(Fx)-H_F(x)$ on typical states, its acceptance is small for
positive $\Delta H_F$, so it can have arbitrarily poor efficiency.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The invariance proof uses only the map properties, not equality between
$g_\phi$ and $\nabla U_F$.  Acceptance is
$\min\{1,e^{-\Delta H_F}\}$.  For any $B>0$, states with
$\Delta H_F\ge B$ have acceptance at most $e^{-B}$.  Thus invariance does not
bound mixing away from zero.
\end{proof}

\section{Limits of the broader learned-Hamiltonian argument}
\label{sec:bf-hnn-paper-audit}

The method in \citet{dhulipala2022latent} learns a scalar Hamiltonian from both
position and momentum, obtains its vector field by differentiation, advances
that field using a synchronized-leapfrog formula, and applies a true endpoint
Hamiltonian test.  Its Sections~3.2--3.3 and Algorithms~3.2--3.3 are the
relevant source anchors.  The paper appeals to Liouville's theorem and observed
forward/reverse trajectories.  Those facts do not by themselves prove the
discrete proposal required by Proposition~\ref{prop:bf-hnn-deterministic-mh}.

The issue is mathematical, not terminological.  There are three different
claims:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item a Hamiltonian vector field has zero divergence and its exact flow
 preserves phase volume;
 \item an exact Hamiltonian flow is reversible under momentum flip only when
 the Hamiltonian has the appropriate momentum symmetry;
 \item a numerical map must itself be volume-preserving and reversible, or its
 actual proposal/Jacobian terms must enter MH.
\end{enumerate}
None of these claims implies the next without additional assumptions.

\begin{proposition}[Liouville volume preservation does not prove time reversal]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-liouville-not-reversibility}
There are smooth Hamiltonians whose exact flows preserve phase volume but are
not reversible under $R(u,p)=(u,-p)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take one dimension and
\begin{equation}
 \widehat H(u,p)=\tfrac12u^2+\tfrac12p^2+cp,
 \qquad c\ne0.
\end{equation}
Hamilton's equations are $\dot u=p+c$ and $\dot p=-u$.  Their vector field has
divergence
$\partial_u(p+c)+\partial_p(-u)=0$, so Liouville's formula gives unit volume.
But $\widehat H(u,-p)=\widehat H(u,p)-2cp$, so the Hamiltonian is not even in
momentum.  If $(u(t),p(t))$ is a solution, the putative reversed path
$(u(-t),-p(-t))$ has first derivative
$-\dot u(-t)=-(p(-t)+c)$, whereas the equation evaluated on the reversed state
requires $-p(-t)+c$.  They differ by $2c$.  Momentum-flip reversibility fails
although volume is preserved.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Momentum evenness implies exact-flow reversibility]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-even-momentum}
If a smooth Hamiltonian satisfies
$\widehat H(u,-p)=\widehat H(u,p)$, then, for every $t$ for which its exact
flow $\varphi_t$ is defined,
\begin{equation}
 R\varphi_tR=\varphi_{-t}=\varphi_t^{-1}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Evenness in $p$ makes $\nabla_p\widehat H(u,p)$ odd in $p$ and
$\nabla_u\widehat H(u,p)$ even in $p$.  If
$z(t)=(u(t),p(t))$ solves Hamilton's equations, define
$\widetilde z(t)=Rz(-t)=(u(-t),-p(-t))$.  Differentiating and applying those
parities shows
\begin{align}
 \dot{\widetilde u}(t)
 &=-\nabla_p\widehat H(u(-t),p(-t))
 =\nabla_p\widehat H(\widetilde u(t),\widetilde p(t)),\\
 \dot{\widetilde p}(t)
 &=\dot p(-t)
 =-\nabla_u\widehat H(\widetilde u(t),\widetilde p(t)).
\end{align}
Thus $\widetilde z$ is the Hamiltonian solution starting from $Rz(0)$.
Uniqueness of the ODE solution yields the identity.
\end{proof}

The latent-HNN architecture in the cited paper takes $(u,p)$ jointly as input
and does not impose exact evenness in $p$.  Consequently,
Proposition~\ref{prop:bf-hnn-even-momentum} is unavailable without an added
architectural constraint or a different reversal operation.

There is also an integrator mismatch.  The position-Verlet formula used in the
paper's Algorithm~3.2 is derived for a separable Hamiltonian
$\widehat U(u)+K(p)$ with known quadratic $K$.  If a learned
$\widehat H(u,p)$ has general momentum dependence, then
$\dot u=\nabla_p\widehat H(u,p)$ need not equal $M^{-1}p$.  In the paper's
notation, the executed position update is
\begin{equation}
 u_i(t+\epsilon)=u_i(t)+\frac{\epsilon}{m_i}p_i(t)
 -\frac{\epsilon^2}{2m_i}
  \frac{\partial\widehat H}{\partial u_i}(u(t),p(t)).
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-paper-position-update}
\end{equation}
Its leading velocity is $p_i/m_i$, not the general learned velocity
$\partial\widehat H/\partial p_i$.  Unless
$\nabla_p\widehat H(u,p)=M^{-1}p$, the executed update is not an integrator of
the learned Hamiltonian vector field it claims to approximate.  A label such as
``synchronized leapfrog'' does not prove that the resulting discrete map is
symplectic or reversible for that general learned Hamiltonian.

\begin{proposition}[The ordinary energy ratio is incomplete for a general map]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-general-map-jacobian}
Let $F$ be a differentiable involution but do not assume it preserves volume.
The deterministic Metropolis acceptance that balances $\rho_F$ is
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_J(x)=\min\left\{1,
 \frac{\rho_F(Fx)|\det DF(x)|}{\rho_F(x)}\right\}.
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-jacobian-acceptance}
\end{equation}
Thus the energy-only ratio
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-true-energy-acceptance} is justified only when the absolute
Jacobian determinant is one.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The accepted mass density sent from $x$ to $y=F(x)$ is
$\rho_F(x)\alpha_J(x)dx$.  Since $dy=|\det DF(x)|dx$, balance with the reverse
accepted mass requires
\begin{equation}
 \rho_F(x)\alpha_J(x)
 =\rho_F(Fx)\alpha_J(Fx)|\det DF(x)|.
\end{equation}
Involution implies
$|\det DF(Fx)|=|\det DF(x)|^{-1}$.  Substituting
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-jacobian-acceptance} on both sides gives the same minimum of
$\rho_F(x)$ and $\rho_F(Fx)|\det DF(x)|$, proving balance.  Removing the
Jacobian is valid only when it equals one.
\end{proof}

For a proposal that is neither an involution nor otherwise supplied with a
tractable reverse proposal density, even
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-jacobian-acceptance} is not enough.  One must construct an
involutive extension, randomize over forward/reverse directions with the right
law, or use the full Hastings ratio.  Therefore ``evaluate the true posterior
at the endpoint'' is not a general correctness theorem.

\section{True-energy and delayed-acceptance kernels}
\label{sec:bf-hnn-delayed-acceptance}

Two valid algorithms are often conflated.

\paragraph{One-stage true-energy correction.}
Generate the deterministic learned-force proposal of
Section~\ref{sec:bf-hnn-safe-construction} and apply only
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-true-energy-acceptance}.  This is the preferred first
BayesFilter implementation because its proof is direct.

\paragraph{Two-stage delayed acceptance.}
Let $\widehat\rho(x)\propto e^{-\widehat H(x)}$ be a cheap surrogate joint
density, and let $q(y\mid x)$ be a proposal kernel that is reversible with
respect to $\widehat\rho$.  Stage one accepts the proposal using the surrogate
kernel; conditional on that acceptance, stage two accepts with
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_2(x,y)=\min\left\{1,
 \frac{\rho_F(y)\widehat\rho(x)}
      {\rho_F(x)\widehat\rho(y)}\right\}.
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-stage2-da}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Two-stage delayed-acceptance balance]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-da-balance}
Suppose the stage-one accepted proposal kernel satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\rho(x)q(y\mid x)\alpha_1(x,y)
 =\widehat\rho(y)q(x\mid y)\alpha_1(y,x).
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-stage1-balance}
\end{equation}
Then accepting a stage-one move with
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-stage2-da} yields a kernel reversible with respect to
$\rho_F$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $x\ne y$, multiply the forward accepted flow by its reverse counterpart's
denominator:
\begin{align}
 &\rho_F(x)q(y\mid x)\alpha_1(x,y)
 \min\left\{1,
 \frac{\rho_F(y)\widehat\rho(x)}
      {\rho_F(x)\widehat\rho(y)}\right\}\\
 &=q(y\mid x)\alpha_1(x,y)
 \min\left\{\rho_F(x),
 \frac{\rho_F(y)\widehat\rho(x)}{\widehat\rho(y)}\right\}.
\end{align}
Use \eqref{eq:bf-hnn-stage1-balance} to replace
$q(y\mid x)\alpha_1(x,y)$ by
$\widehat\rho(y)q(x\mid y)\alpha_1(y,x)/\widehat\rho(x)$.  The result is
\begin{equation}
 q(x\mid y)\alpha_1(y,x)
 \min\left\{
 \frac{\rho_F(x)\widehat\rho(y)}{\widehat\rho(x)},
 \rho_F(y)\right\},
\end{equation}
which is the reverse accepted flow.  Rejections balance on the diagonal.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A stage-two ratio cannot replace a missing stage one]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-missing-stage1}
The correction \eqref{eq:bf-hnn-stage2-da} alone does not generally target
$\rho_F$ when the proposal kernel is not already reversible with respect to
$\widehat\rho$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $\rho_F=\widehat\rho$ on a three-state space
$\{0,1,2\}$.  Then \eqref{eq:bf-hnn-stage2-da} equals one for every move.
Let the proposal deterministically cycle $0\mapsto1\mapsto2\mapsto0$.
For a nonuniform $\rho_F$, for example masses $(1/2,1/3,1/6)$, the resulting
chain merely rotates those masses and does not preserve $\rho_F$.  Thus the
stage-two ratio cannot repair an arbitrary proposal.  The missing premise is
exactly the stage-one balance identity
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-stage1-balance}.
\end{proof}

This proposition identifies the local historical implementation problem.  In
the audited earlier \code{dsge\_hmc.estimation.DelayedAcceptanceHMC}, the
executed logic is schematically
\begin{align}
 y,\widehat\alpha_1&\leftarrow
   \operatorname{surrogate\_leapfrog}(x),\\
 \operatorname{adapt}(\widehat\alpha_1)&\quad\text{during warmup},\\
 \alpha_{\rm executed}(x,y)&=
 \min\left\{1,
 \frac{\rho_F(y)\widehat\rho(x)}
      {\rho_F(x)\widehat\rho(y)}\right\},
 \end{align}
with no Bernoulli accept/reject using $\widehat\alpha_1$ between the first and
third lines.  Thus the surrogate statistic affects adaptation but is not the
stage-one kernel assumed by \eqref{eq:bf-hnn-stage1-balance}.  The
documentation claims the two-stage theorem while the executed kernel omits a
premise of Proposition~\ref{prop:bf-hnn-da-balance}.  The implementation is
therefore wrong relative to that exact-posterior claim.  The clean repair is
either the one-stage true joint-energy kernel of
Proposition~\ref{prop:bf-hnn-safe-exactness}, or a complete two-stage kernel
that retains both endpoint momenta and performs both acceptances.

\section{The filtering boundary}
\label{sec:bf-hnn-filtering-boundary}

The proposal proof and the target definition are separate obligations.  Even a
perfect proposal kernel is exact only for the scalar placed in its MH ratio.
This section states that boundary precisely.

Let the latent state-space model be
\begin{align}
 x_0&\sim\mu_u,\\
 x_t\mid x_{t-1}&\sim f_u(\cdot\mid x_{t-1}),\\
 y_t\mid x_t&\sim g_u(\cdot\mid x_t),
\end{align}
with exact likelihood
\begin{equation}
 L(u)=\int \mu_u(dx_0)
 \prod_{t=1}^T f_u(dx_t\mid x_{t-1})g_u(y_t\mid x_t).
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-exact-ssm-likelihood}
\end{equation}
The exact unconstrained posterior is
\begin{equation}
 \pi(u\mid y)\propto p(u)L(u)e^{J(u)}.
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-exact-posterior}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Deterministic exact and approximate filters}

Let a deterministic filter $F$ return a positive scalar $L_F(u)$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \pi_F(u\mid y)\propto p(u)L_F(u)e^{J(u)}.
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-filter-posterior}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exactness relative to the declared deterministic filter]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-filter-relative-exactness}
If the endpoint correction in
Proposition~\ref{prop:bf-hnn-safe-exactness} evaluates
$U_F(u)=-\log p(u)-\log L_F(u)-J(u)$ deterministically, the kernel leaves
$\pi_F$ invariant.  It leaves the exact posterior
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-exact-posterior} invariant by this argument only if
$L_F(u)=cL(u)$ almost everywhere for a constant $c>0$ independent of $u$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first claim is Proposition~\ref{prop:bf-hnn-safe-exactness} with the named
potential.  The normalized densities $\pi_F$ and $\pi$ are equal if their
unnormalized likelihood factors differ by a positive $u$-independent constant.
Conversely, if the priors and charts agree and the two normalized posterior
densities are equal wherever the prior is positive, then their likelihood
ratio is the ratio of their normalizing constants and is therefore constant
almost everywhere.
\end{proof}

Thus a Kalman filter for the declared linear Gaussian model can supply the
exact $L$.  A UKF, SGQF, assumed-density filter, deterministic particle flow,
or fixed low-rank filter ordinarily supplies a named $L_F\ne L$.  Corrected
neural-force HMC can be exact for that approximate-filter posterior, not for
the latent model posterior.  The MH step corrects the trajectory surrogate; it
does not correct the filtering approximation.

\subsection{Fixed randomness and relaxed filters}

Suppose a randomized program returns
$\widehat L(u;\omega)$ for random numbers $\omega$.  Freezing one realization
$\omega_0$ makes
\begin{equation}
 \pi_{\omega_0}(u)\propto p(u)\widehat L(u;\omega_0)e^{J(u)}
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-fixed-randomness-target}
\end{equation}
a deterministic target, provided the executed branch is well defined.

\begin{proposition}[What frozen randomness does and does not prove]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-frozen-randomness}
Under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:bf-hnn-safe-exactness}, using the
same $\omega_0$ for every endpoint value can produce a valid HMC kernel for
$\pi_{\omega_0}$.  Unbiasedness
$\E_\omega[\widehat L(u;\omega)]=L(u)$ does not imply
$\pi_{\omega_0}=\pi$ for any fixed realization, nor does it imply
$\E[\log\widehat L]=\log L$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $\omega_0$ frozen, the previous deterministic-target proof applies to
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-fixed-randomness-target}.  For the nonimplications, take
$\omega\sim\operatorname{Unif}[-1,1]$ and
\begin{equation}
 \widehat L(u;\omega)=L(u)\{1+a(u)\omega\},
 \qquad |a(u)|<1,
\end{equation}
where $a(u)$ is nonconstant.  The estimator is positive and unbiased because
$\E\omega=0$.  For any fixed $\omega_0\ne0$, its realized ratio to $L(u)$ is
$1+a(u)\omega_0$, which is nonconstant, so the realized posterior generally
differs from $\pi$.  Moreover, strict concavity of log gives
$\E\log\widehat L\le\log\E\widehat L=\log L$, with strict inequality for a
nondegenerate estimator.  Neither desired conclusion follows from likelihood
unbiasedness alone.
\end{proof}

A continuous relaxation of resampling, a deterministic optimal-transport
replacement, or a frozen branch may improve differentiability.  It defines
the scalar it executes.  Unless that scalar equals the original likelihood up
to a constant, it is a relaxed or branch-conditional target; the neural-force
MH step cannot silently upgrade it.

\subsection{Pseudo-marginal likelihoods}

Assume $\widehat L(u;\omega)\ge0$.  Let $\nu(d\omega)$ be a dominating measure
and write $m(\omega\mid u)$ for the density, with respect to $\nu$, of the
auxiliary random variables used by the estimator.  Define the extended
density with respect to $du\,\nu(d\omega)$ by
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde\pi(u,\omega)
 =\frac{p(u)e^{J(u)}\widehat L(u;\omega)
         m(\omega\mid u)}{Z}.
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-pseudo-marginal-target}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Pseudo-marginal marginalization]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-pseudo-marginal}
If $\widehat L$ is nonnegative and unbiased,
\begin{equation}
 \int\widehat L(u;\omega)m(\omega\mid u)\nu(d\omega)=L(u),
 \end{equation}
then the $u$-marginal of
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-pseudo-marginal-target} is the exact posterior
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-exact-posterior}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Integrating \eqref{eq:bf-hnn-pseudo-marginal-target} over $\omega$ gives
$Z^{-1}p(u)e^{J(u)}L(u)$ by unbiasedness, which is the normalized exact
posterior.
\end{proof}

This proposition does not authorize naive HMC on
$-\log\widehat L(u;\omega)$ with fresh $\omega$ at each force or endpoint
evaluation.  The Markov state is $(u,\omega)$, and a valid kernel must preserve
the extended density.  If $m(\omega\mid u)$ depends on $u$, its log density is
also part of the conditional $u$-energy.  More explicitly, conditional on
fixed $\omega$, that energy contains
\begin{equation}
 -\log p(u)-J(u)-\log\widehat L(u;\omega)-\log m(\omega\mid u).
\end{equation}
Thus using only
$-\log\widehat L(u;\omega)$ differentiates and corrects the wrong extended
scalar.  Discrete resampling variables do not automatically
admit Hamiltonian dynamics; refreshing them requires an MH/Gibbs/pseudo-
marginal step with the appropriate forward and reverse laws.  A neural force
may propose $u$ conditional on retained $\omega$, but correctness then depends
on a complete extended-state kernel, not on
Proposition~\ref{prop:bf-hnn-safe-exactness} applied only to $u$.

\begin{proposition}[Fresh noisy endpoint values do not define the deterministic MH ratio]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-fresh-noise-invalid}
Replacing $L_F(u)$ in
\eqref{eq:bf-hnn-true-energy-acceptance} by independent fresh estimates at the
current and proposed states is not, in general, a valid implementation of
either deterministic HMC for $\pi$ or pseudo-marginal MCMC for
$\widetilde\pi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The deterministic proof fails because the acceptance probability is random
conditional on $(u,p)$ and is not a function of one fixed target density.
The pseudo-marginal proof fails because the estimator realization at the
current state is part of the extended state and must be retained on rejection;
redrawing it changes the denominator without an extended-state proposal ratio.
Only special noisy-MCMC constructions with their own balance proof avoid this
conclusion for $\widetilde\pi$.
\end{proof}

\section{Training a useful position potential}
\label{sec:bf-hnn-training}

Correctness does not require an accurate force, but efficiency does.  A robust
design uses a known baseline plus a learned residual.  In affine or NeuTra
coordinates $z$, write
\begin{equation}
 \widehat U_\phi(z)=\tfrac12z^\top M_0^{-1}z+r_\phi(z).
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-residual-potential}
\end{equation}
The zero residual is already a meaningful no-training baseline.  It tests
whether the chosen coordinates have made the target nearly Gaussian.  Learning
$r_\phi$ should be attempted only if this baseline leaves a material force or
acceptance gap.

Given training triples $(z_i,U_F(z_i),\nabla U_F(z_i))$, a typical scalar
potential loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L(\phi)
 =\lambda_V\frac1N\sum_i
   \left(\widehat U_\phi(z_i)-U_F(z_i)-c\right)^2
 +\lambda_G\frac1N\sum_i
   \left\|\nabla\widehat U_\phi(z_i)-\nabla U_F(z_i)\right\|_{W_i}^2,
 \label{eq:bf-hnn-training-loss}
\end{equation}
where $c$ absorbs the irrelevant potential offset and $W_i\succeq0$ records
the declared scaling or robust weighting.  Training on values alone may fit a
potential while missing its force.  Training on force alone determines the
potential only up to a constant, which is harmless for leapfrog but still
requires heldout energy diagnostics.

\begin{proposition}[Potential offsets do not affect the learned-force kernel]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-offset}
Replacing $\widehat U_\phi$ by $\widehat U_\phi+c$ for any constant $c$ leaves
every learned-force proposal and the true endpoint acceptance unchanged.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The gradient of a constant is zero, so every kick is unchanged.  The MH ratio
uses $H_F$, not $\widehat U_\phi$, so its value is also unchanged.
\end{proof}

Training points must cover posterior-relevant trajectories, not merely a large
axis-aligned box.  Useful sources include tuned ordinary-HMC warmup states,
NeuTra base draws mapped into source coordinates, accepted and rejected
endpoints, and selected interior points.  Validation must be split by
trajectory or chain rather than randomly splitting highly correlated points.
Tail and mode coverage remain explicit failure modes.

The network is frozen before the kernel is used for inference.  Online updates
during retained sampling make the proposal time-inhomogeneous and invalidate
the fixed-kernel proof unless an adaptive-MCMC theorem with diminishing
adaptation and containment is supplied.  The safe operational response to a
large energy-error alarm is to use a separately valid fallback transition and
record the event, not silently retrain inside the retained chain.

\begin{proposition}[Mixtures of valid fallback kernels remain valid]
\label{prop:bf-hnn-mixture-kernels}
Let $P_1$ be the corrected learned-force kernel and $P_2$ an ordinary HMC or
random-walk MH kernel, each invariant for the same $\pi_F$.  For a fixed
$a\in[0,1]$, the mixture $P=aP_1+(1-a)P_2$ is invariant for $\pi_F$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Linearity of integration gives
$\pi_FP=a\pi_FP_1+(1-a)\pi_FP_2=a\pi_F+(1-a)\pi_F=\pi_F$.
\end{proof}

A state-dependent fallback probability is not covered by this proposition; it
requires its own balance argument.  One safe design draws the kernel identity
independently before the transition.  A second safe pattern rejects a proposal
using a pair-symmetric veto, for example
$\mathbf1\{|H_F(Fx)-H_F(x)|\le B\}$, because the absolute energy difference is
the same for $x$ and $Fx$ when $F$ is an involution; the ordinary MH balance
proof then applies on the surviving pairs.  A one-sided alarm or a fallback
selected after inspecting only the proposed endpoint is not covered.  After a
diagnostic rejection, a separately scheduled fixed-mixture fallback may be
used on a later transition.

\section{Algorithm and implementation contract}
\label{sec:bf-hnn-algorithm}

For a deterministic filter-defined potential, the minimal valid algorithm is:

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{True-energy-corrected neural-force HMC}
\label{alg:bf-hnn-safe-hmc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Current $u$ and cached $U_F(u)$; frozen $g_\phi$; $M\succ0$;
         step size $\epsilon$; steps $L$.
\State Draw $p\sim\calN(0,M)$ and store $p_0\gets p$, $u_0\gets u$.
\State $p\gets p-(\epsilon/2)g_\phi(u)$.
\For{$j=1,\ldots,L$}
  \State $u\gets u+\epsilon M^{-1}p$.
  \If{$j<L$}
    \State $p\gets p-\epsilon g_\phi(u)$.
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State $p\gets p-(\epsilon/2)g_\phi(u)$.
\State Set $(u',p')\gets(u,-p)$ and evaluate the new true value $U_F(u')$.
\State $\Delta H\gets U_F(u')+K(p')-U_F(u_0)-K(p_0)$.
\State Accept $(u',U_F(u'))$ with probability $\min\{1,e^{-\Delta H}\}$;
       otherwise retain $(u_0,U_F(u_0))$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The implementation must preserve the following contracts.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Target identity.}  Endpoint values include the same prior,
 parameter chart, Jacobian, data, filter, branch policy, and numerical settings
 as the declared posterior.
 \item \textbf{Joint energy.}  Initial and final momenta are retained until
 $\Delta H$ is computed.  A posterior-only correction is forbidden.
 \item \textbf{Frozen proposal.}  Network weights, normalization, affine or
 NeuTra map, mass matrix, $\epsilon$, and $L$ are fixed during retained
 sampling.  Warmup adaptation ends before evidence draws.
 \item \textbf{Symmetric map.}  The executed update is exactly the symmetric
 kick--drift--kick composition.  Early termination, clipping, projection,
 state-dependent step size, non-symmetric finite-value rescue, or a direct
 neural state-update map needs a new proof.
 \item \textbf{Deterministic endpoint.}  Deterministic filters must reproduce
 the same value on repeat calls.  Stochastic filters must use a separately
 specified extended-state construction.
 \item \textbf{Precision and failure.}  A mathematically declared
 out-of-support endpoint has $U_F=+\infty$ and rejects.  By contrast, an
 undefined learned force, failed factorization, overflow, or numerically
 indeterminate filter value means that the executed proposal or target is not
 the proved map.  It must emit a failed-transition diagnostic and block
 promotion; it must not be hidden as ordinary rejection.  Replacing any failed
 true value by a convenient neural value changes the kernel and is forbidden.
 \item \textbf{Batching and XLA.}  Chain and training batches should be
 TensorFlow/XLA operations on the BayesFilter route.  Batching is an efficiency
 requirement; it does not alter the proof.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Tests that answer the mathematical question}
\label{sec:bf-hnn-tests}

Unit tests should target the proof assumptions rather than only finite output.

\paragraph{Map tests.}
For random finite phase points, verify
\begin{equation}
 \|R\Phi_{\epsilon,L}R\Phi_{\epsilon,L}(x)-x\|
 \end{equation}
at a dtype-appropriate tolerance.  Check a small-dimensional autodiff Jacobian
against determinant magnitude one.  These tests do not prove the property in
all dimensions, but they catch implementation drift from the proved formula.

\paragraph{Acceptance tests.}
On a Gaussian target with an exact force, acceptance should approach one as
$\epsilon\to0$, but need not equal one for finite $\epsilon$.  Tests that
require identically one because the surrogate equals the target are testing the
wrong statement: leapfrog still has discretization error.  A deliberately
biased force should be tested for agreement with Gaussian moments under a
long-enough, irreducible corrected-chain experiment, while recognizing that
poor mixing can make a finite run inconclusive.

\paragraph{Counterexample tests.}
Test three deliberate defects: remove the final kinetic term; make the force
momentum-dependent; and omit stage-one delayed acceptance.  Small reference
tests should then detect stationary-moment or detailed-balance failure.  These
negative tests guard the exact mistakes documented in this chapter.

\paragraph{Filtering tests.}
For deterministic filters, repeat endpoint values and compare direct versus
cached evaluations.  For an exact LGSSM, compare the corrected chain with the
analytic/Kalman posterior.  For UKF or SGQF, label results as filter-defined and
compare to an independent higher-fidelity likelihood only as a sensitivity
study.  For a particle filter, reject any harness that refreshes estimator
randomness inside a nominally deterministic HMC ratio without an extended-
state manifest.

\section{Scientific evaluation ladder}
\label{sec:bf-hnn-evaluation}

The smallest informative comparison is not HNN-HMC versus an untuned HMC.  Use
the following matched ladder:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item tuned ordinary HMC on $U_F$;
 \item affine or NeuTra coordinates with ordinary true gradients;
 \item the same coordinates with the zero-residual Gaussian force from
 \eqref{eq:bf-hnn-residual-potential};
 \item the trained residual potential with true-energy correction;
 \item the trained route plus a predeclared fixed-probability valid fallback,
 if fallback is needed.
\end{enumerate}
All arms use the same target, retained-draw policy, diagnostic thresholds, and
hardware class.  Training data generation and network training are charged in
the amortized comparison.  Report at least true endpoint values, learned-force
calls, true-gradient calls, acceptance, modern rank/folded $\widehat R$, bulk
and tail ESS, energy-error tails, fallback rate, wall time, and ESS per second.

Pointwise value RMSE, force cosine similarity, and validation loss are
explanatory diagnostics.  They can reject a clearly broken learner but cannot
promote it.  The promotion criterion is downstream corrected-chain validity
and amortized efficiency against the tuned matched baselines.  Passing one
filter-defined posterior does not establish latent-model exactness or broad
cross-model superiority.

\section{Conclusions and nonclaims}

Metropolis-corrected neural-force HMC is a good research direction for
filtering posteriors because it can replace $L$ expensive filtering scores by
$L$ cheap neural-force calls and one new true endpoint value.  The valid core
is narrower than the slogan:
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\text{frozen position-only symmetric proposal}
 +\text{ true joint endpoint energy}.}
\end{equation}
Under the proved assumptions, force approximation affects efficiency rather
than the invariant deterministic target.  The result does not extend to any of
the following constructions without additional analysis:
\begin{itemize}
 \item an arbitrary neural state-update map;
 \item a momentum-dependent learned Hamiltonian without proved symmetry and
 integrator properties;
 \item a Metropolis correction that drops endpoint kinetic energy; or
 \item a nominal delayed-acceptance kernel that omits stage one.
\end{itemize}

Most importantly, exactness is relative to the endpoint scalar.  Exact Kalman
likelihood yields the corresponding latent-model posterior.  UKF, SGQF, fixed
branch, relaxed, or learned filtering likelihoods yield their named
filter-defined posteriors.  An unbiased particle likelihood can recover the
exact posterior only through a valid extended-state pseudo-marginal kernel;
fresh noisy endpoint estimates are not a substitute.  No amount of accuracy in
the neural force crosses that filtering boundary.
