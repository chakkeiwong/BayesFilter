# Claim-support ledger

This ledger keeps the proposed program's claims separate from what the
literature actually supports.

| Program claim or proposed sentence | Evidence class | Supporting source/derivation | Verdict and required qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Fisher-identity particle recursion is a regular-transition model-score estimator | Primary theorem/derivation | Poyiadjis et al.; Del Moral et al. derivative theory | Supported under the paper's domination, differentiability, and stability assumptions. Do not claim universal finite-\( N \) unbiasedness or transfer to singular DSGE transitions |
| Nemeth's KDM can be used as the missing KDM score arm | Primary algorithm | Nemeth et al., Sections 3--4 and Algorithms 1--2 | Supported as a named estimator of the regular-state-space score approximation. Add the fixed-bandwidth and Rao--Blackwell assumptions; finite-\( N \) exactness is unsupported |
| PaRIS is a valid “backward-pair” replacement | Primary algorithm/theorem | Olsson & Westerborn, PaRIS recursion and variance theorem | Supported only as a regular HMM additive-functional smoother. The number of backward draws and mixing assumptions must be explicit; it is not a universal singular-transition remedy |
| Coupling can remove smoothing-estimator bias | Primary debiasing theorem | Jacob, Lindsten & Schön, coupled conditional particle filters, Theorem 3.1 and score remark | Supported for finite-horizon smoothing expectations under the CPF meeting/moment assumptions. Combined with Fisher's identity it gives an unbiased score when the transition density is tractable; it does not imply finite-cost, finite-variance exactness for singular or intractable transitions |
| Coupling two LEDH particle filters automatically lowers the variance of the score | Primary coupling algorithm plus variance derivation | Sen, Thiéry & Jasra, *On Coupling Particle Filter Trajectories*, Sections 1--2 | Wrong as a single-score claim. Coupling is useful for a difference (S(\theta_1)-S(\theta_0)), finite differences, or level corrections because positive cross-replica correlation reduces difference variance. Averaging two positively coupled copies can retain or increase variance; each marginal and its score target are unchanged |
| Continuous or OT resampling preserves the model score | Not established by continuous-resampling papers | Malik--Pitt/DeJong address continuous likelihood evaluation; Corenflos analyzes differentiable OT PF | Wrong as an unqualified claim. Continuous differentiability removes an implementation discontinuity but can change the finite particle measure and introduce bias; the model-score identity must be checked separately |
| Iterated filtering supplies an exact score when the transition density is unavailable | Primary approximation theorem | Ionides et al. iterated-filtering theorems | Wrong relative to “exact finite-N score.” It supplies a vanishing-artificial-noise approximation with a bias/variance/mixing trade-off; useful as a separately labelled simulation-only comparator |
| The Guarniero--Johansen--Lee iterated APF is a replacement score method or UKF score correction | Primary algorithm, target-boundary derivation | Guarniero--Johansen--Lee, Sections 2--3, Algorithms 2--4 | Wrong relative to the score claim. iAPF iterates future-data twisting functions to improve the auxiliary proposal and preserves the likelihood normalizer through correction; it is a proposal-quality arm, not a score identity. It may replace the UKF only as a separately audited proposal-construction module |
| Better UKF/KDM/LEDH covariance automatically improves the score | No general theorem found | Proposal-geometry argument only | Unsupported. A covariance can improve proposal coverage or worsen weight variance; score impact must be measured against the same target and correction |
| Twisting improves the score | Primary proposal theory, not score theory | Whiteley & Lee twisted PF | Wrong as a direct score claim. Twisting is a likelihood/normalizer variance-control mechanism; test its downstream effect while holding the score estimator fixed |
| Multilevel particle filters remove score bias | Primary multilevel filtering theory | Jasra et al. | Wrong without a new derivative/telescope proof. The paper controls discretization/filtering error with coupled levels; the score and random ratio require additional identities |
| A linear combination of two reasonable biased scores is better | Elementary local derivation in master plan | Bias/variance/covariance formula | Supported only as a calibrated estimator experiment. Variance minimization alone cannot establish lower MSE when component biases differ |
| Younis IWSG is an unbiased marginal-score estimator | Source target mismatch | Younis fixed-proposal mixture calculus | Wrong relative to the marginal model score in general. It is unbiased for the conditional/fixed-mixture expectation derivative declared in that construction; it needs a separate measure/normalization identity to equal the marginal score |
| Disturbance coordinates make the DSGE singular score computable | Murray representation plus local derivation | Murray et al.; local disturbance-score propositions | Partly supported as a route to a declared shock-coordinate density. The required base measure, Jacobian, parameter dependence, nonsmooth regime handling, and estimator variance remain to be proved and tested |
| Regular-track success transfers to DSGE | No source support | None | Explicitly forbidden by the program's support split; this would be a scientific overclaim |
| Exact particle likelihood can be used inside PMMH even if its derivative is not valid for HMC | PMCMC theory and target distinction | Andrieu et al. as cited by Kantas; local target registry | Supported as a boundary principle, but the exact consumer and pseudo-marginal assumptions must be checked when HMC/PMMH work is started |
