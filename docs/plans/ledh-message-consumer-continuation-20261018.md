## Evidence contract and research intent

The candidate is an exact seven-component future message consumed by the
existing optional product-moment global map. The main comparator is the
saved fitted kernel message consumed by that SAME map. Keep the old consumer
for both messages as attribution controls. Construct the cheap adversaries:
identity (no flow), global (one common affine map), local (particle maps),
equal mixture (no optimized mixing), Gaussian future/global (moment-only
lookahead), and Gaussian future/equal (moment-only lookahead with diversity).
Evaluate each separately in ordinary, negative-tail and positive-tail data,
and for each of the two score coordinates; never pool these situations.

Primary comparison: paired squared errors against the checked KSC numerical
model-score reference. Eight saved independent fit clusters each receive eight
NEW common-random-number clouds. For each condition/coordinate compare exact
product vs fitted product, and each product arm vs unguided global, for global
and equal mixing: 36 predeclared contrasts. Average paired differences within
each cluster, then use a t interval with seven degrees of freedom and a
Bonferroni simultaneous 95% family across all 36 contrasts. These intervals
are a small-cluster approximation, conditional on the saved fits and three
previously known datasets. They cannot establish fresh-fit generalization.

Promotion vetoes: any conditional heuristic loss; statistically supported
harm to the comparator; or failed numerical validity. Descriptive heuristic
loss is conservatively a promotion veto, not evidence of a statistically
supported ranking. No default promotion is possible in this campaign.
Continuation vetoes: missing/corrupt/unpaired evidence, invalid reference,
nonfinite values or failed score/trace validity, unresolved derivative or
source-identity mismatch, and exhausted budget. A valid candidate losing on
score is a repair trigger, not a continuation veto. Bias, variance, likelihood
values and runtime are explanatory diagnostics.

We will not conclude canonical correctness, HMC readiness, cross-model
transfer, TF32 readiness, tuning validity, or that exact message moments imply
an exact filtering distribution. Finite-program derivative checks validate
the derivative target; they do not validate accuracy of the model score.
