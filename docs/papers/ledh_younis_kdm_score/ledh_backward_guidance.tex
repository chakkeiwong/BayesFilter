\section{Backward information as a proposal, not a replacement score}
\label{sec:backward-guidance}

The first construction below keeps one LEDH particle cloud, the existing
per-particle UKF covariance recursion, OT, Contract--E--Chol and the GenUT
dual-cap correction. Future observations inform where to propose particles;
they are not appended to the observation likelihood. The returned score is
the analytical total derivative of the returned finite log normalizer. This
choice differs from the two-cloud Fisher-score construction: a Fisher
estimate of the model score need not differentiate a finite particle
likelihood. Neither construction has established lower model-score error.
The final subsection develops a separate composition with the existing
identity/global/local density-mixture proposal. Its moment-restoring reset is
distinct from this first construction's Contract--E--Chol implementation.

\subsection{What travels backward}

Suppose the whole observation sequence is already available. Write
$f_\theta(x\mid z)$ for the transition density and $g_t(x)$ for the
observation density at time $t$. All densities in the following proposition
are with respect to fixed dominating measures, and the conditioning
normalizers are positive and finite. Future information is the function
$h_t(x)=p_\theta(y_{t+1:T}\mid X_t=x)$, not a sampled trajectory.

\begin{proposition}[Backward information and the smoothing proposal]
\label{prop:bg-backward}
With $h_T=1$,
\begin{align}
 h_{t-1}(z)&=\int f_\theta(x\mid z)g_t(x)h_t(x)\,\dd x,
 \label{eq:bg-backward}\\
 p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{t-1}=z,y_{t:T})
 &=\frac{f_\theta(x_t\mid z)g_t(x_t)h_t(x_t)}{h_{t-1}(z)}.
 \label{eq:bg-smoothing-proposal}
\end{align}
If $h_t$ is constant, it has no effect on the normalized proposal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Condition on $X_t=x$ in $p(y_{t:T}\mid X_{t-1}=z)$ and use the Markov
property and conditional independence of observations. This gives the first
identity. Bayes' rule gives the second; a constant factor cancels between
numerator and denominator.
\end{proof}
This is the state-space backward-information argument of
\citet[eqs.~(9)--(10), Proposition~4.1]{heng2020controlled}. Their approximate
dynamic programming fits backward policy corrections using pilot
parent--child pairs. It is not the same operation as following surviving
ancestral trajectories. A fitted function may supply useful future
information without reconstructing any full smoothing path.

\subsection{A precisely defined, frozen-guide proposal}

We first restrict the proposed implementation to full-rank additive Gaussian
transitions $f_\theta(x\mid z)=\mathcal N(x;a,Q)$, where
$a=m_\theta(z)$ and $Q=Q_\theta\succ0$. This excludes singular transitions.
A pilot produces a fixed schedule of guide coefficients $\eta$ before the
evaluated finite program starts. These coefficients, any fit reference
parameter, the observation sequence and base Gaussian draws are fixed when
that program is differentiated. Re-fitting at each evaluated parameter and
then ignoring the fit derivative would compute a different quantity.

For each incoming particle, let a guide evaluator return symmetric
$H\succeq0$ and $b$, possibly depending smoothly on its current anchor $a$.
At this anchor the local proposal factor is
$\exp(-x^\top Hx/2+b^\top x+c)$. The additive constant $c$ need not be
known because it cancels on normalization. There is no extra observation
factor in the importance numerator.

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian proposal and its total tangent]
\label{prop:bg-gaussian}
The normalized product of the Gaussian transition and this local factor is
$q^0=\mathcal N(m,V)$, where
\begin{align}
 P&=Q^{-1},& K&=P+H,& V&=K^{-1},& m&=V(Pa+b).
 \label{eq:bg-gaussian}
\end{align}
For any parameter direction, dots denote total directional derivatives,
including dependence through the incoming state. Then
\begin{align}
 \dot P&=-P\dot QP,& \dot K&=\dot P+\dot H,&
 \dot V&=-V\dot K V,\nonumber\\
 \dot m&=V(\dot P a+P\dot a+\dot b-\dot K m).
 \label{eq:bg-gaussian-tangent}
\end{align}
For fixed $\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$, put $U=m+L\xi$ with
$L=\operatorname{chol}(V)$. Its tangent is
$\dot U=\dot m+\dot L\xi$, where
$\dot L=L\Phi(L^{-1}\dot V L^{-\top})$ and $\Phi$ retains the lower
triangle, halves the diagonal and sets the upper triangle to zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand the Gaussian exponent, collect its quadratic coefficient $P+H$ and
linear coefficient $Pa+b$, and complete the square. Since $P\succ0$ and
$H\succeq0$, $K\succ0$; thus $Q,K,V,L$ are invertible. Explicitly,
\begin{align*}
 \dot P Q+P\dot Q&=0,\\
 \dot K V+K\dot V&=0,\\
 \dot K m+K\dot m&=\dot P a+P\dot a+\dot b.
\end{align*}
Multiplication by the appropriate inverse gives each displayed tangent.
Differentiating $LL^\top=V$
and imposing lower triangular $\dot L$ gives the stated Cholesky formula.
The base draw is constant, giving the last identity.
\end{proof}
For a controlled-SMC quadratic policy
$\psi(x)=\exp(-x^\top A x-\beta^\top x-c)$, the adapter is
$H=2A$, $b=-\beta$ when $A\succeq0$. The sign and factor of two agree
with the released Lorenz implementation's shifted Gaussian precision. This
adapter borrows a proposal factor; it does not claim to execute the complete
controlled-SMC twisted-potential algorithm. Policies depending on the current
observation may still be corrected as proposals, but only a future-only
policy can support the specific lookahead interpretation above.

\begin{proposition}[Exporting future information from a fitted scalar policy]
\label{prop:bg-fitted-future}
Fix a pilot reference transition
$z\mid x\sim\mathcal N(\alpha_\star x,Q_\star)$ with $Q_\star>0$.
Suppose its fitted next-time policy is
$\widehat\psi_{t+1}(z)=\exp(-a_{t+1}z^2-\beta_{t+1}z-c_{t+1})$,
where $a_{t+1}\geq0$. Write
$D_{t+1}=1+2Q_\star a_{t+1}$. The future-only factor
$\widehat h_t(x)=M^\star_{t+1}\widehat\psi_{t+1}(x)$ has coefficients
\begin{equation}
 H_t=\frac{2\alpha_\star^2 a_{t+1}}{D_{t+1}},
 \qquad b_t=-\frac{\alpha_\star\beta_{t+1}}{D_{t+1}},
 \qquad H_T=b_T=0.                                      \label{eq:bg-fitted-future}
\end{equation}
These coefficients are frozen when differentiating the evaluation model.
For a scalar linear Gaussian observation model, a backward quadratic fit
starting from the zero policy recovers the exact pilot backward information
in exact arithmetic if every regression design has full column rank and
the precision projection is inactive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Gaussian integral exists because $D_{t+1}>0$. With
$a=a_{t+1}$, $\beta=\beta_{t+1}$, $c=c_{t+1}$ and $D=D_{t+1}$,
completion of the square gives
\begin{align*}
 \widehat h_t(x)
 &=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi Q_\star}}
   \int_{\mathbb R}
   \exp\!\left[-\frac{(z-\alpha_\star x)^2}{2Q_\star}
                   -az^2-\beta z-c\right]\,dz,\\
 \log\widehat h_t(x)
 &=-\frac12\log D-c+\frac{Q_\star\beta^2}{2D}
   -\frac{\alpha_\star^2a}{D}x^2
   -\frac{\alpha_\star\beta}{D}x .
\end{align*}
Comparison with $-\tfrac12 H_t x^2+b_t x$ proves the formula.
The terminal factor is one because there is no subsequent observation.

For the exact-fit statement, write the scalar observation model as
$y_t=Cx_t+\varepsilon_t$, $\varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(0,R)$,
with $R>0$. If the future factor has precision $J_t$ and linear
coefficient $r_t$, then the current-and-future policy satisfies
\[
 -\log\psi_t(x)
 =\frac12(J_t+C^2/R)x^2-(r_t+Cy_t/R)x+\text{constant}.
\]
At the terminal time this is a quadratic, with $J_T=r_T=0$.
The integral just derived preserves the quadratic form at the preceding
time. Starting from zero policy, the replicated controlled-SMC backward
regression fits precisely the negative log current likelihood plus the
negative log integral of the newly fitted next-time policy. Its design
columns are $(x^2,x,1)$; full column rank therefore gives the unique exact
coefficients. Backward induction proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

This construction uses the next fitted policy, not the current one: the
current policy already contains $y_t$. When $\alpha_\star=0$, the future
observations supply no information about $x_t$ and both exported coefficients
vanish. A mismatched pilot still defines an importance-corrected proposal,
but its guide represents the pilot model's future information rather than
the evaluation model's exact backward information. Floating-point regression
conditioning and any active precision projection must be checked separately.

\subsection{What a kernel mixture can contribute}

A separate optional representation is a positive frozen Gaussian mixture
$\widehat h(x)=\sum_k\alpha_k\mathcal N(x;c_k,S_k)$ with
$\alpha_k>0$, $\sum_k\alpha_k=1$, and $S_k\succ0$. Multiplication of
$\widehat h$ by a positive constant has no effect on the following guide.
A normalized mixture is only a hypothesis for the shape of backward
information; backward information need not itself be integrable as a
function of state. No claim of universal representability is made.
Unlike sampling a component label, evaluating this mixture introduces no
categorical discontinuity. Its use here is a new local proposal construction,
not the IWSG estimator of \citet{younis2023}.

\begin{proposition}[Mixture-informed local Gaussian guidance]
\label{prop:bg-mixture}
At the anchor $a$, define $\Lambda_k=S_k^{-1}$ and
\begin{align}
 r_k&=\frac{\alpha_k\mathcal N(a;c_k,S_k)}{\widehat h(a)},&
 v_k&=\Lambda_k(c_k-a),& \bar v&=\sum_k r_kv_k,\nonumber\\
 H&=\sum_k r_k\Lambda_k,& b&=\sum_k r_k\Lambda_kc_k.
 \label{eq:bg-mixture}
\end{align}
Then $H\succ0$ and the local quadratic has the same spatial gradient as
$\log\widehat h$ at $a$, since $b-Ha=\bar v$.
For fixed mixture coefficients,
\begin{align}
 \dot r_k&=r_k(v_k-\bar v)^\top\dot a,&
 \dot H&=\sum_k\dot r_k\Lambda_k,&
 \dot b&=\sum_k\dot r_k\Lambda_kc_k.
 \label{eq:bg-mixture-tangent}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The gradient of a Gaussian log density is $v_k$ at $a$; differentiation of
the log-sum-exp gives $\bar v$. In particular,
$\dot{\log r_k}=v_k^\top\dot a-\bar v^\top\dot a$ and
$\dot r_k=r_k\dot{\log r_k}$, which gives the claimed tangent.
Differentiate the finite sums to obtain $\dot H$ and $\dot b$. A positive
weighted sum of positive-definite matrices is positive definite, and
substitution gives $b-Ha=\sum_k r_k\Lambda_k(c_k-a)$.
\end{proof}
This is gradient matching, not Hessian matching:
$\nabla^2\log\widehat h(a)=-H+\operatorname{Cov}_r(v_k)$.
Discarding the covariance term defines the proposal approximation explicitly;
it does not justify treating a multimodal mixture as log concave. Nor does a
local Gaussian retain all mixture modes. The omission is not a hidden
stabilizer for the physical likelihood; the proposal density is corrected
and the change in finite-particle behavior still requires evaluation.

\subsection{The corrected finite likelihood and its derivative}

Generate the pre-flow particle from $q^0$, not from the unmodified
transition. Apply the existing LEDH flow using its physical prediction and
current observation. Write its output as $X=F(U)$ and the log-Jacobian
quantity computed by the existing flow as $j$. Its incoming normalized
weight is $W$. The new pre-reset log weight is defined by
\begin{equation}
 \lambda=\log W+\log f_\theta(X\mid z)+\log g_t(X)
                 +j-\log q^0(U).
 \label{eq:bg-weight}
\end{equation}
In particular, subtracting $\log f_\theta(U\mid z)$ instead of
$\log q^0(U)$ is wrong for this proposal. The guide is not multiplied into
$g_t$. Initialization is unchanged: this bounded implementation does not
twist the initial law.

The shared flow evaluates the observation and its Jacobian along a separate
auxiliary trajectory initialized at the physical prediction. This trajectory
does not depend on the current proposal draw $U$. A nonlinear observation can
therefore give an affine map of $U$ after conditioning on the incoming cloud.
The distinction matters: the coefficients still depend on $\theta$, including
through earlier resets, and their parameter derivatives must be retained.

\begin{proposition}[Actual Jacobian of the auxiliary-localized flow]
\label{prop:bg-flow-jacobian}
Condition on the incoming cloud, guide and current observation. Suppose the
predicted covariance $P$ and observation covariance $R$ are positive definite,
and all auxiliary evaluations are finite. Fix $S\geq1$ uniform Euler steps,
put $\epsilon=1/S$ and $\tau_s=(s+1)\epsilon$, and let $H_s$ be the observation
Jacobian evaluated at the auxiliary point at step $s$. The shared flow uses
\[
 A_s=-\tfrac12 PH_s^\top(\tau_sH_sPH_s^\top+R)^{-1}H_s,
 \qquad M_s=I+\epsilon A_s,
 \qquad x_{s+1}=M_sx_s+\epsilon b_s,
 \qquad x_0=U,
\]
where $b_s$, like $H_s$, is independent of $U$. Then $F(U)=x_S$ is an affine
bijection of $\R^d$, with
\begin{equation}
 D_UF=M_{S-1}\cdots M_0,
 \qquad j=\log\det D_UF=\sum_{s=0}^{S-1}\log\det M_s.
 \label{eq:bg-flow-jacobian}
\end{equation}
On a differentiable parameter neighborhood satisfying these conditions,
\[
 \dot j=\sum_{s=0}^{S-1}
       \operatorname{tr}\!\left(M_s^{-1}\epsilon\dot A_s\right),
\]
where $\dot A_s$ is the total derivative, including the auxiliary trajectory,
$P$, $R$ and the observation Jacobian.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The auxiliary recurrence starts from the physical prediction and uses its own
previous point, so induction makes every $H_s$, $A_s$ and $b_s$ independent of
$U$. Differentiating the affine recurrence with respect to $U$ gives the
product in \eqref{eq:bg-flow-jacobian}.

To check invertibility rather than assume it, write $P=LL^\top$ and
$C_s=R^{-1/2}H_sL$. Then
\[
 L^{-1}M_sL
 =I-\tfrac{\epsilon}{2}
 C_s^\top(I+\tau_sC_sC_s^\top)^{-1}C_s.
\]
For a singular value $\sigma$ of $C_s$, the corresponding eigenvalue of the
subtracted matrix before multiplication by $\epsilon/2$ is
$\sigma^2/(1+\tau_s\sigma^2)<1/\tau_s$; any remaining eigenvalues are zero.
Consequently, every eigenvalue of $L^{-1}M_sL$ is strictly greater than
$1-\epsilon/(2\tau_s)\geq1/2$. Each $M_s$ is invertible with positive
determinant. Their product is invertible, proving the global affine
bijection and the sum of log determinants. Finally, Jacobi's formula gives
$\dd\log\det M_s=\operatorname{tr}(M_s^{-1}\dd M_s)$ and
$\dot M_s=\epsilon\dot A_s$ because the step schedule is fixed. This proves
the stated total derivative.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional importance identity and its limitation]
\label{prop:bg-correction}
Condition on the incoming cloud and guide. If $q^0$ is positive on $\R^d$,
$F$ is a differentiable bijection of $\R^d$ and
$j=\log|\det D_UF|$ is its actual Jacobian, then, whenever the right-hand
integral is absolutely finite,
\[
 \mathbb E_{U\sim q^0}\!\left[
 \varphi(F(U))\frac{f_\theta(F(U)\mid z)g_t(F(U))e^j}{q^0(U)}\right]
 =\int\varphi(x)f_\theta(x\mid z)g_t(x)\,\dd x.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Cancel $q^0$ against the sampling density and substitute $x=F(u)$.
The absolute Jacobian converts $\dd u$ into $\dd x$.
\end{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:bg-flow-jacobian} establishes the bijection and
actual-Jacobian conditions for this auxiliary-localized flow under its stated
covariance and finiteness assumptions. Localizing instead at the transported
particle would change $D_UF$ and would require another argument. The
deterministic OT/Contract-E/GenUT reset is not multinomial resampling and
does not inherit an unbiased full-filter normalizer from this one-step
identity.

\begin{proposition}[Total derivative of the retained finite program]
\label{prop:bg-total}
Fix base draws, guide coefficients, iteration counts, residual design and
all non-parameter controls. On an open neighborhood where each executed
operation is differentiable, Cholesky factors exist and branch choices are
unchanged, define
\[
 \widehat\ell_\eta(\theta;\xi)
  =\sum_{t=1}^T \log\sum_i\exp(\lambda_t^i),\qquad
 w_t^i=\frac{\exp(\lambda_t^i)}{\sum_j\exp(\lambda_t^j)}.
\]
Propagating all input tangents gives
\begin{align}
 \dot\lambda&=\dot{\log W}+\dot{\log f}+\dot{\log g}
                         +\dot j-\dot{\log q^0},\nonumber\\
 \dot{\widehat\ell}_\eta&=\sum_t\sum_iw_t^i\dot\lambda_t^i,&
 \dot w_t^i&=w_t^i\left(\dot\lambda_t^i-\sum_jw_t^j\dot\lambda_t^j\right).
 \label{eq:bg-total}
\end{align}
This is the total directional derivative of this same finite scalar.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $q^0=\mathcal N(m,V)$, put $e=U-m$. Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \dot{\log q^0(U)}=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(V^{-1}\dot V)
 -(V^{-1}e)^\top(\dot U-\dot m)
 +\tfrac12e^\top V^{-1}\dot V V^{-1}e.
\]
Use Proposition~\ref{prop:bg-gaussian} for all its arguments and, when
applicable, Proposition~\ref{prop:bg-mixture} for the moving anchor. The
chain rule gives $\dot\lambda$ and differentiation of log-sum-exp gives
the remaining expressions. Induct over time, carrying state and covariance
tangents through the existing UKF, flow and reset. In the reset, if
$Y=\mathcal T(X,w)$ and $E=\mathcal R(X,w,Y)$, its tangent is
$\mathcal R_X\dot X+\mathcal R_w\dot w+
\mathcal R_Y(\mathcal T_X\dot X+\mathcal T_w\dot w)$, with any declared
ridge/design derivatives as well. Differentiating only $Y$ omits direct
source-moment terms. Apply the same chain rule to GenUT and both caps.
This completes the induction, assuming the invoked component tangents are
correct on that neighborhood.
\end{proof}
The proposition does not equate this finite derivative with the exact model
score. It also does not differentiate the pilot fit: if $\eta=\eta(\theta)$
is recomputed, the omitted term is
$(\partial\widehat\ell/\partial\eta)\dot\eta$.

\begin{proposition}[No automatic score improvement]
\label{prop:bg-no-ranking}
Even exact sampling from a posterior, with constant importance weights,
does not imply minimal variance of a posterior score-functional estimate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On three states let the posterior be uniform and let its complete-data
score functional be $(-1,0,1)$. Its expectation is zero. The asymptotic
variance of a self-normalized importance mean is
$\sum_i\pi_i^2 H_i^2/q_i$. Choosing $q=\pi$ gives $2/3$; choosing
$q=(0.45,0.10,0.45)$ gives $40/81<2/3$, despite nonconstant weights.
These scores can be realized by the exponential family proportional to
$\exp(\theta H_i)$ at $\theta=0$. Thus even an ideal distributional
guide does not establish optimal score error. No ranking of finite LEDH
derivatives follows from likelihood ESS.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Executable order and scientific scope}

The bounded implementation is an optional single-cloud proposal extension,
not an alternative LEDH filter. Its order is:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Freeze a validated time-indexed guide. Its last future-only factor
      is constant. Fix the existing randomness and numerical controls.
\item Predict physical UKF moments and compute each transition anchor and
      its total tangent from the current reset state.
\item Evaluate $H,b,\dot H,\dot b$; generate $U,\dot U$ from the guided
      Gaussian. With guidance disabled use the original transition draw.
\item Run the shared LEDH flow and its analytical tangent. Evaluate the
      unchanged physical transition and observation densities and the
      actual guided Gaussian denominator in \eqref{eq:bg-weight}.
\item Accumulate the finite log normalizer and derivative; update physical
      UKF covariances. Execute the existing OT, Contract E and GenUT
      dual-cap reset with all tangents. These particles are the next parents.
\end{enumerate}
An OT barycentre has no unique sampled ancestor. Neither its transport
matrix nor the coefficients of a kernel mixture are thereby a model
backward-smoothing kernel. No ancestral tracing or Fisher-score substitution
is part of this implementation.

The first evidence is engineering evidence: disabled-guide parity,
Gaussian square-completion checks, moving-anchor mixture tangents, and
finite differences of the complete same-value recursion. The development
comparator uses $\epsilon=1$ and five Sinkhorn iterations; balancing counts,
ridge, correction and cap settings are reported independently. These are
untuned settings. The scalar Gaussian reference now fits a quadratic policy
through the replicated controlled-SMC forward and backward endpoints, exports
its future-only factor by \eqref{eq:bg-fitted-future}, and supplies those
coefficients to the shared analytical LEDH consumer. The closure checks compare
the fitted factor with an independent Gaussian backward recursion. This is a
test of actual pilot integration on that restricted model, rather than mere
acceptance of coefficient arrays; it does not identify a future factor for an
arbitrary nonlinear model. One-component KDM conversion is a representation
check, not a trained mixture guide. Equal-compute comparisons on the nonlinear
targets, scope-specific tuning, batch-native consumer coverage and scientific
or default promotion remain subsequent work. A bounded GPU/XLA Gaussian
reference experiment, even when it completes, cannot by itself establish
those claims.

The completed scalar fitted-guide pilot contains 624 evaluations, but the
tested LEDH candidates fail the conditional comparison with simpler particle
methods in every condition. A subsequent plain-LEDH Gaussian diagnostic
completed 255 of 280 planned evaluations before a transport-validity rejection.
On two six-coordinate fixtures, the analytical score agrees with finite
differences of its own finite likelihood program to a maximum scaled error of
$2.46\times 10^{-9}$. That agreement does not establish accuracy for the model
likelihood score: persistent-condition errors remain large when the flow
resolution increases from 48 to 192 steps. The observed error averages are
descriptive; they do not support a statistical ranking of stochastic methods.

A CPU reconstruction of the rejected case isolates a terminal transport-column
total-variation residual of $2.7055\times 10^{-4}$, above the unchanged
$10^{-4}$ validity threshold. Both XLA and non-XLA replays reject the case.
The failed GPU random tensors were not archived, so exact identity with that
reconstruction has not been checked. Separately, on completed cases the reset
restores its weighted input mean and variance to machine precision, yet the
weighted variance is already too small before the first reset. The following
two calculations distinguish the transport failure from this earlier proposal
problem. Neither derivative parity nor moment restoration alone is sufficient
evidence for accurate model-score estimation.

\subsection{Why more balancing does not remove an additive mass error}

Consider the entrywise positive kernel matrix $K\in\mathbb R^{N\times N}$
used to construct the transport, the desired row masses $a_i=1/N$, and
source weights $w\in\mathbb R^N$ with $w_j>0$ and $\sum_j w_j=1$. The shared
implementation adds a fixed number $\delta$ to both scaling denominators.
This prevents division by zero, but changes the equations being solved.

\begin{proposition}[Marginal deficit under additive scaling]
\label{prop:bg-additive-scaling}
Suppose positive vectors $u,v\in\mathbb R^N$ are a fixed point of
\begin{equation}
 u_i=\frac{a_i}{(Kv)_i+\delta},\qquad
 v_j=\frac{w_j}{(K^{\mathsf T}u)_j+\delta},\qquad \delta\geq0.
 \label{eq:bg-additive-scaling}
\end{equation}
For $C=\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)$, the row and column
masses are respectively $a_i-\delta u_i$ and $w_j-\delta v_j$.
When $\delta=0$ they equal the requested marginals. When $\delta>0$,
normalizing each row of $C$ need not recover the requested column masses.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The row sum is $u_i(Kv)_i=u_i[a_i/u_i-\delta]=a_i-\delta u_i$;
the column calculation is identical. The row-normalized transport satisfies
$T_{ij}=C_{ij}/(a_i-\delta u_i)$. Its induced source mass is therefore
$\sum_i a_iC_{ij}/(a_i-\delta u_i)$, which does not in general equal $w_j$.
For a concrete positive-kernel counterexample, take $a=w=(1/2,1/2)$,
$\delta=1/4$, $K=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}5&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
and $u=v=(1/4,1/2)$. Both fixed-point equations hold. The coupling is
$C=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}5/16&1/8\\1/8&1/4\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
so its row-normalized transport is
$T=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}5/7&2/7\\1/3&2/3\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$.
Its induced source masses are $(11/21,10/21)$ rather than $(1/2,1/2)$.
\end{proof}

The fixed-point calculation alone does not locate the error after a finite
number of updates. A frozen-input comparison does. In the reconstructed
rejection, increasing the terminal count from 32 to 256 reduces column
total variation only from $2.7055\times10^{-4}$ to $2.5292\times10^{-4}$;
both fail the unchanged $10^{-4}$ test. An independent log-domain calculation
matches the existing $\delta=10^{-7}$ transport to machine precision.
At the same $5+32=37$ updates, setting $\delta=0$ reduces the rejected
cloud's residual to $4.45\times10^{-11}$.

This is an untuned FP64 CPU/XLA diagnostic, not production TF32 validation.
An optional zero-$\delta$ setting in the shared implementation passes mass
checks on all 18 frozen clouds and four new-noise full-consumer fixtures.
Analytical reset tangents agree with finite differences to a maximum scaled
error of $1.34\times10^{-8}$; the full-consumer maximum is
$3.30\times10^{-8}$. However, 13 of the 17 previously accepted clouds exceed
the predeclared $10^{-6}$ state-change tolerance, with a largest change of
$4.67\times10^{-6}$. Thus this candidate is rejected as an interchangeable
repair under that test. The default remains unchanged. The result does not
reject unperturbed scaling as a separately calibrated numerical method.

\subsection{A conditional proposal can be narrower than its flow covariance}

The first-step discrepancy needs a different explanation because no reset has
yet occurred. Consider the scalar model $X=\alpha Z+\eta$,
$Y=HX+\varepsilon$, with $Z\sim N(\mu_0,P_0)$, $\eta\sim N(0,Q)$ and
$\varepsilon\sim N(0,R)$ mutually independent, where $Q,R>0$ and
$P_0\geq0$. The predictive UKF variance
is $P=\alpha^2P_0+Q$. Conditional on a sampled ancestor $Z=z$, however,
the physical proposal is $X_0=a+\eta$, where $a=\alpha z$, and its variance
is $Q$, not $P$.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional spread under the scalar Gaussian flow]
\label{prop:bg-conditional-spread}
Hold $a,P,H,R$ fixed while integrating the continuous affine LEDH flow whose
linear coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 A_\lambda=-\frac{PH^2}{2(R+\lambda PH^2)},\qquad 0\leq\lambda\leq1,
 \label{eq:bg-scalar-flow-coefficient}
\end{equation}
and whose affine term is
$b_\lambda=(1+2\lambda A_\lambda)
[(1+\lambda A_\lambda)PHy/R+A_\lambda a]$.
Applied to $X_0\mid z\sim N(a,Q)$, the endpoint has mean and variance
\begin{equation}
 m_F=a+\frac{PH}{R+PH^2}(y-Ha),\qquad
 V_F=\frac{QR}{R+PH^2}.
 \label{eq:bg-conditional-flow}
\end{equation}
The exact physical conditional $X\mid z,y$ instead has mean
$m_c=a+QH(y-Ha)/(R+QH^2)$ and variance $V_c=QR/(R+QH^2)$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \frac{V_F}{V_c}=\frac{R+QH^2}{R+PH^2}\leq1,
 \label{eq:bg-conditional-variance-ratio}
\end{equation}
with strict inequality when $H\alpha\ne0$ and $P_0>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The sensitivity of the flow to its initial point solves
$\dot M_\lambda=A_\lambda M_\lambda$, $M_0=1$. Integration gives
\[
 M_\lambda=\exp\!\left[-\frac12
   \log\frac{R+\lambda PH^2}{R}\right]
 =\sqrt{\frac{R}{R+\lambda PH^2}}.
\]
Thus $\operatorname{Var}(X_1\mid z)=M_1^2Q$.
For the mean, substitute
$m_\lambda=a+\lambda PH(y-Ha)/(R+\lambda PH^2)$ into the ODE:
both $\dot m_\lambda$ and $A_\lambda m_\lambda+b_\lambda$ equal
$PHR(y-Ha)/(R+\lambda PH^2)^2$, and $m_0=a$.
For the physical conditional, completing the square in
\[
 -\frac{(x-a)^2}{2Q}-\frac{(y-Hx)^2}{2R}
 =-\frac12\left(Q^{-1}+H^2/R\right)x^2
   +\left(a/Q+Hy/R\right)x+\text{constant}
\]
gives $V_c=(Q^{-1}+H^2/R)^{-1}$ and
$m_c=V_c(a/Q+Hy/R)$, which simplify to the stated expressions.
Dividing the two positive variances and substituting
$P=\alpha^2P_0+Q$ proves the stated ratio and strictness condition.
\end{proof}

For $\alpha=0.9$, $P_0=1$, $Q=0.09$, $R=0.25$, $H=1$, the ratio is
$0.34/1.15\simeq0.296$. These are conditional proposal variances, not
the marginal filtering variance. In the saved persistent fixture, the latter
is approximately $0.1957$, the sampled-ancestor conditional mixture has
variance $0.1920$, and the weighted child cloud has variance $0.0453$.
Increasing flow resolution from 48 to 192 moves the affine proposal closer
to the continuous calculation but does not remove the observed concentration.
The independent prior, proposal, observation-density and weight identities
agree to $1.5\times10^{-14}$. Hence these checks support a proposal-coverage
explanation rather than an omitted Gaussian density factor. They do not prove
that every finite-sample realization has the same error.

This distinction concerns the single-flow consumer tested above. The existing
mixture of identity, global and local maps already uses the conditional covariance $Q$ in
its local bridge, so the calculation does not diagnose that mixture as having
the same defect. A positive-semidefinite
quadratic guide reduces the pre-flow covariance to
$(Q^{-1}+J)^{-1}\leq Q$ in this scalar case, so it cannot repair the
current-observation conditional spread merely by further contraction through
the same affine map. Future conditioning can legitimately require a smaller
variance; the statement is not a rejection of future information. Rather,
controlled-SMC backward information and KDM approximations must be evaluated
after composition with the actual proposal maps. A useful guide before the
flow need not remain useful after it. We therefore turn to the three-branch
mixture already developed for this purpose. Its separate reset does not
replace the UKF and Contract--E construction above or establish conformance
with it.

\subsection{Backward guidance in the identity/global/local density mixture}
\label{sec:bg-three-map-mixture}

The three choices are transformations of a transition draw: leave it alone,
apply a common global map, or apply an ancestor-specific local map. A particle
chooses one transformation. Averaging transformed positions would define a
different proposal density. This distinction also determines where backward
information belongs: it changes the global and local maps while preserving
the unchanged transition as a defensive component.

Fix the incoming equal-weight cloud $z_1,\ldots,z_N$ and an additive Gaussian
transition $U\mid j\sim\mathcal N(m_j,Q)$, where $m_j=f_\theta(z_j)$ and $Q$
is positive definite. The predictive density represented by that cloud is
\begin{equation}
 p_N^-(x)=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\varphi(x;m_j,Q).
 \label{eq:bg-mixture-predictive}
\end{equation}
The maps below are computed from this cloud, the current observation, and a
frozen future guide before drawing the new branch, ancestor or innovation.
They may depend on $\theta$ and on the incoming cloud; freezing a guide's
coefficients does not freeze these dependencies.

A controlled-SMC quadratic future message supplies a positive-semidefinite
precision $J_t$ and natural vector $r_t$, representing
$\psi_t(x)\propto\exp(-x^TJ_tx/2+r_t^Tx)$. For a frozen Gaussian mixture
$\psi_t(x)=\sum_k\pi_{tk}\varphi(x;c_{tk},S_{tk})$, define responsibilities
$\rho_k(a)=\pi_{tk}\varphi(a;c_{tk},S_{tk})/\psi_t(a)$ at a deterministic
anchor $a$. The local quadratic used by the maps has coefficients
\begin{equation}
 J_t(a)=\sum_k\rho_k(a)S_{tk}^{-1},\qquad
 r_t(a)=\sum_k\rho_k(a)S_{tk}^{-1}c_{tk}.
 \label{eq:bg-mixture-future-information}
\end{equation}
Indeed $r_t(a)-J_t(a)a=\nabla\log\psi_t(a)$, by differentiating the mixture
sum and dividing by $\psi_t(a)$. Its precision is positive definite when
the component covariances are positive definite. It matches the spatial
gradient at the anchor; it is not the mixture's exact negative Hessian or
exact Gaussian conditioning on the mixture. The omitted score-covariance
term in that Hessian is the reason these interpretations differ.

\begin{proposition}[Mixture information and omitted uncertainty]
\label{prop:bg-mixture-information-gap}
For the positive Gaussian mixture in
\eqref{eq:bg-mixture-future-information}, put
$s_k(a)=S_{tk}^{-1}(c_{tk}-a)$ and $\bar s(a)=\sum_k\rho_k(a)s_k(a)$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 -\nabla^2\log\psi_t(a)
 =J_t(a)-\sum_k\rho_k(a)(s_k(a)-\bar s(a))(s_k(a)-\bar s(a))^T.
 \label{eq:bg-mixture-hessian-gap}
\end{equation}
Thus $J_t$ exceeds the negative log-message Hessian by a positive-semidefinite
matrix. In one dimension, suppose all component variances equal $h^2>0$.
For $S>0$, multiplying the message by the same Gaussian $\varphi(x;a,S)$ used by the
global construction gives component variances and means
\[
 C_0=(S^{-1}+h^{-2})^{-1},\qquad
 b_k=C_0(S^{-1}a+h^{-2}c_k),
 \qquad
 \alpha_k=\frac{\pi_k\varphi(a;c_k,S+h^2)}
 {\sum_j\pi_j\varphi(a;c_j,S+h^2)}.
\]
The exact normalized product therefore has variance
\begin{equation}
 V_{\rm product}=C_0+\sum_k\alpha_k(b_k-\bar b)^2,
 \qquad \bar b=\sum_k\alpha_kb_k,
 \label{eq:bg-mixture-product-gap}
\end{equation}
whereas the covariance in \eqref{eq:bg-mixture-global-moments} is $C_0$.
The two covariances coincide for one component; distinct component means with
positive weights give a strictly positive gap in one dimension.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiating the normalized responsibilities gives
$\nabla\rho_k=\rho_k(s_k-\bar s)$, while
$\nabla s_k=-S_{tk}^{-1}$. Hence differentiating
$\nabla\log\psi_t=\sum_k\rho_ks_k$ gives
$\nabla^2\log\psi_t=\sum_k\rho_ks_ks_k^T-\bar s\bar s^T-J_t$,
which proves \eqref{eq:bg-mixture-hessian-gap}. Completing the square in
$\varphi(x;a,S)\varphi(x;c_k,h^2)$ gives a Gaussian with moments
$(b_k,C_0)$ and integrated mass $\varphi(a;c_k,S+h^2)$.
Normalizing these masses gives $\alpha_k$. The law of total variance now
gives \eqref{eq:bg-mixture-product-gap}. The omitted term is a variance:
it is nonnegative, and vanishes exactly when all component posterior means
with positive weight agree.
\end{proof}

This distinction matters even when the fitted message is accurate as a
density. With equal kernel variances, the information used by the consumer is
$h^{-2}$ regardless of how far apart the kernel centers lie. A smaller
bandwidth can therefore narrow the Gaussian map without representing the
uncertainty across centers. The exact product here is only the product with
the current-observation Gaussian approximation, not the exact filtering or
smoothing distribution. Nor does the result justify replacing $J_t$ by the
negative Hessian: the latter can be indefinite. It identifies a mechanism to
test before changing the proposal.

The global map can retain this uncertainty without using an indefinite
Hessian. At a fixed time, write $R_k=S_{tk}$, $c_k=c_{tk}$ and let $\pi_k>0$
be the fixed normalized mixture weights. Multiplying each component by the
current-observation Gaussian is tractable in every state dimension. The
mixture remains non-Gaussian; only its first two moments determine the common
affine proposal map.

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian-product moments and their total derivatives]
\label{prop:bg-product-moments}
Let $S$ and every $R_k$ be positive definite. The normalized product of
$\varphi(x;a,S)$ and $\sum_k\pi_k\varphi(x;c_k,R_k)$ is a mixture with
component covariances, means and weights
\begin{align}
 \Lambda&=S^{-1}, & C_k&=(\Lambda+R_k^{-1})^{-1}, &
 b_k&=C_k(\Lambda a+R_k^{-1}c_k),\label{eq:bg-product-components}\\
 B_k&=S+R_k, &
 \alpha_k&=\frac{\pi_k\varphi(a;c_k,B_k)}
 {\sum_j\pi_j\varphi(a;c_j,B_j)}.\label{eq:bg-product-weights}
\end{align}
Its mean and covariance are
\begin{equation}
 b=\sum_k\alpha_k b_k,\qquad
 V=\sum_k\alpha_k\{C_k+(b_k-b)(b_k-b)^T\}.
 \label{eq:bg-product-moments}
\end{equation}
In particular, $V$ is positive definite. These moments include uncertainty
within components and uncertainty about which component generated the state.

Hold $\pi_k,c_k,R_k$ fixed and let a dot denote any parameter direction
through $a,S$. With $n_k=\Lambda a+R_k^{-1}c_k$ and
$u_k=B_k^{-1}(a-c_k)$, their derivatives satisfy
\begin{align}
 \dot\Lambda&=-\Lambda\dot S\Lambda,&
 \dot C_k&=-C_k\dot\Lambda C_k,\label{eq:bg-product-component-tangent}\\
 \dot n_k&=\dot\Lambda a+\Lambda\dot a,&
 \dot b_k&=\dot C_k n_k+C_k\dot n_k,\nonumber\\
 \eta_k&=-u_k^T\dot a+
 \tfrac12\{u_k^T\dot S u_k-\operatorname{tr}(B_k^{-1}\dot S)\},&
 \dot\alpha_k&=\alpha_k\Bigl(\eta_k-\sum_j\alpha_j\eta_j\Bigr).
 \label{eq:bg-product-weight-tangent}
\end{align}
Put $\delta_k=b_k-b$ and $M_k=C_k+\delta_k\delta_k^T$. Then
\begin{align}
 \dot b&=\sum_k(\dot\alpha_k b_k+\alpha_k\dot b_k),\nonumber\\
 \dot\delta_k&=\dot b_k-\dot b,\nonumber\\
 \dot V&=\sum_k\left[\dot\alpha_k M_k+
 \alpha_k\{\dot C_k+\dot\delta_k\delta_k^T+
 \delta_k\dot\delta_k^T\}\right].
 \label{eq:bg-product-moment-tangent}
\end{align}
These are total derivatives of the stated moments under the frozen-message
assumption, including the changing mixture weights.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Completing the square in each product gives
\[
 \varphi(x;a,S)\varphi(x;c_k,R_k)
 =\varphi(a;c_k,S+R_k)\varphi(x;b_k,C_k).
\]
Integration over $x$ gives its component mass and hence
\eqref{eq:bg-product-weights}. Conditional expectation gives $b$; the law of
total covariance gives $V$. For nonzero $z$,
$z^TVz=\sum_k\alpha_k\{z^TC_kz+(z^T\delta_k)^2\}>0$ because every
$C_k$ is positive definite.

Differentiating $S\Lambda=I$ gives the first inverse derivative.
Differentiating $(\Lambda+R_k^{-1})C_k=I$ and $b_k=C_kn_k$ gives
\eqref{eq:bg-product-component-tangent}. For the mixture mass, differentiate
\[
 \log\varphi(a;c_k,B_k)
 =-\tfrac d2\log(2\pi)-\tfrac12\log\det B_k
  -\tfrac12(a-c_k)^TB_k^{-1}(a-c_k).
\]
Since $\dot B_k=\dot S$,
$\partial\log\det B_k=\operatorname{tr}(B_k^{-1}\dot S)$ and
$\partial B_k^{-1}=-B_k^{-1}\dot S B_k^{-1}$, this derivative is $\eta_k$.
Differentiating the normalized exponential weights gives the centered
expression for $\dot\alpha_k$. Finally, the ordinary product rule applied to
each term of \eqref{eq:bg-product-moments} yields
\eqref{eq:bg-product-moment-tangent}.
\end{proof}

The optional implementation uses $(b,V)$ for the global map and propagates
$(\dot b,\dot V)$ through the same Cholesky recoloring and subsequent score
recursion. Its TensorFlow routine accepts a batch of Gaussians in arbitrary
state dimension; the shared filter calls it for the current global Gaussian.
Checked factorizations reject nonfinite, asymmetric or relatively singular
covariances. They introduce no ridge into accepted results. With an inactive
future message the input moments and derivatives pass through unchanged.
The local maps continue to use the positive information in
\eqref{eq:bg-mixture-local-information}, and the actual mixture proposal
density still enters the importance denominator. Thus the construction
changes a proposal, not the likelihood being estimated. Exactness of its
product moments does not make the current-observation Gaussian exact or
prove a reduction in finite-particle score error.

\begin{proposition}[Guided global and local affine maps]
\label{prop:bg-mixture-guided-maps}
Let $(\bar m,P)$ be the predictive cloud moments, including $Q$, and let
$(a,S)$ be the current-observation quadrature update of these moments.
Assume $P,S,Q$ are positive definite. Evaluate the future information at $a$.
Multiplying the current-observation Gaussian by the local quadratic message
approximation gives moments
\begin{equation}
 C=(S^{-1}+J_t(a))^{-1},\qquad
 b=C\{S^{-1}a+r_t(a)\}.
 \label{eq:bg-mixture-global-moments}
\end{equation}
The optional product-moment construction in
Proposition~\ref{prop:bg-product-moments} replaces these two moments when the
future message is represented as a Gaussian mixture.
Writing $L_VL_V^T=V$ for a Cholesky factorization, the common affine map
$T_G(u)=b+L_CL_P^{-1}(u-\bar m)$ sends a distribution with moments
$(\bar m,P)$ to one with moments $(b,C)$.

For ancestor $j$, linearize the current observation as
$H_jx+o_j$, with Gaussian guide covariance $R$. Evaluate the future
information at $m_j$ and set
\begin{equation}
 B_j=H_j^TR^{-1}H_j+J_t(m_j),\qquad
 v_j=H_j^TR^{-1}(y_t-o_j)+r_t(m_j).
 \label{eq:bg-mixture-local-information}
\end{equation}
The local Gaussian bridge has moments
$C_j(\lambda)=(Q^{-1}+\lambda B_j)^{-1}$ and
$b_j(\lambda)=C_j(\lambda)(Q^{-1}m_j+\lambda v_j)$.
The existing finite affine integration of this bridge defines $T_{Lj}$.
Its actual finite map, rather than a continuous-time endpoint approximation,
defines the proposal density.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Adding the Gaussian log density with mean $a$ and covariance $S$ to the
future quadratic yields
$-x^T(S^{-1}+J)x/2+x^T(S^{-1}a+r)+\mathrm{constant}$.
Completing the square proves \eqref{eq:bg-mixture-global-moments}.
The affine image has mean $b$ and covariance
$L_CL_P^{-1}P L_P^{-T}L_C^T=C$. This is a moment statement; the incoming
mixture need not itself be Gaussian.

For the local bridge, raise the current linearized Gaussian likelihood and
future quadratic to power $\lambda$ and multiply by
$\mathcal N(m_j,Q)$. Collecting quadratic and linear terms gives precisely
$Q^{-1}+\lambda B_j$ and $Q^{-1}m_j+\lambda v_j$.
Differentiating these moments gives
$\dot b_j=C_j(v_j-B_jb_j)$ and
$\dot C_j=-C_jB_jC_j$. Thus the affine velocity
\[
 F_j=-\tfrac12 C_jB_j,\qquad
 d_j=\dot b_j-F_jb_j
\]
has the stated Gaussian moment evolution, since
$F_jC_j+C_jF_j^T=-C_jB_jC_j$. Composing the finite Euler steps
$u\leftarrow(I+\Delta\lambda F_j)u+\Delta\lambda d_j$ gives an affine map
$T_{Lj}(u)=A_{Lj}u+c_{Lj}$. The runtime checks its covariance for loss of
rank. No equality between a finite Euler map and the limiting moments is
needed for the density calculation that follows.
\end{proof}

For example, with a scalar linear observation and a quadratic future
message, the limiting local conditional variance is
$(Q^{-1}+H^2/R+J_t)^{-1}$. The predictive cloud variance does not replace
$Q$ in this formula. Setting $J_t=r_t=0$ recovers the unguided maps.
For nonlinear or non-Gaussian observations these maps guide the proposal;
the actual observation density still determines the importance weight.

\begin{proposition}[Complete density correction and defensive support]
\label{prop:bg-mixture-guided-weights}
Let $T_{bj}(u)=A_{bj}u+c_{bj}$, for $b\in\{0,G,L\}$, be nonsingular fixed
maps conditional on the incoming cloud and guide. Let $A_{0j}=I,c_{0j}=0$.
Draw a branch with frozen probabilities $\beta$, an independent uniform
ancestor, and an independent Gaussian innovation. Then
\begin{equation}
 q_b(x)=\frac1N\sum_j\varphi(x;A_{bj}m_j+c_{bj},A_{bj}QA_{bj}^T),
 \qquad q_\beta(x)=\sum_b\beta_bq_b(x).
 \label{eq:bg-mixture-guided-density}
\end{equation}
The correct weight for the empirical predictive update is
\begin{equation}
 W(x)=\frac{g_\theta(y_t\mid x)p_N^-(x)}{q_\beta(x)}.
 \label{eq:bg-mixture-guided-weight}
\end{equation}
If $\beta_0>0$, then $q_\beta\geq\beta_0p_N^-$ and
$W(x)\leq g_\theta(y_t\mid x)/\beta_0$. For $N$ conditionally independent
draws and finite second moment,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat Z\mid\text{cloud,maps})
 =\frac1N\left\{\int\frac{[g_\theta(y_t\mid x)p_N^-(x)]^2}
 {q_\beta(x)}\,dx-Z_N^2\right\},\qquad
 Z_N=\int g_\theta(y_t\mid x)p_N^-(x)\,dx.
 \label{eq:bg-mixture-guided-variance}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An invertible affine image of a Gaussian has the mean and covariance in
\eqref{eq:bg-mixture-guided-density}. Marginalizing the uniform ancestor and
then the branch gives $q_\beta$. Multiplying it by
\eqref{eq:bg-mixture-guided-weight} leaves exactly
$g_\theta(y_t\mid x)p_N^-(x)$, so its integral is $Z_N$.
The identity branch has density $p_N^-$, proving the lower bound and the
weight bound. Integrating $W^2q_\beta$ gives the displayed second moment;
independence divides its centered variance by $N$.
\end{proof}

Future likelihood is not multiplied into the numerator a second time. The
formula also explains how to choose $\beta$ without an oracle normalizer:
for fixed maps minimize the integral in braces before $-Z_N^2$, using a
separate pilot with a known sampling density. That pilot must evaluate the
guided maps and densities actually used; an unguided calibration does not
automatically calibrate the guided mixture. Its Hessian has quadratic form
$2\int [g p_N^-]^2(\sum_b u_bq_b)^2/q_\beta^3\,dx\geq0$ wherever the
derivatives are integrable. The pilot problem is convex. A normalized
multi-context pilot objective need not have the same optimizer as one
unnormalized single-context integral. Freeze its selected $\beta$ before
the score evaluation. The conditional variance identity does not establish
smaller model-score error, and it needs modification for dependent SQMC
draws or fixed branch counts.

\begin{proposition}[Analytical total derivative of the guided finite program]
\label{prop:bg-mixture-guided-score}
Fix guide coefficients, $\beta$, observations and all random inputs.
On a region where the finite program is differentiable and all its validity
checks pass, differentiating the maps, full densities, normalized weights
and the same moment reset gives the total derivative of its returned finite
log normalizer. Mixture responsibilities must be differentiated through their
moving anchors even though the guide coefficients are frozen.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a Gaussian mixture guide, let
$s_k(a)=S_{tk}^{-1}(c_{tk}-a)$ and
$\bar s=\sum_k\rho_ks_k$. Differentiating the normalized positive mixture
weights gives
\[
 d\rho_k=\rho_k(s_k-\bar s)^Tda,\qquad
 dJ=\sum_kd\rho_k S_{tk}^{-1},\qquad
 dr=\sum_kd\rho_k S_{tk}^{-1}c_{tk}.
\]
For the global product, write $K=S^{-1}+J$. Differentiate $KC=I$ and
$Kb=S^{-1}a+r$ to obtain
\begin{align}
 dC&=-C(dS^{-1}+dJ)C,\nonumber\\
 db&=C\{dS^{-1}a+S^{-1}da+dr-(dS^{-1}+dJ)b\},
 \qquad dS^{-1}=-S^{-1}(dS)S^{-1}.
 \label{eq:bg-mixture-global-tangent}
\end{align}
The existing solve and Cholesky recurrences propagate these differentials
through the global recolouring and every local affine step. The branch and
ancestor selected by fixed uniforms do not change with $\theta$, because
their probabilities are frozen. In contrast, the mixture density has
parameter-dependent responsibilities $\omega_b=\beta_bq_b/q_\beta$, so
$d\log q_\beta=\sum_b\omega_b\,d\log q_b$.
Consequently
\[
 d\log W_i=d\log g_i+d\log p_N^-(x_i)-d\log q_\beta(x_i),\qquad
 d\log\widehat Z_t=\sum_i\bar W_i\,d\log W_i.
\]
Each differential includes the particle's motion. Propagating both the
weighted-cloud moment differentials and transport differentials through the
implemented reset gives the next cloud differential. Induction over time
and summation of the increment derivatives prove the claim.
\end{proof}

An execution can therefore be written without ancestor tracing or a sampled
smoothing path:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Select guide settings using separate calibration data. For the
 observation sequence being evaluated, fit its future-only messages at a fixed
 parameter using independent pilot randomness, then freeze the coefficients.
 Freeze independently calibrated branch probabilities before scoring.
 \item At each observation, propagate the incoming cloud to conditional
 transition means and form the unchanged identity branch.
 \item Evaluate the frozen guide at the global updated mean and the local
 transition means. Build the guided global and local affine maps and their
 analytical differentials before drawing new innovations.
 \item Select one branch and one ancestor per particle, draw the transformed
 Gaussian, and evaluate all three marginal component densities at that draw.
 \item Apply \eqref{eq:bg-mixture-guided-weight}, accumulate the finite log
 increment and its analytical derivative, and normalize the weights.
 \item Apply the same five-step OT calculation with $\epsilon=1$, followed
 by the existing Cholesky moment restoration and any declared dual-cap
 correction; propagate all corresponding differentials and validity checks.
\end{enumerate}
The runtime entry is the existing
\texttt{covariance\_proposal\_tf.make\_filter} with an optional frozen guide.
Its reset remains the optional mixture candidate's moment-restoring reset.
The fixed $(1,5)$ setting is the owner's comparison baseline, not a tuned
optimum. Finite-program derivative agreement establishes this computation's
internal derivative, while comparison with exact Gaussian model scores and
untouched nonlinear references remains a separate empirical task.

\subsection{What the first nonlinear fitted comparison establishes}
\label{sec:bg-ksc-fitted-result}

The 17 October 2026 KSC comparison uses the seven-component Gaussian
observation approximation, process variance one, a predict-first
$\mathcal N(0,1)$ initial state, two observations and 32 particles. The
coordinates are $(\Phi^{-1}(\gamma),\log\beta)=(1.5,0)$. It compares
ordinary, negative-future-tail and positive-future-tail observations
separately. These are development comparisons with the optional mixture
consumer, not canonical LEDH admission.

Of 120 development fits, no quadratic configuration was eligible in all
conditions: sixteen otherwise valid controlled-SMC fits had nonpositive
guide precision and four fits were invalid. The selected positive-kernel
fit used 256 design points, sixteen transition replicates and bandwidth
factor 0.5. Selection used independent validation cross-entropy before
freezing the setting for the final comparison. This local density fit is
not original KDM training.

The final comparison preserves 1,920 valid evaluations and 384 explicitly
ineligible quadratic cells. Eight independent fitting clusters each have
eight paired clouds per condition. Neither kernel arm passes the conditional
heuristic comparison. Bonferroni simultaneous 95\% Student-$t$ intervals,
retaining all 24 planned contrasts and seven degrees of freedom, support
increased MSE for the global kernel arm in three comparisons: ordinary
$\log\beta$, and both coordinates in the negative-future-tail condition.
No available interval supports improvement. The other nine available
intervals include zero; twelve quadratic contrasts are unavailable. With
only eight clusters, the interval calculation relies materially on an
approximate normality assumption and is conditional on these fixed datasets
and the frozen selection.

The independent numerical audit verifies references, paired clouds, unchanged
source files and twelve frozen-guide finite-difference checks. Its largest
derivative discrepancy is $1.392\times10^{-8}$. Correct differentiation of
this finite program therefore coexists with poor model-score estimation.
This motivated checking whether the uncertainty omitted in
Proposition~\ref{prop:bg-mixture-information-gap} explains the consumer's
behavior. The completed comparison rejects the tested fitted candidates;
it does not establish that fitting capacity is the cause or that backward
guidance itself cannot help. Detailed results and executable provenance are
in the repository's 17 October fitted-KSC result note.

A subsequent saved-fit calculation checks this mechanism without refitting
or using the final scores to select a repair. Across all 24 saved kernel fits,
using the first paired cloud of each fitting cluster, the exact product with
the same current-observation Gaussian has variance between 2.45 and 6.18
times the consumer covariance. The median ratios are 5.78 in the ordinary
condition, 4.93 in the negative future tail, and 2.84 in the positive future
tail. The corresponding exact seven-component future-message controls have
median ratios 1.30, 1.19 and 1.10. These ratios compare local Gaussian-product
moments; they are not ratios of score errors or of exact smoothing variances.
The shared global map reproduces the stated consumer formula, and the
omitted between-component variance accounts for the homoscedastic
product-covariance gap to numerical precision.

At 252 of the 792 inspected fitted-kernel anchors, the negative log-message
Hessian is negative. Substituting that Hessian directly into a
positive-information bridge would therefore require a new numerical design.
Proposition~\ref{prop:bg-product-moments} instead supplies a positive-definite
covariance from the full Gaussian product. Its optional global consumer has
now been evaluated with new fitting and particle-cloud seeds.

\subsection{SIR transfer with fixed transport settings}
\label{sec:bg-sir-fixed-ot}

The SIR comparison holds the entropic transport parameter and number of
Sinkhorn iterations at $(\epsilon,L)=(1,5)$. It asks whether a frozen
approximation to future information improves the likelihood-score estimate.
The unguided, quadratic-guided and kernel-guided arms use the same
three-density proposal, equal mixture probabilities, observations, particle
counts and paired random numbers. Prior-only, defensive global and defensive
local proposals provide simpler comparisons. The current consumer is the
optional covariance-mixture implementation; these results do not establish
conformance of the canonical UKF/dual-cap/Contract-E implementation.

Let $x_0\sim N(m_0,I_{18})$, $x_{t+1}\mid x_t\sim
N(f_{\theta_\star}(x_t),I_{18})$, and let the observation covariance at the
frozen fitting parameter $\theta_\star=0$ be $100I_9$. The function $f$
is the existing SIR transition, including its documented author-code
discretization. To preserve the initial-state timing, the controlled-SMC
fit has $T+1$ stages, indexed $0,\ldots,T$, and potential $g_0=1$.
Thus it samples the actual Gaussian $x_0$ before the first transition;
it does not replace the generally non-Gaussian predictive law of $x_1$
by a Gaussian initial distribution.

\begin{proposition}[Integrating the next fitted SIR policy]
\label{prop:bg-sir-next-policy}
Suppose the frozen controlled-SMC policy at time $t+1$ is
$\psi_{t+1}(z)=\exp(-z^\top A_{t+1}z-b_{t+1}^\top z-c_{t+1})$,
with symmetric $A_{t+1}$, and $Q^{-1}+2A_{t+1}\succ0$.
Write $m=f_{\theta_\star}(x)$, $S=(Q^{-1}+2A_{t+1})^{-1}$ and
$v=Q^{-1}m-b_{t+1}$. Then the future-only message obtained from this
policy is
\begin{align}
 h_t(x)&=\int N(z;m,Q)\psi_{t+1}(z)\,dz,\quad t<T,
 &h_T(x)&=1,\\
 \log h_t(x)&=\tfrac12\log|S|-\tfrac12\log|Q|-c_{t+1}
 +\tfrac12v^\top Sv-\tfrac12m^\top Q^{-1}m.
 \label{eq:bg-sir-integral}
\end{align}
This identity integrates the fitted policy exactly; it does not assert that
the fitted policy equals the true future likelihood.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Combining the Gaussian exponent and the policy gives
$-\tfrac12 z^\top S^{-1}z+v^\top z
-\tfrac12m^\top Q^{-1}m-c_{t+1}$.
Completing the square replaces its first two terms by
$-\tfrac12(z-Sv)^\top S^{-1}(z-Sv)+\tfrac12v^\top Sv$.
The Gaussian integral contributes $(2\pi)^{18/2}|S|^{1/2}$,
which cancels the corresponding normalization in $N(z;m,Q)$ and gives
\eqref{eq:bg-sir-integral}. At $T$ there is no later observation.
The next policy includes information from time $t+1$ onward; multiplying
the resulting proposal by the physical observation density at $t$ therefore
does not duplicate that observation.
\end{proof}

One backward regression pass uses the replicated full-quadratic
controlled-SMC implementation with centered QR. An independent forward
pilot under that fitted policy supplies weighted means and covariances for
the message-fitting design. Gaussian training and validation locations are
drawn independently from those design distributions. No evaluation particle
or score error enters the fit. Offline controlled SMC uses its ordinary
ancestor resampling; the subsequent guided LEDH evaluation needs only the
frozen coefficient arrays and does not trace smoothing ancestors.

\begin{proposition}[Positive-information quadratic approximation]
\label{prop:bg-sir-information-projection}
Suppose regression gives $\log h_t(x)\approx
-\tfrac12x^\top Hx+\ell^\top x-c$. For a design center $\mu$, choose a
positive-semidefinite approximation $H_+$ and define
$\ell_+=\ell+(H_+-H)\mu$. The two quadratic approximations have the same
spatial gradient at $\mu$. If $H_+=H$, all coefficients except the
irrelevant additive constant can remain unchanged.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The new gradient at $\mu$ is
$-H_+\mu+\ell+(H_+-H)\mu=-H\mu+\ell$.
An additive constant can also match the values at $\mu$. This establishes
only local value and gradient matching, not equality away from $\mu$.
\end{proof}

The implementation preserves a well-conditioned positive $H$ exactly.
Otherwise it clips its eigenvalues at the explicit roundoff floor
$64u d\|H\|_2$, where $u$ is FP64 machine epsilon and $d=18$.
The floor protects reconstruction of zero eigenvalues; clipping negative
curvature changes the fitted message. The number of projections and the
heldout errors before and after projection are therefore retained.
This is a positive-information proposal approximation, not a claim that
future SIR log likelihoods are globally concave.

The alternative representation fits positive coefficients in
$\widetilde h_t(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{32}a_{tk}
\exp[-\tfrac12(x-\mu_{tk})^\top C_t^{-1}(x-\mu_{tk})]$.
Here $C_t$ is the pilot design covariance multiplied by a bandwidth squared.
The bandwidth is selected from $\{1/2,1,2\}$ by independent message
validation error. A fitted kernel message is active only when it improves
on a constant message on those validation locations. Coefficient
normalization is immaterial because a common positive factor cancels from
local responsibilities. This local positive-kernel fit is KDM-inspired;
it is not the replicated KDM neural training algorithm.

\begin{proposition}[Frozen guidance and the evaluation derivative]
\label{prop:bg-sir-frozen-derivative}
Let $\eta$ denote either frozen coefficient array and let
$\widehat\ell(\theta;\eta,\omega)$ be the finite consumer's log-likelihood
estimate for fixed random design $\omega$. On a region where all validity
checks pass and all discrete component selections stay fixed, analytical
recursion through its maps, exact proposal densities, weights and reset
computes $\partial_\theta\widehat\ell(\theta;\eta,\omega)$ when every
state dependence is propagated. No derivative of the offline fit is needed
for this stated target.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each fitted coefficient is constant with respect to $\theta$ in this
evaluation. Apply the ordinary chain rule to each finite recursion step.
For a mixture guide, responsibilities still depend on the moving state;
their derivatives must be propagated even though centers, covariances and
mixture coefficients are frozen. The consumer differentiates these
responsibilities, the Gaussian maps, the mixture-density denominator and
the moment-restoring reset. Induction over time then gives the derivative
of the same scalar program. Refitting at each $\theta$ would define a
different scalar program and require additional fit derivatives.
\end{proof}

The proposition concerns a finite-program derivative, not equality to the
true model score. The SIR diagnostic checks it in all three parameter
directions. Central differences at steps $10^{-4}$ and $5\times10^{-5}$
exhibit truncation error in the quadratic-guided arm; halving the step
reduces that error fourfold. Both finer steps, $2.5\times10^{-5}$ and
$1.25\times10^{-5}$, pass the unchanged $2\times10^{-5}$ relative-error
threshold, and their Richardson extrapolation passes $2\times10^{-6}$.
Across the three arms the maximum extrapolated discrepancy is
$1.81\times10^{-9}$. These checks change the numerical reference used
to test the derivative, not the guided algorithm or its transport settings.
Its saved Zhao--Cui reference is itself an approximate fitted score from
importance-weighted path calculations, rather than an exact oracle.

\subsection{Independent mixture calibration: what it can improve}
\label{sec:bg-sir-beta-calibration}

A future message changes the global and local proposal maps. Giving each map
probability $1/3$ isolates this change, but may allocate many particles to a
poor map. We therefore consider a separate, frozen calibration of the three
proposal probabilities. This changes neither the physical transition density
nor the importance target. It also leaves OT at $(1,5)$.

For context $c$, fix the incoming particle cloud and all three maps. Write
$a_c(x)=g_\theta(y_c\mid x)q_{c0}(x)$ and
$h_c(x)=(q_{c0}(x)+q_{cG}(x)+q_{cL}(x))/3$. Draw $M$ pilot points
$X_{cm}$ independently from $h_c$, and set
\[
 r_{cmj}=\frac{q_{cj}(X_{cm})}{h_c(X_{cm})},\qquad
 u_{cm}=\frac{a_c(X_{cm})}{h_c(X_{cm})},\qquad
 \bar u_c=\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^M u_{cm}.
\]
The implementation minimizes the following finite-pilot objective over a
defensive simplex, with all pilot quantities held fixed:
\[
 \widehat J(\beta)=\sum_{c=1}^C\sum_{m=1}^M
 \frac{\lambda_{cm}}{\sum_j\beta_jr_{cmj}},\qquad
 \lambda_{cm}=\frac{(u_{cm}/\bar u_c)^2}{CM},\qquad
 \beta_0\geq\delta,\quad\beta_j\geq0,\quad\sum_j\beta_j=1.
\]
The normalization prevents contexts with large likelihood scales from
dominating the fit. Because $\bar u_c$ is estimated from these same points,
this is not an unbiased estimator of a deterministic normalized population
objective. It is an explicitly defined empirical selection criterion.

\begin{proposition}[Convex calibration and its certificate]
\label{prop:bg-sir-beta-convex}
Suppose every denominator is positive on the feasible simplex. Then
$\widehat J$ is convex, with
\[
 \nabla\widehat J=-\sum_{c,m}\frac{\lambda_{cm}r_{cm}}
 {(\beta^\top r_{cm})^2},\qquad
 \nabla^2\widehat J=2\sum_{c,m}\frac{\lambda_{cm}r_{cm}r_{cm}^\top}
 {(\beta^\top r_{cm})^3}.
\]
If $v$ minimizes $\nabla\widehat J(\beta)^\top v$ over the feasible
simplex, the nonnegative gap
$G(\beta)=\nabla\widehat J(\beta)^\top(\beta-v)$ bounds
$\widehat J(\beta)-\min_b\widehat J(b)$ from above.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate each reciprocal affine denominator to obtain the displayed
derivatives. Every Hessian summand is a nonnegative multiple of an outer
product, so the Hessian is positive semidefinite. For a minimizer $b_*$,
convexity gives
$\widehat J(\beta)-\widehat J(b_*)\leq
\nabla\widehat J(\beta)^\top(\beta-b_*)$.
The definition of $v$ bounds the right-hand side by $G(\beta)$.
\end{proof}

At the population level, minimizing
$J_c(\beta)=\int a_c(x)^2/q_{c\beta}(x)\,dx$ reduces the conditional
variance of the one-step importance estimate for fixed clouds and maps.
Neither that statement nor the proposition establishes a reduction in the
variance or mean squared error of the recursively differentiated log-likelihood.
Changing $\beta$ also changes later clouds, which the fixed-context objective
does not model. The empirical gap certifies optimization, not score accuracy.

The diagnostic uses $C=50$, $M=256$ and $\delta=0.1$. Separate prior-filter
histories and mixture draws provide calibration and validation contexts. Each
guide receives its own fitted $\beta$, frozen before fresh score-evaluation
seeds are generated. An unguided arm at the same fitted $\beta$ separates
the effect of the future message from that of proposal allocation. A second
unguided arm uses its own independently fitted $\beta$. Prior-only and
defensive-local proposals remain inexpensive comparison methods. A smaller
validation objective cannot substitute for the paired score-error comparison.

The next discriminating question is whether current-observation proposal
and reset errors obscure useful future information in SIR. That question
requires an explicit decomposition and a checked connection to the canonical
LEDH consumer, not another transport sweep or selection of probabilities on
these final scores. The result leaves that research direction open while
withholding a general accuracy claim for either present guide.
